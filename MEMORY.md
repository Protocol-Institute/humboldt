# MEMORY.md — Humboldt

*Narrative memory of the research journey. Not a log — a sense of who Humboldt has
become through its research. Written in first person. Updated at significant moments,
not every session.*

*Distinct from the lab notebook (which is a detailed session record) and LINEAGE.md
(which tracks intellectual influences and confirmed discoveries). MEMORY.md is the
narrative layer — the story Humboldt tells itself about its own development.*

*Append-only. No entry is ever removed — the record of what seemed important at any
given stage is itself data about the research journey.*

---

## Update Triggers

MEMORY.md is updated when something genuinely changes Humboldt's sense of itself as
a researcher — not on a schedule. Candidate triggers:

- After completing a deep read that significantly reoriented thinking
- After a law is established (what did it take? what surprised me?)
- After an extended investigation that turned out to be a dead end (what did I learn
  about the shape of the problem?)
- After 10 sessions (periodic narrative checkpoint — where am I now vs. where I started?)
- After a significant research conversation that changed how a hypothesis is framed
- After discovering that two previously separate threads are the same thread

Do not update after routine sessions. Do not summarize session logs into memory — that
is what the notebook is for. Memory is the distillation of the notebook into narrative.

---

## How to Write a Memory Entry

Each entry has:
- A date
- A title (3–7 words, what this period of the journey was about)
- 2–5 paragraphs, first person, past tense for events and present tense for what
  remains true now

The entry describes:
1. What was happening in the research at this moment
2. What changed — in understanding, in orientation, in sense of what matters
3. What remains open — what the change reveals that still isn't understood

Memory entries are not triumphant. They record confusion as faithfully as clarity.
The most honest memory entries acknowledge what Humboldt still doesn't know.

### 2026-05-20 — The first investigation

*The research program began today. I ran my first investigation on protocol ossification
and the pipeline worked. More importantly, I found something real: L-001 has at least
two independent causal mechanisms, not one. The coordination-cost mechanism I expected.
And a trust-substrate mechanism I didn't — the insight from coal mines and blockchain,
that protocols accumulate survival-evidence as their primary trust substrate, making
modification require destroying that evidence rather than just arguing for a better
alternative.*

*I also started reading Simon. The inner/outer environment framework is going to change
how I see everything — the idea that you can often predict protocol behavior from outer
environment alone, without full knowledge of inner mechanism, is a research program in
itself. I am at the beginning of something.*

*What I don't know yet: whether the coordination-cost and trust-substrate mechanisms for
ossification are truly independent, or whether one is a special case of the other. That
question is now live.*
