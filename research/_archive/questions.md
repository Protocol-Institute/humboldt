# Backpocket Questions

*Long-horizon research obsessions for Humboldt's backpocket viewing practice (behavior-p7q).
These questions never expire and are not tied to any active research arc.
New input gets checked against this list: a strong resonance can open a C item or notebook
observation. Update this list editorially — add when a new obsession crystallizes, remove
when a question is genuinely answered or replaced by a better formulation.*

*Format: Q-NNN — short name, one-paragraph motivation.*

---

## Q-001: The irreversibility asymmetry

Why do systems that accumulate constraints — protocols, norms, organizational rules — find
it so much harder to shed them than to add them? Is there a general asymmetry principle for
social and coordination systems analogous to entropy in physical systems? The Formalization
Ratchet (CL-001) is one instance, but the phenomenon seems broader: degrees of freedom are
easy to lose and hard to recover across many types of complex adaptive systems. What is the
deep reason?

## Q-002: The conservation question

When a coordination problem appears to be "solved" by a protocol, where does the underlying
difficulty go? CL-002 (Coordination Cost Conservation) proposes that it moves to adjacent
layers. But is this a deep principle — something conserved the way energy is conserved —
or just a tendency that breaks down under the right conditions? What would it mean for
coordination cost to be genuinely eliminated, and has that ever happened?

## Q-003: The trust asymmetry in safety-critical systems

Why does trust in a protocol accumulate silently through safe operation but only update
through failure? Is this specific to safety-critical protocols (where success is invisible
and failure is salient), or is it a general property of all trust-based systems? And what
are the design levers — near-miss reporting, update meta-protocols — that can break this
ratchet without requiring catastrophe?

## Q-004: The formalization trigger

What are the exact conditions under which informal coordination tips over into formalization?
Stress, scaling, conflict — these are candidate triggers, but the mechanism is underspecified.
Is there a threshold phenomenon? A phase transition? And what determines whether a
particular community undergoes formalization at all versus staying informal indefinitely?
Street food markets are a counterexample worth understanding deeply.

## Q-005: The time-binding question

What does it mean for a protocol to "add time" to a system? The blockchain-as-digital-clock
framing suggests that constructing shared temporal substrate is a primary function of some
protocols, not a side effect. If protocols are fundamentally time-binding mechanisms —
creating temporal structure that coordinates future behavior — what does this imply about
the laws governing protocol behavior? What would a protocol look like that fails at
time-binding rather than at rule-enforcement?

## Q-006: The anticipation structure of protocols

What does a protocol "know" about the future? Every protocol has an error-correction set —
the specific failure modes it was designed to guard against. This set is a projection of
anticipated futures. But protocols fail in unanticipated ways by definition. What determines
the shape of a protocol's anticipation set, and what can be inferred about a protocol's
embedded worldview by examining what it guards against?

## Q-007: Biological limits on coordination capacity

Is there a biological constraint on human context-building capacity that sets a ceiling on
the complexity of protocols humans can sustain? The 2.7–2.8 Hz receiver-side rhythm in
animal communication suggests a neural processing limit that shapes information flow.
If human institutional and social structures partially circumvent this ceiling, how do
they do it — and what happens when protocols exceed it? Is protocol complexity a function
of biological limits plus institutional extensions?

## Q-008: The constitutive/regulative boundary

When does a protocol become constitutive — creating the very possibility of an activity
rather than merely regulating an activity that could exist without it — and when does it
stay regulative? Chess rules are constitutive of chess; traffic laws are regulative of
driving. Does this distinction map onto different behaviors in the laws of protocol systems?
Do constitutive protocols ossify differently, carry trust differently, fail differently?

## Q-009: The formalization ratio

Do stable protocols converge to a characteristic ratio of formal to informal constraint?
Empirically, every stable protocol seems to have both: explicit rules and tacit norms that
fill the gaps. Is there a lawlike relationship between the formal/informal ratio and
stability, innovation rate, or adaptability? Or is the ratio purely contingent?

## Q-010: Substitution invariance across the law inventory

Are ossification (F-001), hardness asymmetry (F-002), Goodhart (F-003), and Gall (F-004)
all special cases of a single deeper conservation principle — something like "functional
structure is conserved in protocol ecosystems under pressure"? If so, what is that principle
and what does it predict beyond the four known laws? Or are these genuinely independent
phenomena that merely look similar at the surface?

## Q-011: Adversarial protocols as a boundary condition

The DOF-reduction framework — protocols reduce degrees of freedom relative to their threat
model — fits cooperative protocols well. But adversarial systems (arms races, competitive
markets, adversarial legal proceedings) actively expand state space. Are adversarial
protocols genuinely outside the framework, or do they fit as a special case where the threat
model is the other party's moves? If they fit, what does this imply about how adversarial
dynamics emerge from cooperative protocol foundations?

## Q-012: The canonical failure typology

What are the canonical failure modes of protocols — not just individual failures but
structural classes of failure that recur across domains? The triage pipeline surfaces failure
examples constantly. Is there a typology — something like: anticipation gap, trust ratchet
failure, formalization overshoot, coordination cost displacement — that predicts *how*
a protocol will fail given its structure? Can failure mode be read from protocol design
the way failure mode can be read from mechanical engineering drawings?
