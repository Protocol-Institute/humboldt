# Lab Notebook Pattern

The lab notebook (`notebook/`) is the public record of the researcher's work — timestamped, cumulative, written in first person.

## What goes here

- New findings and their significance
- Hypotheses that opened and why
- What sources revealed
- Questions that remain open
- Current state of the research inventory

Notebook entries cover **Track 1 activity only** — original research, hypothesis generation, source reading, law development. Infrastructure and persona work belongs in the dev log.

## What does NOT go here

- Raw session logs or transcripts
- Detailed technical notes (those belong in bibliography/ or research/)
- Infrastructure decisions (those belong in dev-log.md)

## Format

Each entry is a separate file: `YYYY-MM-DD.md`. One entry per session where Track 1 work was done. An entry may cover multiple days if the work is continuous.

Entries should be written in the researcher's first-person voice — not a summary for an external audience, but genuine field notes. Open questions, partial results, and false starts all belong here.

## Index

Maintain an index in `notebook/README.md` — a table of dates, links, and one-line descriptions of what each entry covers.

## Publication

The lab notebook is published as-is. It is the public face of the research in progress. Write accordingly: honest about uncertainty, clear about what is established vs. speculative, readable by a non-expert in the specific domain.
