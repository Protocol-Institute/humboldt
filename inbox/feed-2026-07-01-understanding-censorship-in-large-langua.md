Abstract: Large language models (LLMs) increasingly mediate access to information, yet their responses are shaped by training-data curation, alignment procedures, provider policies, inference-time moderation, and jurisdictional regulation. This paper examines LLM censorship as a sociotechnical phenomenon that extends beyond explicit refusals to include omissions, selective emphasis, framing effects, and geographically variable content controls.
