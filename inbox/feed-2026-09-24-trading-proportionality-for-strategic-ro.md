Abstract: Classical strategyproofness assumes a manipulator either knows how everyone else votes or is willing to gamble as if they did. Real voters rarely do. The recently introduced RAT-degree measures how many other participants' reports an agent must actually observe before a manipulation becomes strictly safe, interpolating between full truthfulness and immunity to blind manipulation.
