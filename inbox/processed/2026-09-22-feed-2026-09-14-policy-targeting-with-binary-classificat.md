## Summary

arXiv:2609.13068v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Empirical researchers often use binary classification trees to identify subgroups at risk of adverse outcomes. We compare two classification tree algorithms in this policy-targeting context: classical classification and regression trees (CART) and the maximizing-distance final-split approach (MDFS). We establish a theoretical setting in which MDFS identifies a high-risk subgroup while CART identifies none.
