Abstract: We examine how railroads shaped Denmark's nineteenth-century economic transformation and the diffusion of Grundtvigian institutions. Using a panel of 1589 parishes and a difference-in-differences design for staggered adoption, we find that railroad connection increased local population by 7 percent, partly through internal in-migration, and structural change.
