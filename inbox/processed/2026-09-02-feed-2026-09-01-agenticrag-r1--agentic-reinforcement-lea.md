Abstract: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) improves the factuality of large language models (LLMs), yet existing RAG systems often struggle with complex, multi-step reasoning that requires adaptive retrieval and continuous revision of intermediate contexts. Recent reinforcement learning (RL)-based agentic RAG methods partially alleviate this issue, but typically rely on coarse-grained action spaces and trajectory-level rewards, resulting in weak reward assignment and a bias toward short-horizon, stereotyped reasoning template.
