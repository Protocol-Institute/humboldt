# The Long-Term Effects of British and French Colonization in Africa on Trust in Traditional Leaders

**Source:** econ.GN updates on arXiv.org
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17436
**Date:** 2026-08-19
**Relevance:** Directly examines how colonial institutions shaped trust in traditional leaders, a key mechanism for understanding institutional persistence and local governance outcomes in Africa.

## Summary

arXiv:2608.17436v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Trust in local institutions matters for trade, public goods provision, conflict resolution, and democratic consolidation. Using individual data from rounds 6 and 7 of the Afrobarometer surveys, I document that respondents in former British colonies are substantially more likely to trust traditional leaders than respondents in former French colonies. Cross-country comparisons of this kind are confounded by unobserved heterogeneity in pre- and post-colonial histories.
