## Summary

arXiv:2609.10565v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: Connectivity can turn separate contributions into a public good that benefits participants and nonparticipants alike. Yet only participants pay for it, and each can avoid her charge by withdrawing while enjoying whatever public output survives. How do network connections constrain who can or must pay, and how large a cost can the group bear? We study a participation game in which public output equals the size of the largest connected group of active players, whose members divide a fixed operating cost.
