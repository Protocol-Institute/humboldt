Abstract: Restaking-based protocols enable verifiable LLM inference without the high proving cost of zkML or the hardware trust assumptions of TEEs. Their security is commonly justified by a one-round slashing condition: a rational provider should not cheat when the expected penalty exceeds the cost saving from dishonest inference. This paper shows that this condition can overstate security when inference is supplied repeatedly under the same stake.
