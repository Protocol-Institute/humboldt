Abstract: A recurring question in the design of scalable multi-agent systems -- from robot swarms to collectives of large-language-model (LLM) agents -- is whether adding more agents can, on its own, overcome performance limits, or whether a qualitatively \emph{deeper} organization is required. A recent preprint argues that flat, homogeneous multi-agent systems face an irreducible, population-independent ``causal floor'' on achievable error, removable only by hierarchical (nested-loop) organization.
