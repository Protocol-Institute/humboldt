# The Shared Discovery Paradox: How a One-Answer Rule Turns Better Information into Worse Search

## Summary

arXiv:2607.18045v1 Announce Type: cross 
Abstract: Organizations often pool dispersed information into one ranking and then allow many agents to act on that shared view. In a discovery problem, this can improve beliefs while reducing coverage. We develop an exactly solvable benchmark with sixteen boxes, one target, eight searchers, and noisy private clues. Pooling raises the accuracy of the best single recommendation from 0.20 to 0.3835, but repeating that recommendation lowers group discovery from 0.8322 under decentralized clue-following to 0.3835.
