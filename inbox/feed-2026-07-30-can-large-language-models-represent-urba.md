## Summary

arXiv:2607.27100v1 Announce Type: new 
Abstract: There is growing interest in using large language models (LLMs) as low-cost proxies for resident attitudes in urban planning. Previous work shows that LLMs can predict average results of survey experiments, but less is known about whether they preserve the spatially anchored, identity-conditioned structure behind those averages, namely how support changes as a project approaches homes and how that response divides across tenure and partisan groups.
