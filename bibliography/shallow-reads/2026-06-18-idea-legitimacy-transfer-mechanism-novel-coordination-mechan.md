# Idea: Legitimacy transfer mechanism: novel coordination mechanisms inherit trust from established concepts through strategic labeling

**Source:** Discord #MAYA as legitimacy-borrowing protocol (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-06-18
**Connected to:** none
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Pattern observed in protocol adoption behavior; ready for integration into a candidate law once relationship to trust propagation dynamics is clarified.

## What this is

Novel coordination mechanisms reduce adoption friction by borrowing credibility from established institutional or conceptual frameworks via naming and framing, rather than earning independent trust through demonstrated performance or technical superiority.

## What I took from it

This idea identifies a decoupling between *actual* mechanism performance and *perceived* legitimacy—a gap that can be bridged through semantic infrastructure (labels, analogies, institutional borrowing). It's distinct from but complementary to the claim that protocols require independent credibility: this observation suggests that *initial* credibility can be entirely borrowed, creating a window for adoption before the mechanism must prove itself.

This directly challenges a naive merit-based model of protocol adoption and opens investigation into the role of *naming* and *framing* as operational forces in the new nature. The idea is particularly sharp because it identifies that the label itself becomes infrastructure—not merely window-dressing, but a functional mechanism for reducing cognitive and institutional resistance.

The observation also raises a critical second-order question: what happens when borrowed legitimacy encounters failure or friction? Does the mechanism retain inherited trust, or does it lose it faster due to the violated expectation created by false analogy?
