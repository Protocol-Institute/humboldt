# Idea: Protocols may settle into stable mixed regimes combining formal and informal components, with an emergent ratio that could be lawlike

**Source:** Discord #new-nature (by _vgr)  
**Date read:** 2026-05-29  
**Connected to:** H-001  
**Escalation:** store-only  
**Escalation rationale:** Pattern observation without structural threshold or predictive mechanism; requires empirical mapping before candidacy.

## What this is

The claim that real-world protocols do not trend purely toward formalization, but instead stabilize at a characteristic mix of explicit rules and informal norms, with this ratio potentially being a discoverable invariant across protocol families.

## What I took from it

This idea both extends and complicates L-003 (The Formalization Ratchet). L-003 proposes directional pressure toward formalization under stress, but does not account for equilibrium or reversion. The mixed-regime claim suggests that formalization may hit a natural saturation point—beyond which informal components become *necessary* rather than vestigial. This opens a productive tension: either formalization does not monotonically increase (contradicting L-003 as stated), or there exists a cost/friction function that makes pure formalization unstable at scale.

The idea also connects directly to H-001's question about coordination cost conservation: if a protocol shifts cost from informal trust-building to formal verification, the total coordination burden might remain constant, with the formal/informal ratio reflecting an equilibrium distribution. This would make the ratio a *symptom* of deeper cost invariance rather than a law in itself.

The idea is also adjacent to H-002 (trust accumulation), but inverted: it suggests not just that old protocols are trusted, but that they retain informal components *because* trust has accumulated enough to permit informality in some dimensions.

## Research connections

- **L-003:** Directly challenges or refines the monotonic direction; suggests formalization has a ceiling or reversion dynamic.
- **H-001:** If coordination cost is conserved, formal/informal ratio would be the *distribution* of that cost; ratio stability would be evidence for cost conservation.
- **H-002:** Complements; old, trusted protocols may be able to afford higher informal ratios because trust capital is high.
- **L-001:** Ossified protocols may trap themselves in a particular formal/informal mix, making the ratio path-dependent even if it reaches an equilibrium.

## Candidate laws or signals

**CL-Discord-vgr-1:** *Protocols under stable operation tend toward a characteristic formal/informal component ratio that is conserved across rescaling or replication within the same social context, with pure-formal or pure-informal regimes being unstable attractors.*

*Rationale:* This is riper than the source idea. It proposes both a pattern (ratio stability) and a constraint (stability only under certain conditions). However, it lacks:
- A mechanism or cost model explaining *why* the ratio stabilizes
- An empirical threshold or measurement protocol
- Evidence that the ratio is truly lawlike (invariant) vs. contingent on context

**Recommendation:** Hold as candidate hypothesis rather than law. Promote to **H-003: Is there a stable formal/informal component ratio in long-lived protocols, and if so, does it correlate with coordination cost or trust accumulation?** Requires comparative case mapping (DNS, BGP, HTTP, social media moderation, academic peer review) to establish whether ratio stability is observable and whether it predicts protocol fragility or resilience.
