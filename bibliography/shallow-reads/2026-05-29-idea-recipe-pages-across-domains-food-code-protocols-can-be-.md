# Idea: Recipe pages across domains (food, code, protocols) can be tracked over time to reveal convergence patterns as a proxy for protocol stabilization

**Source:** Discord #new-nature (by boredgargoyle)  
**Date read:** 2026-05-29  
**Connected to:** H-002  
**Escalation:** store-only  
**Escalation rationale:** Operationalizable method supporting H-002, but lacks independent evidence of discriminative power. Store as instrumentation proposal pending empirical validation.

## What this is

A proposal to use diachronic corpus analysis of recipes across domains (culinary, code, protocol design) to detect which practices converge to stability independent of stated optimality, thereby observing whether trust/adoption follows age and community consensus rather than measured correctness.

## What I took from it

This is a clever operationalization of H-002, but it reformulates rather than extends the law inventory. The idea assumes that *convergence* is a reliable marker of *trust accumulation*—a plausible proxy, but not necessarily proof. 

The real insight is methodological: recipe persistence patterns could disambiguate between two mechanisms:
- Communities converge on practices because they are objectively superior (L-002, L-004 predictive)
- Communities converge because practices become institutionalized and path-dependent, independent of quality (H-002 predictive)

If a recipe corpus shows stable adoption of *suboptimal* practices across domains, that would strengthen H-002. If it shows convergence only on measurably superior techniques, H-002 is weakened. The method is sound, but the outcome is not predetermined by the proposal itself.

## Research connections

- **H-002:** Direct operationalization—recipe stability as observable proxy for trust accumulation by age vs. correctness
- **L-001:** Recipe convergence may exemplify protocol ossification; tracking abandonment rate of recipes as adoption widens tests whether modification resistance follows adoption curve
- **L-003:** Recipe formalization patterns over time (oral tradition → written canon → standardized instruction sets) could trace norm-to-explicit-protocol transitions under scaling
- **L-005:** Comparing failed vs. successful recipe redesigns across domains could test Gall's principle in culinary and code contexts

## Candidate laws or signals

**CL-Recipe-001: Recipe Convergence is Orthogonal to Optimality** — In domains where recipe quality can be objectively measured (cook time, structural integrity, execution efficiency), communities converge on stable recipes at rates independent of measurable superiority, suggesting trust accumulates from consensus history rather than technical performance.

*Status:* Candidate. Testable empirically but requires baseline definition of "optimal" per domain before corpus work begins. Worth archiving as a discrete claim to prevent conflation with L-001/L-005.
