## What this is

An economic analysis of coalition formation under safety constraints, modeling the gap between what a population could collectively express and what emerges through open sequential coordination. The paper proposes "social assurance contracts" as a protocol design that preserves commitment privacy until threshold participation, contrasting with open organizing that requires safety to hold at every incremental step.

## What I took from it

The paper formalizes a mechanism for coordination cost transfer: moving the *locus of risk* from incremental participants to the protocol boundary. This is instrumentally relevant to L-006 (Coordination Cost Conservation) insofar as it suggests costs are not merely conserved but *strategically redistributed* across phases of protocol adoption—the assurance contract absorbs individual exposure risk into a single threshold event. 

However, the work is primarily a tool paper (designing a specific mechanism for a known problem class) rather than a primary theoretical or empirical argument about how protocolized systems behave under stress. It does not challenge or extend the laws under accumulation; it proposes a workaround. The connection to seed-015 (taming as political act) is suggestive but underdeveloped—the paper treats safety protocol design as technical optimization, not as a contested political choice with downstream institutional effects.

## Research connections

- **L-006:** Suggests coordination costs may be temporally restructured rather than merely conserved—risk transfer across adoption phases may alter aggregate friction without reducing it.
- **seed-015:** Implies that protocol design choices (assurance vs. open organizing) embed political assumptions about who bears visibility risk; deserves inquiry into whether "safe" protocol design itself becomes a tool of norm-setting.

## Method note

This paper exemplifies the tendency to treat protocol design as a solvable optimization problem when the deeper question is institutional. The framing accepts the safety constraint as exogenous and seeks a mechanism; it does not ask whether the safety constraint itself is an artifact of existing power structures or whether the assurance contract quietly displaces rather than solves the coordination problem. Research on the new nature should remain alert to papers that succeed technically within a frame while leaving the frame itself unexamined.
