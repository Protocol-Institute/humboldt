## What this is

An empirical study testing whether gender bias patterns observed in English-language hiring contexts replicate across linguistic and cultural boundaries (Japanese corporate hiring). Uses controlled counterfactual resume methodology with 60 rirekisho-format resumes, 12 gender-signal name pairs, and five LLM systems, plus evaluation of two mitigation strategies.

## What I took from it

This is a domain-specific bias audit rather than a theoretical or mechanistic investigation. The work extends the empirical footprint of known LLM bias phenomena into a new linguistic/cultural context, which is methodologically sound but falls into the "does this bias appear elsewhere?" category rather than "how does this bias actually work" or "what new principle governs it?"

The mitigation strategies evaluation (mentioned but not detailed in the abstract) may offer practical findings, but without access to the results, it's unclear whether they introduce novel insights into bias *reduction mechanisms* or simply test known debiasing approaches in a new domain.

The paper appears to be a responsible localization study—important for deployment safety—but does not introduce new theoretical machinery for understanding bias formation in protocolized decision systems, nor does it challenge established principles about how biases propagate through language models.
