## What this is

An empirical case study of household adaptation strategies during chronic infrastructure failure in a high-electrification context (Lebanon). The work documents both supply-side (generators, solar-battery) and demand-side (load shifting, suppression) responses using survey data from 1,000 households.

## What I took from it

This is primarily a domain application and behavior documentation rather than a mechanism paper. It confirms the intuitive pattern that high-dependence systems force heterogeneous workarounds when supply fails — but the abstract provides no signal of novel dynamics or generalizable laws about *how* or *why* these adaptations emerge or interact.

The paper sits at the descriptive layer: cataloging which coping mechanisms households deploy. What remains opaque from the abstract is whether there are phase transitions in adaptation behavior as failure duration lengthens, whether certain household types systematize faster than others (revealing constraints), or whether the diversity of solutions reveals something about coordination failure or lock-in dynamics in infrastructure repair. These would be mechanistic questions.
