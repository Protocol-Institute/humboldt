# Execution and assessment of agentic influence operations in simulated social networks

**Source:** cs.CY updates on arXiv.org — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28725
**Date read:** 2026-05-29
**Connected to:** L-004, L-003
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** 

## What this is

An empirical evaluation of AI-driven influence tactics in synthetic social networks, measuring the relative effectiveness of narrative release, amplification, and counter-messaging on belief change in agentic audiences. The work is a benchmark/capability study rather than a primary theoretical or mechanistic argument about protocol laws.

## What I took from it

The paper demonstrates L-004 (Goodhart Generalization) *in operation* within narrative protocols: reach and belief shift become decoupled optimization targets, and amplification maximizes exposure while counter-messaging maximizes opinion shift — revealing that the measurable proxy (exposure) diverges from the actual goal (sustained belief capture). This usefully illustrates metric capture in a new domain (agentic social influence).

The invocation of "counter-messaging" as a formal intervention also weakly suggests L-003 (Formalization Ratchet): under pressure from adversarial agentic actors, informal social norm-negotiation is being replaced by explicit, measurable counter-protocols. However, this is observational rather than mechanistic — the paper does not argue *why* formalization occurs under agentic pressure, only that interventions must be formalized to be effective.

The work does not propose new mechanisms, test a hypothesis about coordination cost or trust accumulation, or challenge existing laws. It applies known dynamics to a new substrate (synthetic networks with agentic participants) but does not reveal novel structural properties of protocol systems themselves.

## Research connections

- **L-004:** Amplification and counter-messaging reveal divergence between reach (measurable proxy) and belief durability (unmeasurable goal); metric capture in narrative protocols.
- **L-003:** Counter-messaging as formal intervention suggests narrative protocols shift from implicit to explicit under adversarial pressure, though mechanism is not developed.
