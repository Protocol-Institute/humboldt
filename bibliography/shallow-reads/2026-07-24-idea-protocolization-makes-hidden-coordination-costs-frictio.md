# Idea: Protocolization makes hidden coordination costs (friction, maintenance burden, failure modes) legible across distributed systems, functioning as a total cost estimation mechanism.

**Source:** Discord #Protocols as Total Cost Estimators (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-07-24
**Connected to:** CL-002
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Restates and refines existing legibility mechanism without introducing new structural claims about protocol behavior or cost conservation. Useful as a precision refinement but does not warrant independent candidate status given substantial overlap with [5], [7], [11].

## What this is

The claim that protocols function as instruments for rendering coordination costs visible and measurable across distributed systems—converting tacit friction into explicit, auditable cost surfaces.

## What I took from it

This idea sharpens CL-002 by adding a *functional* specificity: it's not merely that protocols make costs visible, but that they do so in a way that *enables total cost estimation*. This is a useful refinement because it suggests protocols serve an epistemic role—they transform unmeasurable friction into legible inventory.

However, the core mechanism (legibility via protocolization) is already captured in the existing hypothesis inventory. The distinction this adds is marginal: it emphasizes the *aggregability* of costs once protocolized, rather than simply their visibility. This is a qualifier, not a new law.

The idea does raise one latent question worth tracking: **Does legibility automatically enable accurate estimation, or does protocolization sometimes obscure costs by formalizing them into systems that hide distributed externalities?** That tension is worth flagging for future investigation.
