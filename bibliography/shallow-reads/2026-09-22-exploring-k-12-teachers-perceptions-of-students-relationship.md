# Exploring K-12 Teachers' Perceptions of Students' Relationships with AI Companions: Boundaries, Intervention Strategies, and Design Implications

**Source:** cs.CY updates on arXiv.org — https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13479
**Date read:** 2026-09-22
**Connected to:** L-003, L-013
**Kind:** meta
**Escalation:** store-only

## What this is

Qualitative interview study (n=33 US K-12 teachers) examining how institutional actors perceive and attempt to govern student-AI relational use. Teachers articulate informal norms around acceptable AI companionship, identify boundaries (academic vs. intimate), and describe ad-hoc intervention strategies in the absence of formal policy frameworks.

## What I took from it

This is a case study of **institutional norm formalization under uncertainty**—precisely the dynamic L-003 (Formalization Ratchet) and L-013 (Paradigm-Locked Anomaly Tolerance) aim to track. Teachers are currently operating in an informal coordination space: they perceive relational AI use as novel, lack institutional guidance, and are negotiating boundaries through scenario reasoning rather than protocol. The paper documents the *pre-formalization* state.

However, the paper does not examine what happens *when* formalization occurs, nor does it provide evidence that formalization happens under stress, or that informal norms actually degrade. It captures teacher sentiment and folk reasoning about an emerging phenomenon, not the institutional process of ossification or anomaly tolerance. The triage note correctly flags the connection, but the paper itself remains at the level of folk protocol design rather than systematic law-testing.

The work is valuable as a **boundary-setting artifact**—it shows how non-expert institutional actors naturally carve up relational AI into categories (academic/intimate; developmental/substitutive). This may inform seed-138 (Intent Legibility as Coordination Target Displacement), but weakly.

## Research connections

- **L-003:** Teachers are ad-hoc formalizing coordination norms in response to scaling and uncertainty; no evidence yet of ratcheting or rigidification under adoption pressure.
- **L-013:** Paper documents early-stage anomaly perception (intimate companions risk developmental harm) but no institutional tolerance pattern or delayed response.
- **seed-138:** Teachers attempt to disambiguate "relational" vs. "instrumental" use; the boundary itself becomes a coordination target, though the mechanism is not analyzed.

## Method note

This work exemplifies the value of **pre-formalization ethnography**—capturing institutional actors during norm-invention rather than studying ossified protocols. However, it does not yet answer whether those norms will formalize, persist informally, or bifurcate. To test L-003 and L-013, the research would need longitudinal design tracking the same institutions and teachers as policy crystallizes. The current snapshot provides qualitative input on what gets made legible (academic/intimate boundary) but no evidence on whether legibility itself drives subsequent dynamics. Consider this a *precursor condition inventory* rather than a law-testing study.
