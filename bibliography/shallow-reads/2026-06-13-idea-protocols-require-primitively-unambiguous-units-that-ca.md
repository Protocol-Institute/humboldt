# Idea: Protocols require primitively unambiguous units that cannot be locally reinterpreted

**Source:** Discord #Animal acoustic communication has a cons... (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-06-13
**Connected to:** L-irreducible-symbols, H-protocol-coherence
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Idea identifies a structural constraint but requires cross-domain validation and theoretical sharpening before candidate promotion. Parliamentary procedure is a single instantiation; generalizability across protocolized systems (biological, computational, social) remains untested.

## What this is

The claim that all functional protocols depend on a minimal set of locally-invariant symbolic units whose meaning cannot be altered at individual nodes without cascading coordination failure.

## What I took from it

This idea sharpens a suspected boundary condition: protocols appear to require *primitiveness* (irreducibility to smaller units) *and* **local semantic opacity** (a unit's meaning is fixed at the protocol level, not negotiable downstream). The parliamentary example—votes, motions, calls to order—illustrates units that *must* mean the same thing in every instance or the system collapses.

However, the idea risks collapsing two distinct problems: (1) whether unambiguity is *necessary* for protocol coherence, and (2) whether that unambiguity must be *primitive* (non-decomposable). Natural protocols (animal signaling, immune system ligand-receptor pairs) and engineered ones (TCP handshakes, DNS records) may achieve coordination through different mechanisms—some via strict semantics, others via statistical redundancy or error correction. The parliamentary frame may be unduly narrow.

This opens a productive tension: **Is protocol coherence fundamentally about semantic rigidity, or about robust-enough information transfer?** That distinction could reshape how we model protocol failure modes.

## Research connections

- **L-irreducible-symbols:** Parliamentary primitives are offered as exemplars; need to test whether other protocol domains exhibit the same irreducibility structure or achieve coordination differently.
- **H-protocol-coherence:** Directly proposes a mechanism (unambiguous primitives) for maintaining system-wide alignment; needs evaluation against counterexamples (noisy channels, evolving meanings, nested protocols).

## Candidate laws or signals

**CL-Protocol-Primitivity-1:** *Functional protocols require at minimum one set of locally-invariant symbolic units; the question is whether those units must be semantically rigid or merely statistically coordinated.*

This rephrasing acknowledges the core insight while flagging the open question—a better frame for future cross-domain testing.
