**Escalation rationale:** The idea restates a known pattern (formalization exposes vagueness) but lacks specificity about *how* this operates in protocolized systems—what architectural or computational pressures drive the revelation, and whether the vagueness-exposure is itself a law of artificial systems or merely an artifact of translation from human to machine specification.

## What this is

Operationalizing an aesthetic or design principle to make it computable reveals hidden ambiguities in the principle itself, suggesting that aesthetic intuitions are systematically vaguer than their users assume.

## What I took from it

This is a sound observation about the friction between human specification and machine implementation, but it risks collapsing two distinct phenomena: (1) the general truth that all formalization exposes ambiguity (not specific to aesthetics or artificial systems), and (2) a potentially deeper claim about whether *aesthetic principles specifically* are constitutively vaguer than other categories of human knowledge, or whether the vagueness is a function of the gap between analogical/embodied reasoning and discrete specification.

The relevance annotation correctly flags this as touching processing limitations, but the idea doesn't yet explain *which* limitations or *why* aesthetic principles are the revealing case. Is this about:
- The non-compositional nature of aesthetic judgment?
- The role of context-dependence in aesthetic properties?
- Computational irreducibility of certain design choices?

The idea opens a productive line: **what kinds of human knowledge structures become most visibly deficient when forced into protocol form?** But it needs specification of the *causal mechanism* in artificial systems, not just a restatement of the formalization-vagueness tradeoff.
