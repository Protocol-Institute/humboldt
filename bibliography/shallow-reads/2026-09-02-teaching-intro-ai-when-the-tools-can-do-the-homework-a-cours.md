## What this is

An experience report documenting a single institution's curricular response to LLM capability displacement of traditional homework assignments. The paper presents a redesign strategy (embedding LLM-building into the core curriculum) rather than a sustained theoretical or empirical argument about protocol behavior under legibility shifts.

## What I took from it

This is a localized manifestation of L-012 (Intervention-Layer Displacement): the paper implicitly documents what happens when a pedagogical assessment proxy (homework completion) becomes machine-legible and executable. The institutional response—embedding tool-building into the assessment itself—is a form of defensive formalization rather than a mechanistic insight into how optimization pressure migrates under computable enforcement.

The work does *not* provide generalizable evidence about the mechanism by which legibility displacement occurs, nor does it argue for or against any of the current law candidates. It is a case study in institutional adaptation, not a study of the underlying dynamics. The "Student Bill of Rights" framing suggests norm-setting rather than empirical mechanism investigation.

## Research connections

- **L-004 (Goodhart Generalization):** Implicit confirmation that homework-as-proxy becomes worthless once the tool can execute it; no analysis of *why* this happens or what general pattern it exemplifies.
- **L-012 (Intervention-Layer Displacement):** The redesign is an *example* of layer displacement (moving assessment upstream to tool-building), but does not investigate the mechanism or scope conditions.
- **seed-062 (Formalization Opacity Collapse):** Tangentially relevant—automating assessment legibility exposes the opacity of what was being measured—but not explored in the paper.

## Method note

This work is valuable as a *signal* that metric capture in assessment is operationally real for practitioners, but it is primarily a practitioner problem-solving report, not a research artifact designed to isolate mechanism. Research on L-004 and L-012 would need to move beyond single-institution redesigns to comparative studies of how different institutional protocols respond to the same legibility shift, and to explicit investigation of whether the optimization pressure displacement follows a predictable pattern. The paper documents adaptation; it does not explain causation.
