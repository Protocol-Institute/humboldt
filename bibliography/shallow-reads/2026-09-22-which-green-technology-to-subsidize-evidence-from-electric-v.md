## What this is

Applied microeconomic study of subsidy mechanism design in vehicle markets, using demand estimation to compare emission-reduction effectiveness across technology tiers. The core finding: intermediate-clean technologies may outperform the cleanest option if substitution elasticities favor them, suggesting subsidy allocation should track behavioral response, not absolute cleanliness.

## What I took from it

This is a narrow but competent application of L-004 (Goodhart Generalization: Metric Capture) to environmental policy. The paper's central insight—that optimizing for a simple proxy (emissions per dollar of subsidy) can produce worse outcomes than expected because agents respond to incentive structure rather than intent—confirms that metric capture occurs when the measurable goal (cleanliness ranking) diverges from the unmeasurable goal (total emission reduction). 

However, the work remains domain-specific. It does not establish a new mechanism, does not challenge or extend the conditions under which metric capture occurs, and does not probe what happens when the subsidy protocol itself becomes rigid or contested (the ossification pathway in L-001). The heterogeneity in mileage and fuel-type choice is treated as a demand parameter, not as evidence of how protocol formalization changes agent behavior over time. No exploration of how subsidy regimes lock in once adopted, or how enforcement legibility shapes what substitution patterns become visible to future policymakers.

## Research connections

- **L-004:** Confirms that proxy optimization (subsidizing intermediate tech) under measurement pressure can defeat stated intent; subsidy mechanism is exactly a proxy-legibility regime.
- **L-001:** Unexamined: once a subsidy allocation stabilizes, does it become harder to reoptimize even when demand patterns shift?
- **seed-133:** Metric formalization (cleanest-per-dollar) as paradigm lock—the framework assumes subsidy effectiveness *is* measurable as a scalar, which itself may constrain what reallocation strategies become thinkable.
