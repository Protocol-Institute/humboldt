## What this is

A simulator and serious-game design (Megabike) for studying mixed human-AI governance in common-pool resource and constitutional choice scenarios. The work is an experimental testbed and tool, not a primary theoretical or empirical argument about protocol dynamics.

## What I took from it

The abstract signals an experimental venue that *could* produce evidence on coordination cost conservation (L-006), adoption nonmonotonicity (L-010), and intervention-layer displacement (L-012), but the abstract does not present sustained findings on any of these. The framing ("whether such mixed groups can govern themselves") is a research question, not a claimed regularity. 

The paper appears to be a *platform* for future investigation of human-machine governance rather than a source presenting its own theoretical or empirical claims about protocol laws. The interdependent arenas (resource management, survival, constitutional choice) are designed to surface institutional dynamics, but no evidence of what those dynamics are has been shown in the provided text. Without the full paper, I cannot assess whether it contains sustained argument about how coordination costs redistribute across human-machine protocol layers, or how adoption curves behave under mixed governance signals.

## Research connections

- **L-006:** Coordination Cost Conservation — The megabike scenario with "interdependent arenas" may instantiate the conditions under which coordination cost migrates between layers (human deliberation ↔ machine enforcement), but the abstract does not report findings on cost trajectories.
- **L-010:** Coordination Adoption Nonmonotonicity — Mixed human-machine signaling could create conditions where adoption curves are non-monotonic (agents condition on other agents' trust in machine components). The testbed may surface this, but no evidence is presented.
- **L-012:** Intervention-Layer Displacement — If human governance choices are formalized as inputs to agent decision protocols, optimization pressure may displace the governance locus. The "constitutional choice" arena is suggestive but underdeveloped in the abstract.
