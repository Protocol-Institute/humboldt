## What this is

The claim that AI development labs and pharmaceutical manufacturers face structurally similar trust accumulation pressures, risk-concentration incentives, and regulatory lock-in dynamics that produce comparable deployment conservatism and paradigm stickiness.

## What I took from it

The idea correctly identifies that high-stakes protocol systems (whether drug approval or AI deployment) accrete trust through operational age and stability rather than rapid technical advance—this restates L-007 without adding mechanism. More interesting is the implicit claim about *why* both industries show resistance to paradigm shift: both face concentrated liability, institutional credibility stakes, and regulatory gatekeeping. However, the analogy breaks on a critical point: pharmaceutical trust ratchets operate on *outcome legibility* (efficacy signals, adverse event registries) across a fixed protocol space (the drug itself does not adapt). AI labs face the opposite: *outcome illegibility* paired with *rapid capability drift*. This is not convergent; it is structurally opposed. The parallel is suggestive but requires decomposition into specific protocol mechanisms before it can seed. What *does* generalize: industries where reputation is the binding constraint on deployment will show paradigm lock (L-013), but the *source* of lock differs. Pharma locks because verification costs scale with adoption; AI labs lock because *capability measurement* itself becomes unstable.

## Research connections

- **L-007 (Trust Ratchet):** The idea restates this for a new domain but does not add mechanism or boundary condition. Trust in both industries does accumulate with operational stability, but the source differs (outcome legibility vs. capability stability).
- **L-009 (Catastrophic Risk Cancellation):** Pharma and AI labs both face concentrated deployment prizes and distributed early-stage risks, but the symmetry breaks: pharma can formalize risk via trials; AI capability risk remains partially uncomputable before deployment.
- **L-013 (Paradigm Lock):** The analogy is strongest here. Both industries tolerate technical debt and conservative design to preserve institutional credibility. But pharmaceutical paradigm lock operates on *method* (approved manufacturing process); AI paradigm lock operates on *unknown unknowns* (unobserved capability profiles).
- **seed-193 (Trust Accumulation Without Verification Closure):** The idea resonates with this seed but does not deepen it; pharma achieves verification closure (phase outcomes), AI does not.

## Seed

**Seed title:** Trust Ratchet Substrate Divergence Under Outcome Legibility Asymmetry

**Seed type:** question

**Seed text:** High-stakes protocol systems (pharmaceutical approval, AI deployment) both exhibit trust ratcheting under adoption pressure, but the *substrate* of that ratchet differs: systems with formalized outcome verification (pharma trials) lock via method ossification and regulatory conservatism; systems with structurally illegible outcomes (AI capability measurement before deployment) lock via credibility staking and paradigm resistance. Does trust ratcheting behavior generalize across outcome-legibility regimes, or does outcome illegibility produce a distinct ratcheting mechanism (credibility-as-collateral vs. verification-as-precedent)? This suggests L-007 may require a legibility partition.
