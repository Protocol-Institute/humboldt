# Idea: Laws in natural science span theory-based constraints (Newton), constitutive rel

**Source:** Discord #new-nature (by _vgr)

**Connected to:** none
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** This is a meta-theoretical clarification on what kinds of statements count as "laws" in this research program. It does not propose a new empirical regularity about protocolized systems, but rather refines acceptance criteria for the inventory itself. Belongs in foundational assumptions, not the active hypothesis or candidate law track.

## What this is

The idea argues that valid laws in natural science include hard structural impossibilities (theory-based), fixed constitutive relations (like elasticity), and probabilistic macro-level regularities (like thermodynamic ensembles)—and that our new-nature law inventory should permit all three types, rather than privileging only hard constraints.

## What I took from it

This is a helpful metacritical observation. Our current established laws (L-001 through L-005) are phrased as structural impossibilities or strong tendencies, but the inventory may be unnecessarily narrow if we exclude probabilistic or scaling regularities that hold reliably without being absolute. The remark that H-001 and H-002 might be *empirical regularities rather than hard constraints* is particularly sharp: it suggests we've been collapsing two categories that natural science keeps distinct. If coordination cost is *conserved on average* under layer transitions (not universally), that is still a lawlike claim worth formalizing. Similarly, if trust accumulates by age-and-stability *as a statistical tendency* in safety-critical domains, that patterns deserves candidacy even if counterexamples exist. The clarification opens the question: which of our current laws are actually hard impossibilities, and which are robust patterns we should restate probabilistically?

## Research connections

- **L-001, L-002, L-003, L-005:** All currently phrased as near-absolute structural tendencies; this idea suggests some may be more precisely restatable as scaling or macro-level regularities with well-defined failure modes rather than exceptions.

- **L-004:** Already hedged with "under sufficient optimization pressure"—a probabilistic/scaling qualifier; exemplifies the mixed typology the idea proposes.
