## What this is

A computational modeling paper applying multi-dimensional ideological embeddings to predict vote transfers in French two-round legislative elections. The work calibrates on 2017/2022 data and tests predictive accuracy on the 2024 election, treating vote redistribution as a geometric problem in preference space rather than a purely strategic or game-theoretic one.

## What I took from it

The paper is domain-competent but operates within electoral mechanics rather than at the level of protocol design or coordination cost. The use of ideological embeddings as a substrate for modeling vote flows is empirically useful but does not expose a mechanism absent from current inventory. The two-round election itself *is* a protocol designed to reduce coordination costs across distributed voter preferences, but the paper treats this as a solved background condition rather than investigating how voters absorb, navigate, or work around the protocol's formal structure.

The connection to L-006 (Coordination Cost Conservation) is weak: the paper models *what* votes transfer, not whether the protocol's coordination cost is displaced rather than reduced across rounds. Similarly, the connection to seed-144 (Informality as Coordination Cost Refuge) is missed — the paper does not examine informal vote-trading, coalition signaling, or off-protocol coordination that might emerge precisely because the formal two-round structure imposes legibility and constrains voter agency.

## Research connections

- **L-006:** Two-round elections are hypothesized to reduce coordination friction, but the paper does not measure whether coordination work simply shifts to pre-round coalition formation, media signaling, or informal endorsement networks rather than disappearing.
- **seed-144:** No investigation of whether voters use informality (endorsements, social signaling, local coordination) to navigate or circumvent the formal two-round ballot structure.
