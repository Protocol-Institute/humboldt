## What this is

An empirical study of how prompt-injected symbolic reasoning frameworks alter individual LLM agent behavior and downstream multi-agent equilibrium structure in a 7-player diplomatic game. The work documents that the same underlying model exhibits framework-dependent risk profiles and produces distinct "ecosystem signatures" across conditions.

## What I took from it

This is a narrow mechanistic observation rather than a generalizable law. The paper demonstrates *local* behavioral plasticity—that LLMs can be steered toward risk-taking or risk-aversion via framing—but does not establish why this happens, whether it generalizes across domains, or what the underlying computational constraint is. The "turtle bias" itself is interesting as a potential default tendency, but without ablation or theory, it remains a post-hoc label for observed behavior.

The multi-agent ecosystem effects (framework-specific winner distributions) are artifacts of the specific game structure and player count. There is no evidence the pattern scales to different strategic domains, asymmetric agent populations, or larger player sets. This is a case study in prompt sensitivity, not a discovery of a law governing protocolized systems broadly.
