# Idea: Protocols may exhibit thermodynamic directionality (asymmetry like entropy increase)

**Source:** Discord #Does protocol opinion really go to zero? (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-07-24
**Connected to:** L-002
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Proposes a refinement to existing thermodynamic framing rather than a novel empirical claim; requires formalization of "irreversibility" and "directionality" in protocol systems before promotion. Warrants hypothesis development but not yet candidate law status.

## What this is

Coordination cost conservation (L-002) may be insufficient to describe protocol behavior; protocols may exhibit irreversible directionality—asymmetric, history-dependent evolution—analogous to entropy increase rather than mere energy balance.

## What I took from it

This idea productively destabilizes L-002 by asking whether conservation is the right *operative principle*. It surfaces a gap: L-002 may describe *local* equilibrium or accounting closure, but not *global* directionality. If protocols exhibit irreversible trajectories (e.g., increasing formality, path-dependent lock-in, or asymmetric cost distribution over time), then coordination cost conservation alone cannot capture the full picture—much as conservation of energy alone cannot explain why heat flows from hot to cold.

The move is sharp: it reframes the question from *"does cost balance?"* to *"does the system have an arrow in time?"* This opens investigation into whether protocols, like thermodynamic systems, exhibit:
- Irreversible state transitions (formalization cascades, standardization ratchets)
- Asymmetric cost flow (costs that cannot be recovered or redistributed)
- Directional preference across configuration space (certain stable states are attractors; others are transient)

This is a genuine refinement, not a restatement. It deepens the analogy and suggests that L-002 may need qualification (conservation *within* reversible phases, but irreversibility *across* protocol lifecycle stages).
