# Idea: PageIndex's design goal of enabling LLMs to retrieve relevant sections like human experts may inadvertently suppress novel perspectives

**Source:** Discord #PageIndex (by ncc1031)
**Date read:** 2026-11-23
**Connected to:** L-023, seed-461
**Kind:** content
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** PageIndex is a concrete instantiation of a narrower phenomenon (retrieval optimization under legibility), not a generalized regularity. The idea restates L-023 in domain-specific language. No new mechanism or scope is opened that would justify seed emission at this stage. Store for case evidence if PageIndex deployment generates measurable novelty suppression data.

## What this is

Optimization of information retrieval systems toward the measurable goal of expert-like accuracy channels reasoning through canonical interpretive paths, reducing the probability that non-standard or cross-domain reading paths produce usable outputs.

## What I took from it

This is a crisp instantiation of L-023 (Informality as Novelty Sink Under Legible Optimization), but applied to a retrieval layer rather than an execution layer. The claim is that when you make "retrieve the right section" legible and measurable, you create pressure on the ranking function that privileges sections matching established authority patterns—which is functional for expert-mimicry but systematically penalizes lateral or generative reading paths.

The idea does not open a new mechanism: it documents how legible optimization pressure operates on information surfacing, which L-023 and seed-461 already capture. It does not challenge the inventory—it confirms it. The idea would only escalate to seed if PageIndex deployment produced evidence of *why* this suppression occurs (e.g., does it emerge from training data authority bias, from ranking loss function design, from adoption of established retrieval metrics?), or if it generalized to *when* retrieval systems do NOT suppress novelty (what conditions permit generative retrieval?).

## Research connections

- **L-023:** Direct restatement in retrieval domain. PageIndex's "expert-like accuracy" goal is a legible optimization target; novelty suppression is the predicted outcome.
- **seed-461:** Proxy opacity under nested legible enforcement—the retrieval ranking is a legible proxy for "relevance," which masks what queries it *cannot* route effectively.
- **L-004:** Goodhart signal: optimizing retrieval metrics toward expert accuracy creates incentive to surface high-authority sections, not necessarily sections that resolve novel questions.
- **seed-023 (implicit):** The seed pool may already contain fragments on how canonicality becomes a retrieval attractor; check for seeds on authority bias in information architecture.
