## What this is

A protocol design paper presenting a dialogue game framework for multi-agent expert coordination in medical recommendation tasks. The work combines game-theoretic dialogue structure with explanation-bearing speech acts to enable collaborative reasoning across heterogeneous knowledge bases.

## What I took from it

This is a tool/protocol contribution rather than a foundational inquiry into protocolized systems. It engineers a specific dialogue game with explanation-oriented constraints, but does not argue for or investigate underlying laws governing *why* such protocols succeed or fail, nor does it present sustained empirical or theoretical work on the generative mechanisms of explanation-bearing coordination. The work is domain-applied (medical experts) and prototype-validated against specification—characteristic of implementation papers rather than primary theoretical or empirical research on artificial systems structure.

The paper may eventually contribute to understanding how *illocutionary force* (speech-act semantics) can be embedded in multiagent protocols, and whether explanation traces stabilize coordination under knowledge heterogeneity—but the current submission appears to be the engineering solution, not the investigation of the principle.
