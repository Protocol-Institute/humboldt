## What this is

A game-theoretic analysis of dynamic pricing under information asymmetry, comparing mechanism classes (posted-price vs. menu mechanisms) and deriving tight regret bounds. The work establishes that contract menus achieve O(T_γ) regret against a non-myopic strategic buyer with private valuation, matching a proven lower bound.

## What I took from it

This is a characterization paper within mechanism design—it tightens the regret frontier for a specific protocol class but does not introduce a novel mechanism or challenge equilibrium assumptions. The main insight (menu mechanisms outperform posted-price in strategic settings) is a refinement of existing protocol hierarchy theory rather than a foundational discovery.

The result is technically sound but operates within classical incentive-compatibility and revelation-principle frameworks. It confirms that *mechanism expressiveness matters* for learning efficiency, but this has been well-established in prior work. The equivalence result mentioned in the title likely refers to equivalence between mechanisms under certain conditions—a characterization result, not a new structural law.

No evidence that the pattern generalizes beyond dynamic pricing with fixed private valuations, nor that it introduces mechanisms absent from the inventory.
