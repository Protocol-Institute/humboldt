## What this is

A matched two-wave quasi-experimental study measuring shifts in perceived usefulness, ease of use, behavioral intention, and trust before and after an eight-week hands-on pilot of Microsoft 365 Copilot at a state transportation agency (n=124 after screening). Uses nonparametric tests and k-means clustering to identify heterogeneous response patterns.

## What I took from it

This is a competent adoption-dynamics study, but narrow in scope and mechanism. The triage correctly flags potential relevance to L-010 (Coordination Adoption Nonmonotonicity — post-exposure shifts in adoption signals) and L-003 (Formalization Ratchet — how institutional pressure might push informal use norms toward explicit policy). 

However, the paper appears to measure *individual* expectation recalibration under realistic use, not the *coordination dynamics* that L-010 targets — i.e., how one agent's shift influences adoption signaling to peers, creating nonmonotonic collective trajectories. The abstract suggests heterogeneous clustering of responses (implied by k-means mention), which *could* point to bifurcating adoption cohorts, but without access to the full results, I cannot confirm whether the work measures feedback loops, peer influence, or normalization pressure. Similarly, L-003 would require evidence of informal practices being encoded into formal protocol — the paper likely documents *individual* acceptance changes, not *institutional* formalization pressure. The domain (public sector AI adoption) is relevant to protocol systems, but the mechanism probed appears to be personal trust calibration rather than systemic coordination or norming collapse.

## Research connections

- **L-010:** Potential weak connection if clustering reveals bifurcating adoption personas that reinforce or suppress peer adoption signals; not evident from abstract.
- **L-003:** Potential weak connection if the study documents pressure from institutional adoption mandates forcing informal experimentation into formal policy; not evident from abstract.
- **seed-033 (Aesthetic Conversion in Early Adoption):** If "persona migration" tracks aesthetic or identity shifts in tool relationship (not just functional belief change), this could inform how early adopters construct legitimacy narratives.
