## What this is

A game-theoretic analysis of "social assurance contracts"—private commitments to publicly reveal a controversial position only when coalition size exceeds a safety threshold. The paper models how conditional disclosure removes the individual risk of early speech by making revelation conditional on sufficient prior adoption, and prices this protection via global-games equilibrium selection.

## What I took from it

The paper formalizes a specific coordination mechanism (threshold-gated public commitment) but does not establish a generalizable law about how disclosure protocols behave under adoption pressure, nor does it identify a mechanism absent from existing inventory. It is primarily a mechanism-design solution to a bounded strategic problem: how to reduce coordination failure when individual preference revelation is costly.

The connection to L-010 (Coordination Adoption Nonmonotonicity) is real but shallow—the paper shows one case where adoption becomes monotonic *because* risk is contractually removed, but does not explore the broader conditions under which adoption curves become non-monotonic or investigate the decay dynamics when such contracts fail or leak. The mechanism is legible but domain-specific (opinion revelation under social pressure).

The link to seed-015 (taming-as-political-act) is tenuous. There is no sustained engagement with how formalization of disclosure rules itself becomes a political intervention or how such protocols ossify.
