## What this is

An econometric study introducing the Adoption over Time Index (ATI) to classify renewable energy adoption behaviors across off-grid communities. The work segments adoption trajectories into typologies (leaders, laggards, switchers, etc.) and uses time-series methods to detect trend shifts.

## What I took from it

The paper is a competent empirical segmentation study within energy economics. It offers descriptive typology-building useful for policy targeting but does not advance mechanism-level understanding of how protocol (or norm-like) adoption becomes nonmonotonic, nor does it expose the formalization dynamics that emerge under scaling pressure. The ATI metric itself is a measurement innovation, not a theoretical generalization. 

The off-grid context is genuinely non-trivial — these are systems with lower institutional density and weaker formal enforcement — but the paper treats adoption as a preference-revealed phenomenon rather than as a coordination problem where formal requirements, verification costs, or norm-legibility shifts drive trajectory shape. The typology is post-hoc clustering, not mechanism recovery. No evidence that the observed adoption curves reflect L-010 (coordination adoption nonmonotonicity driven by signal conditioning), L-003 (formalization ratchet under stress), or L-001 (ossification under adoption pressure). The paper does not examine what happens *after* adoption reaches scale, or how informal norms might become formalized as adoption pressure mounts.
