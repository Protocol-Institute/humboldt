## What this is

A normative economic theory proposing that under AGI-driven automation of cognitive work, traditional value metrics (price, labour input, revealed preference) become unreliable proxies for human and societal benefit. The paper introduces Flourishing Value Theory (FVT) as a replacement framework, defining value as counterfactual, distribution-sensitive contribution.

## What I took from it

The paper identifies a genuine instability in proxy metrics under automation pressure — when the input being priced (labour, expertise, information) becomes widely substitutable by machine capital, market price and actual societal contribution decouple. This is a domain-specific instantiation of L-004 (Goodhart Generalization) and L-008 (Proxy Optimization Under Computable Enforcement).

However, the paper's response — proposing a new foundational metric rather than studying the *mechanism* by which proxies fail under automation and how systems adapt to that failure — does not itself generate law-shaped claims about protocol systems. FVT is a proposal for *what should be measured*, not an account of what *will happen* when measurement systems face substitution pressure or when agents optimize against counterfactual-contribution legibility. The work is philosophically sound but does not sustain a theoretical or empirical argument about system dynamics, nor does it introduce a mechanism absent from the current inventory.

## Research connections

- **L-004:** Confirms the general shape (proxy divergence from goal under optimization pressure), but in an economic domain already well-mapped; no new mechanism.
- **L-008:** Tangentially relevant — notes that computable enforcement creates optimization targets, but FVT does not study what happens when "counterfactual contribution" becomes a computable enforcement signal.
- **seed-068 (Unmeasurability as Anomaly Insulation):** FVT's central claim — that true value is unmeasurable — inverts the seed's direction; worth noting but not a confirmation.
