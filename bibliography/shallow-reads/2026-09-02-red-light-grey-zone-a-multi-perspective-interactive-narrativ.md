## What this is

A public engagement tool (interactive narrative prototype) designed to help non-experts navigate ethical reasoning about autonomous vehicle incidents by presenting multiple stakeholder perspectives on a distributed responsibility scenario. The work treats narrative framing as a method for eliciting situated ethical reflection rather than testing a hypothesis about protocol behavior or failure modes.

## What I took from it

This is a pedagogical/communication artifact rather than an investigation of a lawful mechanism. The paper appears to explore *how interpretive frames shape responsibility attribution* in the context of AV ethics—which touches the boundary of seed-021/030 territory (narrative framing as interpretation lock)—but does not present evidence that the framing choice itself generates downstream protocol behavior change, creates stable equilibria, or exhibits generalization across protocol classes.

The work is valuable for understanding how non-experts *construct* responsibility models under narrative constraint, but it does not investigate whether that construction produces measurable behavioral change in the systems they interact with, nor does it test whether the responsibility allocation pattern persists when protocols or contexts shift. It remains within the ethics/communication domain rather than the dynamics of protocolized systems themselves.

## Method note

This work illustrates an important gap: the difference between studying *how people interpret* a protocol scenario (cognitive/narrative level) and studying *how protocol design shapes behavioral outcomes* (systems level). Interactive narratives are useful for externalizing mental models and surfacing stakeholder disagreement, but they do not directly measure whether responsibility-framing choices alter how protocols ossify, how metrics capture goals, or how coordination costs distribute. Future work bridging this gap would need to show that narrative choice produces measurable protocol-level effects—e.g., does stakeholder perspective reframing change adoption patterns, metric selection, or governance drift? That would be a different paper.
