## What this is

An empirical network analysis of global food supply shock propagation using production, reserve, and trade data for four major staples. The paper identifies import dependence and per capita production capacity as primary determinants of shock vulnerability, simulating cascading effects through bilateral trade networks.

## What I took from it

This is a domain-specific instantiation of known network fragility under asymmetric dependency, not a challenge to or extension of L-006 (Coordination Cost Conservation). The paper confirms that shock absorption capacity correlates with autarky capacity and trade flexibility—but this is straightforward resilience theory, not a law about *protocol layer transitions* or *conservation under formalization*.

The work does not examine how coordination protocols change under stress (the actual claim of L-006), nor does it present a sustained mechanism about cost displacement across protocol layers. It is a competent shock-propagation model applied to a real critical infrastructure. The triage note's invocation of L-006 appears to be misfiled—the paper studies vulnerability distribution, not coordination cost conservation during protocol restructuring.
