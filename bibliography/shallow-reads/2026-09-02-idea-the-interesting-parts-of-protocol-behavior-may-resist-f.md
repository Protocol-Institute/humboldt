# Idea: The interesting parts of protocol behavior may resist full mathematization because formalism captures structure but loses the informal load-bearing elements that the Formalization Ratchet tracks.

**Source:** Discord #Protocols as Total Cost Estimators (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-09-02
**Connected to:** L-001, L-003
**Kind:** content
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** The idea restates and slightly refines L-003 without introducing new mechanism or boundary condition; it is a clarification of existing inventory rather than a novel fragment. However, it surfaces a precise problem worth tracking: the *loss function* of formalization itself.

## What this is

The claim that formalization necessarily discards informal coordination load that remains functionally critical — and that this loss may be irreducible rather than merely incomplete.

## What I took from it

This is a strong reading of L-003 (Formalization Ratchet), but it adds an important tension: it suggests that the ratchet doesn't just *push* informal norms out, but that formalization *cannot capture* certain load-bearing coordination work without destroying its function. This is not just about adoption pressure forcing incompleteness — it's about a structural orthogonality between what can be mathematized and what does the work.

The claim sharpens a question that L-003 leaves open: Is the persistence of informality a *strategic choice* (agents keeping slack room) or a *necessity* (the formal model is provably incomplete in a way that matters)? The idea suggests the latter, which would make informality not a bug in formalization but a *structural complement* that no protocol system can eliminate without collapse.

This connects strongly to seed-144 (Informality as Coordination Cost Refuge Under Substitution Pressure) and the whole family of legibility-trap seeds, but doesn't quite break new ground — it's a mechanism hypothesis for something the seeds already smell.

## Seed

**Seed title:** Formalization as Load-Shedding Boundary — Structural Incompleteness

**Seed type:** observation

**Seed text:** Protocols under formalization pressure shed load into informal channels not because agents strategically hide work, but because certain coordination functions lose efficacy when rendered legible and computable. The boundary between what can be mathematized and what requires opaque, context-dependent negotiation is not a gap in current formal systems but a structural limit: attempts to eliminate the informal remainder trigger protocol failure. This suggests formalization creates *not just adoption friction but functional asymmetry* — formal layers handle defined tasks; informal layers handle exception, context-reading, and rapid recalibration that formalization cannot precompute without collapse.
