## What this is

A survey paper mapping the incorporation of human-aligned cognitive, cultural, and normative factors into LLM-based multi-agent systems. The work frames a gap between current agent design (prediction/optimization) and human social environments (bounded rationality, cultural embeddedness, norm-governed).

## What I took from it

The paper identifies a real design asymmetry: most agent systems optimize for task completion under idealized rationality assumptions, while human coordination relies on culturally situated reasoning, shared norms, and value-laden decision-making. This is a **problem-framing** exercise rather than a law-generating one. It acknowledges the mismatch but, based on the abstract, appears to be a survey synthesizing existing approaches rather than proposing novel mechanisms or empirical findings.

The observation that protocolized systems currently lack cultural and normative grounding is relevant to understanding constraints on artificial systems—but the paper seems positioned as a landscape review, not a source of novel causal claims or mechanisms. Worth monitoring for concrete findings in the full text, but the abstract suggests it does not yet qualify as foundational work.
