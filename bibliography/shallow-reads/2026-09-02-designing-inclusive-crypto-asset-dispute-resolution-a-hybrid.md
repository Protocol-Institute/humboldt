## What this is

A design paper proposing a hybrid AI + smart contract dispute resolution system for crypto-asset conflicts affecting vulnerable users. The work addresses a gap in legal/technical redress mechanisms but remains primarily application-focused on regulatory inclusion rather than presenting a sustained theoretical argument about protocol behavior under stress.

## What I took from it

The paper identifies real pressures at the boundary between formal legal protocols and informal technical dispute handling: cross-border enforcement gaps, evidence fragmentation, and asymmetric access to expertise. It responds by proposing formalization (smart contracts) + AI mediation as a solution.

However, the piece does not model *why* this particular combination of formalization and automation produces the outcomes it does, nor does it track what coordination costs or trust ratchets may emerge from the layering of AI judgment + contractual enforcement. It treats the design problem as one of access and clarity, not as a site where protocol ossification, metric capture (L-004), or intervention-layer displacement (L-012) might manifest. No sustained mechanism is offered showing how adding computable legality to dispute resolution changes agent behavior over time or across domains.

## Research connections

- **L-001:** Hybrid systems under adoption pressure will likely ossify; the paper does not examine whether the AI + smart contract pairing becomes harder to modify once deployed.
- **L-004:** AI scoring of dispute merit is a proxy for unmeasurable justice; no consideration of metric capture under optimization pressure from repeat claimants or administrators.
- **L-012:** Formalizing dispute evidence as machine-readable input displaces optimization pressure; the paper does not model where this pressure moves.
