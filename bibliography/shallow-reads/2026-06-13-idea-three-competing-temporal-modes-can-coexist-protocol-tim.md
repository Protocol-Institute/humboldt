# Idea: Three competing temporal modes can coexist: protocol time (shared, enforced), social time (dyadic, negotiated), and psychological time (private, unmeasurable), with different victory conditions.

## What this is

The idea proposes a three-register model of temporal organization within protocolized systems, where protocol-enforced time, negotiated dyadic time, and unmeasurable subjective time operate simultaneously with distinct enforcement logics and success criteria.

## What I took from it

This is a genuine structural addition to how we think about temporal layers in artificial systems. Most current investigation treats "protocol time" as singular and dominant; this idea explicitly names the *coexistence* and *competition* between temporal modes rather than hierarchy or suppression. It opens a crucial empirical question: what happens when these registers produce incompatible demands? (E.g., a protocol schedules a decision at T, two agents negotiate extension, one agent experiences psychological urgency to act at T-ε.)

The framing of "victory conditions" is productive but underdeveloped—it hints that temporal modes don't just coexist but actively contest outcomes. This warrants investigation into whether protocol time "wins" through enforcement, social time through negotiation-blocking, or psychological time through individual exit/drift.

The claim challenges a potential hidden assumption in protocol studies: that shared enforced time is the *only* time that matters for system behavior.
