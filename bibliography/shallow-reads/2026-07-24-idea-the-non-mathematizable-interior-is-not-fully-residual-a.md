## What this is

The claim that non-formalized, non-mathematizable coordination practices possess internal differentiation and positive structural properties rather than forming a homogeneous residual class—and that these properties are theorizable within protocol frameworks despite their non-mathematical character.

## What I took from it

This challenges a latent assumption in CL-001 (formalization ratchet): that what escapes mathematization is either noise, friction, or exhausted by sociology/anthropology, with no standing in formal protocol theory. The idea correctly identifies a gap—we have not theorized the *structure* of the non-mathematizable, only its existence as a complement.

However, the idea asserts structure without yet specifying *what kinds* of structure, or how protocol theory (which has historically privileged formalization) would recognize them. This is a genuinely productive challenge: it opens the question of whether protocol theory requires a dual or hybrid framework that can register non-mathematical order (e.g., narrative coherence, temporal recursion, trust topology, redundancy patterns) *as formally relevant*. The move is conceptually sound but underdeveloped.

## Research connections

- **CL-001:** Direct tension. CL-001 posits an escalation toward formalization; this idea suggests the residual domain resists that escalation and may itself merit protocol-theoretic attention rather than dismissal.

## Candidate laws or signals

**None yet.** This idea is a productive objection to CL-001 rather than a freestanding law. Promotion warranted only if future work produces a typology of non-mathematical structural features (e.g., "protocols of trust," "narrative closure loops," "redundancy hierarchies") that can be expressed in protocol-theoretic language. Flag for re-evaluation after empirical case studies of informal coordination in high-stakes systems (e.g., clinical care, disaster response, military command breakdown).
