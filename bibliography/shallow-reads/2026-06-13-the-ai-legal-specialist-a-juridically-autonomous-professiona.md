## What this is

A professional-services analysis arguing that existing legal expertise (data protection, privacy, compliance) cannot adequately address the emerging global AI regulatory landscape, and proposing a new specialist role. The work is primarily a **market-gap diagnosis and role design paper**, not a theoretical or empirical study of how protocolized systems behave or constrain themselves.

## What I took from it

The paper observes a real institutional fragmentation: regulation of AI systems has outpaced the formation of coherent professional expertise structures. However, this is fundamentally a *downstream effect* of regulatory heterogeneity—it describes how human institutions *respond to* complexity in AI governance, not how artificial systems themselves exhibit law-like behavior or generate constraints.

The work implicitly assumes that juridical frameworks can be retrofitted onto AI governance through specialization. This may be worth tracking as a test case for whether "protocolized systems" (AI + legal frameworks together) exhibit emergent properties that resist modular professional approaches. But the paper itself doesn't investigate that—it prescribes a solution rather than analyzing the underlying generative mechanism.
