## What this is

The idea proposes that prediction markets operate as instruments that commodify temporal expectations *in advance* of broader economic stratification, creating a market in time-indexed claims before full resource consolidation occurs.

## What I took from it

The observation gestures toward something real: prediction markets do treat future states as present-tradeable assets, collapsing temporal distance into exchangeable tokens. However, the framing of this as "pre-commodification" is imprecise. Prediction markets don't *precede* commodification—they *are* a form of it, operating alongside existing commodity markets rather than before them.

What's more interesting, and partially buried: the claim hints at how protocols *structure temporal experience* through incentive alignment. Markets that price future events create epistemic pressure to act as if those futures are certain or probable *now*. This shifts when decisions are made and by whom, which is genuinely protocol-relevant. The temporal claim is stronger if reframed around decision-timing and information asymmetry rather than economic sequencing.
