# The Capability Transfer: Cohort Reversal, Codified Expertise, and the Adaptability Tax of AI Transformation

## What this is

An economic model paper studying labor market dynamics under AI-driven automation, focusing on how the codification and automation of junior-level work disrupts expertise pipeline formation in firms with cohort-based knowledge transmission. The core claim is that because codifiable tasks concentrate in early-career work, automation hits junior demand first, then collapses senior supply later as the pipeline of replacement experts dries up.

## What I took from it

This paper is primarily a labor economics artifact, not a contribution to protocolized systems theory. However, it does offer an *indirect* methodological observation: the paper exemplifies a broader pattern we should track — the assumption that formalization (codification) precedes automation, and that this sequence is deterministic rather than contingent.

The framing assumes a one-way ratchet: informal → codified → automated → disrupted pipeline. This maps onto L-003 (Formalization Ratchet), but as a *consequence* of economic pressure rather than a mechanism we can study in controlled protocol settings. The paper does not analyze the feedback loops by which codification itself changes what junior work becomes, nor does it model whether the pipeline disruption is reversible through norm regeneration or deliberate deskilling. It is a static comparative-statics model, not a study of protocol evolution under stress.

This suggests a gap in our current agenda: we lack models of *how formalization decisions are made* (and by whom) under automation pressure, and what preserves or destroys the informal knowledge-transmission structures that formal protocols are meant to replace.

## Research connections

- **L-003:** Confirms the basic claim that stress (here, automation threat) drives codification; does not explain the mechanism by which informal norms are replaced, only models the economic consequence.
- **seed-553:** Tangentially relevant — the paper shows formalization without reduction in coordination cost; the pipeline still breaks.
- **seed-544:** Metalinguistic displacement — codification changes what "expertise" means; the model does not track semantic shift.

## Method note

This paper demonstrates a common failure mode in AI-impact research: treating codification as exogenous rather than as a strategic choice embedded in protocol governance. The paper is useful as evidence that formalization *precedes* automation in labor contexts, but it does not investigate who decides what gets codified, under what institutional constraints, or what alternative pathways exist. Future work should model codification as a protocol design choice — an object of study rather than a background condition — and examine whether expertise pipeline collapse is an inherent feature of formalization or an artifact of specific governance failures.
