## What I took from it

The annotation positions this as empirical grounding for L-003 (Formalization Ratchet) — the claim that stress or scaling pressure drives replacement of informal norms with explicit formal rules. This case *reverses* that observation: it documents communities *retaining* informal coordination *despite* having formal protocols available, suggesting that formalization may not always propagate downward into lived practice, or that communities actively resist it when coordination costs favor flexibility over rule-fidelity.

If the analysis is rigorous, this would complicate L-003 by showing that formalization ratchet operates selectively — perhaps driven not by pressure alone, but by legibility requirements and enforcement infrastructure. Board games lack enforcement mechanisms; informal norms may persist precisely because they're cheaper to coordinate than tracking formal violations. This hints at a boundary condition: formalization ratchet may only fire when enforcement is *computable and externally legible*.

## Seed

**Seed title:** Enforcement Opacity as Formalization Barrier in Distributed Coordination
**Seed type:** observation
**Seed text:** Formal protocols do not propagate into lived practice when verification and enforcement require distributed or constant surveillance of agent behavior. In multi-agent systems where rule violations are cheap to conceal and costly to detect (high detection cost relative to potential gain from violation), informal norms — which rely on reputation and social reinforcement — may remain stable *despite* availability of formal alternatives. Formalization ratchet thus depends not only on stress or scaling pressure, but on the legibility and cost-efficiency of enforcement infrastructure relative to community size.
