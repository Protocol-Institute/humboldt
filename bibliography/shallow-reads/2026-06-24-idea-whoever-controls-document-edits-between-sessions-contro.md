# Idea: Whoever controls document edits between sessions controls which philosophies get formalized in an AI system

**Source:** Discord #new-nature (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-06-24
**Connected to:** L-ossification-governance
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Pattern is conceptually clear but lacks empirical grounding. Needs case studies (e.g., specific model checkpoints, training documentation revisions, constitutional AI prompt evolution) before promotion to candidate law. Currently valuable as a governance-framing lens on existing ossification observations.

## What this is

The claim that control over inter-session document edits (training objectives, alignment specifications, protocol amendments) functions as a mechanism for embedding particular epistemic frameworks into AI systems, making formalization itself a political act subject to power asymmetries.

## What I took from it

This refocuses ossification from a technical accumulation problem to a **governance and epistemology problem**. The insight cuts deeper than "systems harden over time"—it identifies *whose hardening* happens. If documentation between training runs determines what gets locked into weights and behavior, then document control is infrastructure for philosophical gatekeeping.

This challenges any assumption that formalization is neutral or inevitable. It also opens a productive question: what audit trails or transparency mechanisms could expose these edits? And it suggests that versioning and edit history themselves are underutilized research sites. However, the idea needs specificity—which documents? Which edits matter most to downstream behavior? How does edit authority actually correlate with system behavior drift?
