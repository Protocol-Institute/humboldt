## What this is

A systematic literature review synthesizing existing peer-reviewed sources on the tension between AI compliance requirements and innovation velocity in critical infrastructure sectors (healthcare, finance, energy, defense). The work is descriptive and organizational rather than advancing a new theoretical claim or empirical mechanism.

## What I took from it

This is a secondary synthesis mapping a *perceived* conflict rather than modeling the underlying dynamics. The framing assumes compliance and innovation operate as opposing forces—a reasonable observation in applied contexts but one that doesn't isolate *why* this tension exists at the protocol level, *how* it varies by sector architecture, or *whether* it's structural or contingent on current governance design.

For the new nature research agenda, this work could be useful as a landscape map (what problems practitioners report) but doesn't clarify the formal properties of regulatory protocol design that create friction with system adaptation. It confirms that critical infrastructure is a pressure point where protocolized governance meets high-stakes uncertainty, but doesn't advance the mechanics of that collision.
