## What this is

An empirical team dynamics paper introducing ADA (Attention-Directing Ability) as a latent construct predicting collective coordination and performance. The work frames attention direction as an emergent interaction pattern rather than cognitive state, grounded in econometric team studies.

## What I took from it

The paper operationalizes *how* coordination signals propagate within groups—a mechanism-level question—but does so through team performance metrics and interaction measurement rather than through protocol or formalization. It treats attention allocation as a capability distributed across interaction patterns, which is sociologically sound, but this is orthogonal to the core question of how *protocolized* systems ossify, capture, or decouple under legibility pressure.

The framing could be generative for L-010 (Coordination Adoption Nonmonotonicity) if reformulated: teams with high ADA may exhibit different adoption curves for new coordination protocols than low-ADA teams, and the interaction patterns underlying ADA might themselves become legible targets for optimization (seed-128, seed-069). However, the paper does not track what happens when ADA itself becomes a measurable KPI or when coordination signals are formalized into machine-readable protocol layers. The absence of this concern suggests the work remains in the natural (organizational) domain rather than the protocolized (artificial/algorithmic) domain.

## Research connections

- **seed-020:** Confirms that team coordination is substrate-dependent and traceable to interaction signal patterns; does not interrogate what happens when those signals are formalized or automated.
- **L-010:** Plausible indirect application if adoption heterogeneity correlates with team ADA; paper does not test this.
- **seed-128, seed-069:** High-ADA teams might become targets for legibility extraction and metric capture once attention patterns are rendered computable; paper does not address formalization pressure.
- **L-004 (Goodhart Generalization):** If ADA becomes an explicit optimization target (e.g., via team scoring systems), the mechanism by which attention direction degrades under measurement is not explored.

## Method note

This paper demonstrates that latent interaction structures can be empirically recovered and correlated with collective outcomes. However, it operates in the regime of *measurement without formalization*—ADA is inferred post-hoc from behavioral traces, not prescribed as a protocol. The research agenda would benefit from asking: what changes when such latent structures are rendered explicit, computable, and subject to agent optimization? This suggests a methodological need for staged studies: natural coordination → measured coordination → formalized coordination, tracking what breaks at each transition. The current work stops at stage two and would benefit from instrumentation designed to capture stage-three degradation.
