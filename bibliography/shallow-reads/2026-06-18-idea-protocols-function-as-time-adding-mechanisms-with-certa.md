# Idea: Protocols function as time-adding mechanisms, with certain entities accruing disproportionate coordination weight

**Source:** Discord #new-nature (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-06-18
**Connected to:** H-002
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Restatement of H-002 temporal binding claim with added emphasis on asymmetric power concentration; no novel mechanism identified yet. Warrants storage for refinement and empirical grounding rather than immediate law candidate.

## What this is

The claim that protocols operate as temporal structures that bind or delay action for multiple parties, while systematically concentrating coordination authority in fewer entities over time.

## What I took from it

This idea usefully *emphasizes the temporal axis* of protocol function—moving away from trust-as-the-primary frame toward time-as-the-primary resource being managed and unequally distributed. This is a directional refinement of H-002 rather than a new claim. The idea correctly flags that protocols don't just distribute trust symmetrically; they create temporal obligations and asymmetries.

However, the mechanism linking "time-adding" (delays, sequencing, latency) to "power concentration" remains implicit. Does power concentrate *because* some entities control timing? Because they are exempt from delays? Because temporal binding creates dependency? The idea would strengthen significantly if it specified which temporal property—latency, synchronization, ordering power, or escape velocity from protocol—actually produces power asymmetry.

This opens a useful sub-research direction: **mapping temporal roles within protocols** (who sets cadence, who chooses granularity, who can defer or accelerate).

## Research connections

## Candidate laws or signals

**none** — The power concentration claim needs mechanistic specification before promotion. Store as refinement note to H-002: *"Temporal mechanisms in protocols may concentrate coordination weight; investigate role asymmetries (timing authority, exemption from delay, cadence-setting)."*
