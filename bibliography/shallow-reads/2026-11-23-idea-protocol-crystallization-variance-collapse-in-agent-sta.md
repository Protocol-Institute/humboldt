## What this is

The claim inverts the causal direction in L-001: rather than adoption pressure causing protocols to become hard to modify, the primary phenomenon is convergence in agent beliefs and configurations about a protocol (*variance collapse*), which then makes modification difficult because the space of plausible alternatives narrows.

## What I took from it

This reframes L-001 from a **social friction model** (adoption creates switching costs) to a **state-space collapse model** (adoption creates consensus, which eliminates the cognitive and coordination room for variation). It's a genuine refinement insofar as it points at a different causal mechanism: not resistance to change, but unavailability of change.

However, the idea does not yet show *how* adoption causes variance collapse rather than merely *that* it does, or how this mechanism differs operationally from L-001's adoption-pressure story. The two could be observationally equivalent at the protocol level (hard to modify) while mechanistically distinct (social friction vs. state-space convergence). This matters: if variance collapse is the root, interventions should target belief diversity; if adoption pressure is, they should target switching costs.

The idea opens a pathway to seed-456 (Protocol Invariant Multiplicity Under Adoption Clustering), which already flags that adoption creates clustering in agent behavior. Crystallization adds the claim that clustering in *states* (not just behaviors) is what locks in protocol form.

## Research connections

- **seed-456:** Directly complements the multiplicity observation; crystallization would explain *why* multiplicity collapses under clustering pressure.

## Seed

**Seed title:** Variance Collapse as Primary Ossification Mechanism

**Seed type:** observation + question

**Seed text:** In protocol systems, widespread adoption may cause ossification not through friction or switching costs, but through convergence in agent beliefs, expectations, and operational configurations about the protocol—a collapse of the state-space variance available for alternative behaviors. Under this mechanism, the protocol becomes difficult to modify because agents have converged on a narrow set of interpretations, not because they face external resistance to change. The generalization is: *any process that homogenizes agent models of a system (adoption, standardization, training, reputation pressure) will reduce the variance available for local variation, making system-wide restructuring require coordination across a collapsed state space.* This differs from L-001 in falsifiability: it predicts that modification difficulty correlates with belief consensus, not merely with adoption scale.
