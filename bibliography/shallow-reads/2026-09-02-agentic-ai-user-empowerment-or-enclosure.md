## What this is

A comparative case analysis examining whether agentic AI systems empower or enclose users by studying four historical precedents (ad blockers, recommender systems, robo-advisors, spam governance). The work is primarily diagnostic and empirical, mapping outcome patterns across domains rather than proposing a novel mechanism or theoretical framework.

## What I took from it

The paper appears to test whether coordination cost conservation (L-006) holds across protocol layer transitions in agentic systems—specifically, whether user-facing agency tools displace rather than eliminate coordination friction. The historical cases may also illuminate boundary concentration dynamics (L-014): as agentic tools become legible and machine-readable, do optimizing platforms concentrate enforcement pressure at decision boundaries rather than diffusing it?

However, the shallow abstract suggests this is primarily a pattern-matching exercise across mature domains, not a mechanism proof or challenge to existing laws. The framing ("depends on more than the technology") is wisdom-of-practice rather than law-shaped inference. If the full text does not isolate a novel generative mechanism—e.g., a specific condition under which agency *necessarily* inverts from user empowerment to enclosure—this remains confirmatory rather than exploratory work.
