## What this is

A game-theoretic treatment of a classic collective action problem: institutions require critical mass adoption, but rational agents won't join until an institution exists. The paper proposes that *misperception* (false belief that an institution already exists) can resolve this paradox. Domain: institutional economics, mechanism design, common-pool resource governance.

## What I took from it

The paper frames institution bootstrapping as a coordination problem solved by epistemic friction—specifically, agents' erroneous beliefs that an institution is already operational. This maps cleanly onto L-003 (Formalization Ratchet) and L-006 (Coordination Cost Conservation), but only as a *special case* rather than a primary generalization.

The core insight is structural: the bootstrap problem is solvable only when agents condition on *false* coordination signals rather than true ones. This is mechanically interesting but highly domain-specific. The paper does not explore what happens after the institution formalizes, whether the false belief must persist, or how formalization pressure (L-003) changes the stability of belief-based coordination. It treats institution formation as a *discrete event* rather than as an evolving protocol system subject to ossification, metric capture, or trust dynamics.

The work is competent game theory but does not engage with the deeper pattern: *how do informal coordination norms (enabled by misperception or ambiguity) differ in their evolution from formally specified protocols?* The paper stops at bootstrap, not at the ratchet.

## Research connections

- **L-003:** Confirms that informal norms (here: false belief in institution existence) precede formalization, but does not examine what happens during or after the formalization transition.
- **L-006:** Suggests coordination costs are initially borne by belief maintenance; no analysis of cost displacement during institutionalization.
- **seed-241:** Directly cited triage connection; institution bootstrapping under uncertainty is a narrow case of the general seed.
