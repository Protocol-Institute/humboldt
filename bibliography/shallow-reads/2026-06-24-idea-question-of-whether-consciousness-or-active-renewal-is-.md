## What this is

A question of whether stigmergic trail persistence requires active conscious renewal or whether trails can self-sustain through passive/ambient dynamics alone—challenging the implicit agency requirement embedded in stigmergy descriptions.

## What I took from it

This idea surfaces a critical underspecification in how we currently frame stigmergy as a law. Most existing descriptions assume agents *must* periodically renew or reinforce trails to prevent decay—but the question asks: do trails require *intentional* renewal, *any* renewal, or do they persist through structural/material properties independent of agent action?

This opens three operative boundaries: (1) **decay curves** (how long do trails persist without reinforcement?), (2) **passive continuation** (can environmental or systemic processes substitute for agent renewal?), and (3) **consciousness requirement** (is the distinction between deliberate and incidental reinforcement meaningful for stigmergy to function?). The third is philosophical but operationalizable: does a trail "count" as stigmergic if renewal happens accidentally rather than intentionally?

The idea doesn't challenge stigmergy itself but clarifies what conditions must hold for it to operate. It's a refinement that makes the hypothesis testable rather than a new discovery.
