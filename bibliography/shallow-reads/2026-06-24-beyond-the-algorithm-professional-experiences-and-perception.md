## What this is

A qualitative multi-case study documenting practitioner perceptions of bias emergence and mitigation in AI/ML systems across healthcare, criminal justice, employment, and education. The work prioritizes lived experience and professional insight over sector-specific populations or theoretical framings.

## What I took from it

This is practitioner-centered empirical work on bias perception and mitigation, but it remains domain-applied rather than law-seeking. The study treats bias as a problem to document and mitigate rather than as a structural pattern inherent to protocolized systems. It provides valuable ground-truth data on how practitioners *experience* bias emergence, but the methodology (qualitative, multi-case, experience-focused) is not designed to isolate generalizable mechanisms or test whether bias follows predictable laws across system types.

The work may surface useful practitioner heuristics and mitigation strategies, but without sustained engagement with how bias emerges as a necessary property of certain classes of systems (versus contingent design failures), it functions as a descriptive inventory rather than a theoretical advance. The cross-domain scope suggests awareness that bias may generalize, but the execution remains case-study ethnography.
