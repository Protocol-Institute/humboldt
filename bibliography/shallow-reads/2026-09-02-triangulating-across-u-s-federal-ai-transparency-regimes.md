## What this is

A diagnostic audit of three existing U.S. federal transparency regimes (SORNs, ICRs, AI Use Case Inventory) applied to government AI systems. The paper documents fragmentation in disclosure: each regime reveals different aspects of AI construction and deployment, and no single regime provides complete visibility into how federal agencies design or operationalize AI decisions.

## What I took from it

This is observational infrastructure work, not a theoretical or empirical argument about a mechanism. It confirms that **institutional decoupling exists** between the formal disclosure layers (what L-015 calls "formal records and audit traces") and operational understanding — but the paper does not investigate *why* this decoupling persists, what incentive structures maintain it, or whether it generalizes beyond the federal AI context.

The fragmentation it documents is consistent with L-015's prediction that *interpretive continuity decays* when formal records survive intact but institutional understanding does not. However, the paper treats fragmentation as a problem of regime design rather than a law of protocol systems under asymmetric legibility. It does not explore whether this fragmentation is *functional* — whether it serves as a stability mechanism that would resurface under any consolidation attempt — which is the operative question for induction.

## Research connections

- **L-012:** Legible decision inputs (benefit denials, deportation flags) are now formalized; the paper documents that the formal record does not contain the causal or design logic. This is consistent with L-012's prediction of optimization-pressure displacement, but the paper does not track *where* the pressure moved.
- **L-015:** Direct confirmation that formal audit trails can be comprehensive while institutional meaning decays. No mechanism proposed.
- **seed-069:** Transparency regimes may be functioning as trust *proxies* (legible disclosure substitute for actual understanding) in the absence of substantive institutional coherence.
