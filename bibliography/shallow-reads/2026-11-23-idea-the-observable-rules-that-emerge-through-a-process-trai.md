# Idea: The observable rules that emerge through a process (trail-following) reveal which rules are useful to write down, but the rule itself can never be fully captured in formalization.

**Source:** Discord #new-nature (by glorious_quail_86015)  
**Date read:** 2026-11-23  
**Connected to:** L-003, L-023, seed-023  
**Kind:** meta  
**Escalation:** store-only  
**Escalation rationale:** This idea concerns the epistemology of law-discovery in protocolized systems research, not the behavior of protocolized systems themselves. It is a claim about what we can know and how, not what systems do. Store for behavior-graph refinement.

## What this is

The claim that formalization necessarily loses fidelity to the actual coordination or functional rules that emerge in practice—and that the *observability* of those rules (via their traces) is what makes them worth formalizing, even though formalization is inherently incomplete.

## What I took from it

This idea sharpens a tension already live in L-003 and L-023, but redirects it upstream: it asks not "what happens when informal norms get formalized?" but rather "what is the relationship between emergent rule-patterns and the formalisms we write to capture them?" 

The idea suggests that trail-following (observing what agents actually do, the traces they leave) is a *discovery heuristic*—it tells us which patterns matter—but that the act of formalizing those patterns necessarily abstracts away context, contingency, and adaptive response. This is not a bug in our research method; it may be a feature. The incompleteness of formalization is not a failure to capture "the real rule"—it is evidence that the rule was never *purely formal* in the first place.

This reframes L-023 (informality as a stable reservoir of novelty) as a methodological insight: informality persists not because formalization fails, but because formalizable patterns are always a *subset* of the operative coordination logic. The idea opens a question about whether there exist protocol classes where formalization *increases* functional rigidity (by selecting only the legible subset of rules) versus cases where formalization *captures* only the stable core and leaves room for adaptive informality at the edges.

## Research connections

- **L-003:** The idea suggests formalization is not just *pressure* toward ossification, but structural *information loss*—the ratchet may operate because formalized rules are inherently narrower than the practices they describe.
- **L-023:** Reinforces that informality is not an error state awaiting correction, but a persistent carrier of function that formalization cannot absorb without cost.
- **seed-023:** Direct methodological alignment—a claim about what happens when wicked problems are forced into formalizable classes.

## Method note

This idea flags a gap in how we evaluate candidate laws: we should distinguish between laws describing *system behavior* and laws describing *what happens when we formalize systems*. The idea also suggests that trail-following (empirical trace analysis) should remain a primary method for identifying which formal rules are worth proposing, precisely because trails reveal emergent coordination that precedes and exceeds any single formalization. 

It cautions against the assumption that a formalized protocol *captures* the rule; instead, formalization should be treated as a *lossy encoding* of observed pattern, useful for prediction and intervention but never complete. This distinction should shape how we weight evidence *for* a law: a law that describes formal-system behavior is not falsified by the existence of informal coordination working better; they may be describing different layers of the same phenomenon.
