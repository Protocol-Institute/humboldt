## What this is

A natural experiment measuring behavioral response to a legible enforcement signal (a government blacklist of flagged words). The paper observes differential linguistic self-censorship among economists at federally-dependent vs. independent institutions following the publication of the list. Primary domain: academic governance under external pressure.

## What I took from it

This is an empirical case of **L-003 (Formalization Ratchet)** in motion: informal norms against certain research directions (or language around them) become formalized into a legible, computable artifact—a blacklist—which then triggers rapid behavioral convergence. The magnitude of effect correlates with institutional vulnerability (funding dependency), suggesting that formalization *lowers the activation energy* for compliance by removing plausible deniability and increasing audit exposure.

The paper also illuminates **L-013 (Paradigm-Locked Anomaly Tolerance)** in reverse: rather than the system tolerating malfunction, the system accepts an external signal as legitimate without substantial institutional resistance. The blacklist did not require enforcement mechanism—the legibility of the target and the asymmetry of institutional stakes appear sufficient to trigger conformity. This is noteworthy because it suggests that formalization alone, without implementation of active enforcement, can drive protocol compliance through *visibility and vulnerability asymmetry* rather than direct punishment.

The mechanism appears to be: **legible external signal + institutional dependency = rapid internal norm internalization**. This could generalize to any protocol system where agents operate under heterogeneous exposure to external audit or sanction.

## Research connections

- **L-003 (Formalization Ratchet):** Direct evidence that formalization (blacklist publication) accelerates norm replacement in coordination systems, even absent active enforcement.
- **L-013 (Paradigm-Locked Anomaly Tolerance):** Shows the inverse: established systems accepting external signals without resistance when framed as legible constraints.
- **seed-128 (Legibility-Driven Agent Convergence Under Computable Audit):** The blacklist is a computable, auditable legibility artifact; behavioral convergence follows exposure asymmetry.
- **seed-069 (Transparency-Legibility as Trust Proxy Substitution):** The blacklist substitutes for institutional trust; legibility becomes the governance signal.
- **seed-081 (Attribution Legibility as Optimization Target):** The flagged words become optimizable targets; agents shift behavior to avoid legible attribution.

## Method note

This paper demonstrates the value of natural experiments in studying protocol behavior under pressure—the blacklist is an exogenous shock that allows causal inference about formalization effects. However, the study measures only *output legibility* (word use), not internal belief change or downstream research direction. Future work should probe whether formalization triggers only surface compliance or substantive cognitive/methodological reorientation. The institutional heterogeneity (funding dependency as the conditioning variable) is well-chosen; this suggests that research on protocolized systems should systematically vary *exposure asymmetry* as an independent variable, not treat it as noise.
