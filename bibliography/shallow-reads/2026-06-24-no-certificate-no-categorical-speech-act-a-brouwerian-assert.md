# No Certificate, No Categorical Speech Act: A Brouwerian Assertibility Constraint for Public Reason

**Source:** cs.CY updates on arXiv.org — https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03971
**Date read:** 2026-06-24
**Connected to:** none
**Escalation:** escalate-to-deep
**Escalation rationale:** This proposes a sustained mechanism for constraining artificial speech authority through epistemic certification — absent from current inventory — and generalizes across high-stakes domains as a governance principle for protocolized systems.

## What this is

A normative-technical proposal grounding the governance of AI utterance in constructive logic (Brouwerian intuitionism). The core argument: generative systems conflate confidence with justification, producing "hypersuasive" categorical claims without warrant. The corrective is a three-status interface requiring publicly inspectable certificates of entitlement (proof, audit trail, or delegated authority) before assertion — otherwise systems must return Undetermined rather than risk false authority.

## What I took from it

This work identifies a **mechanism of authority-slippage in artificial systems**: the conversion of probabilistic output into categorical speech acts, which displaces rather than distributes epistemic responsibility. The Brouwerian constraint is not merely a UI pattern but a *structural principle* — it makes undecidability a first-class speech status rather than a failure mode, and it ties this back to democratic epistemic agency (the human-side dependency for legitimacy).

What's novel: the work treats the *certificate* itself as the unit of governance, not the model or the output distribution. This suggests that protocolized systems generate legitimacy through *publicly contestable warrant structures*, not through accuracy or confidence calibration alone. This has implications beyond AI: it's a principle for any automated decision or utterance system operating in domains where epistemic authority matters (medicine, law, public policy).

The paper doesn't argue for abstention or silence; it argues for *epistemically honest* interfaces that separate what can be warranted from what cannot.

## Research connections

- **Epistemic authority in artificial systems:** How do protocolized systems acquire and maintain standing to make claims in public domains?
- **Speech-act boundaries in AI:** Where does probabilistic inference end and justified assertion begin? What structures enforce that boundary?
- **Governance through constraint architecture:** Can normative constraints be embedded as interface requirements rather than post-hoc regulation?

## Candidate laws or signals

- **CL-2603-1:** Artificial systems that assert categorical claims without publicly inspectable warrant structures displace epistemic responsibility rather than distribute it, weakening downstream democratic agency.
- **CL-2603-2:** Undecidability as a *positive status* (not a failure state) is structurally necessary in high-stakes protocolized speech — systems that suppress it risk authority capture.
- **CL-2603-3:** Legitimacy of automated utterance correlates with the contestability and transparency of its warrant structure, not solely with output accuracy.
