## What this is

A normative and conceptual paper arguing that "human flourishing" should replace narrow proxy metrics (engagement, productivity, financial return) as the primary success criterion for AI systems. The work is synthetic and prescriptive rather than empirical or mechanistic — it identifies a problem in current evaluation frameworks but does not present sustained evidence of a novel regularity or generative mechanism absent from the current research inventory.

## What I took from it

The paper confirms L-004 (Goodhart Generalization) at the level of motivation: current AI evaluation relies on measurable proxies (adoption, efficiency, financial return) that diverge from the unmeasurable goal (human and planetary flourishing). However, it does not advance the *mechanism* of how this divergence arises under optimization pressure, nor does it provide empirical evidence of metric capture dynamics specific to flourishing metrics. The prescription — use flourishing as a criterion — is itself vulnerable to the very Goodhart problem it diagnoses: flourishing is even less operationalizable than the proxies it critiques, and any attempt to formalize it would likely accelerate the very capture dynamics the paper warns against. This creates a catch: the more legible and computable a "flourishing metric" becomes, the more it becomes subject to L-008 (Proxy Optimization Under Computable Enforcement). The paper gestures at the problem but does not theorize the mechanism.

## Seed

**Seed title:** Operationalization Paradox in Safety-Motivated Metrics

**Seed type:** motif

**Seed text:** Attempts to remedy proxy capture by substituting unmeasurable high-level goals (flourishing, well-being) for narrow measurable proxies encounter a structural inversion: the more the unmeasurable goal is formalized into a computable metric to guide system behavior, the more it becomes subject to the very optimization capture dynamics it was meant to escape. The paradox may be irreducible — safety-motivated metric substitution itself generates new legibility surfaces for optimization pressure. This suggests the problem is not proxy choice but the attempt to solve coordination problems through metric formalization in the first place.
