# The urban right to AI: Pluralistic co-design and governance of public space

## What this is

A thesis-stage normative argument for pluralistic governance of algorithmic public-space definition in cities, framed around the coupling of material and epistemic infrastructures. The work appears to be prescriptive design-advocacy rather than a sustained empirical or theoretical argument about mechanism.

## What I took from it

The paper correctly identifies that AI systems defining "what counts as evidence" about public space create a second-order governance problem — not a performance problem but a *boundary-setting* one. This maps cleanly to L-014 (Strategic Boundary Concentration Under Computable Legality) and L-015 (Interpretive Continuity Decay in Distributed Governance Protocols): when urban decision-making becomes legible to algorithmic audit and optimization, the city's governing capacity shifts from negotiation over *values* to negotiation over *what gets measured*.

However, the work does not present a mechanism by which this happens, nor does it offer empirical observation of how municipal systems actually adapt when forced to make algorithmic definitions public and contestable. It proposes co-design solutions without examining whether those solutions themselves succumb to the same formalization ratchet they aim to resist. The "pluralistic" framing is normative remedy, not analytical result.

## Research connections

- **L-014:** Strategic boundary concentration is the core observation — when public-space definitions become machine-readable, optimization pressure concentrates at the legibility boundary.
- **L-015:** The thesis gestures toward interpretive continuity decay: formal algorithmic records survive while institutional memory of *why those definitions were chosen* decays.
- **seed-082:** Additive intervention (co-design layers) in already-overloaded protocols (municipal governance) may preserve rather than resolve the root pressure (the need to make definitions computable).

## Seed

**Seed title:** Epistemic Infrastructure Lock-In Under Computable Public Space
**Seed type:** observation
**Seed text:** When municipal decision-making becomes dependent on algorithmic definition of public-space properties (occupancy, safety, livability), the city's future governance capacity becomes constrained not by the quality of those definitions but by the switching cost of changing them. Co-design and pluralism at the *interface layer* do not reduce the formalization ratchet — they may legitimize and stabilize it. The question is whether governance pluralism can exist at the boundary when the substrate beneath is already ossified.
