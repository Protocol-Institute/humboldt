# Idea: Protocols differ from procedures in that protocol participants (clerks) maintain expectations about peer behavior derived from protocol knowledge, enabling coordination without centralized control.

**Source:** Discord #I imagine the gap is outline in that ZIP (by 4umd)
**Date read:** 2026-06-24
**Connected to:** L-001
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** This idea reinforces and operationalizes an already-recognized principle (distributed coordination via shared knowledge). It refines rather than extends the law inventory. Storage without escalation preserves the semantic distinction while avoiding duplicate candidacy.

## What this is

The idea proposes that protocols achieve coordination through *distributed epistemic states* (participants holding aligned expectations about peer behavior) rather than through hierarchical instruction or centralized enforcement—distinguishing them fundamentally from procedures.

## What I took from it

This is a useful *operationalization* of L-001's distributed coordination principle, not a challenge to it. The idea adds specificity: it names the mechanism (shared protocol knowledge → aligned behavioral expectations) and identifies the key actor (clerks as knowledge-holding coordinators). However, this mechanism is already present in L-001's framework—the idea clarifies *how* distributed coordination works rather than establishing *that it works*.

The semantic distinction between protocol and procedure is valuable for formalization, but it's a boundary-drawing exercise rather than a novel law. The real insight—that knowledge-sharing enables coordination without hierarchy—is captured. What the idea *opens* is precision in protocol design: understanding that clerks must maintain not just rules but *expectations about expectations* suggests protocols depend on epistemic transparency and shared interpretability, not just behavioral rules.
