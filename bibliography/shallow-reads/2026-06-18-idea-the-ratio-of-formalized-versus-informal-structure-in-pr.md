# Idea: The ratio of formalized versus informal structure in protocols may have lawlike properties rather than being merely contingent

**Source:** Discord #new-nature (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-06-18
**Connected to:** L-005
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Proposes a quantifiable dimension of protocol architecture with potential lawlike structure. Warrants empirical grounding before promotion; stored as a candidate signal pending case studies across protocol types.

## What this is

The claim that protocols exhibit systematic relationships between their formalized (explicit, rule-bound) and informal (tacit, adaptive) structural components—and that these ratios may follow regular patterns rather than vary arbitrarily across systems.

## What I took from it

This idea targets a real gap in current protocol analysis: we describe protocols as "formal" or "hybrid," but we lack a quantified account of *how much* formalization versus informality they contain, and whether systems across domains cluster around stable ratios. The idea opens a measurement problem: how do we operationalize "formalization degree"? Is it rule density, symbol explicitness, deviation tolerance, human discretion scope?

It also challenges a latent assumption in protocol research—that formalization and informality are design choices rather than constrained by deeper system properties. If ratios cluster predictably (e.g., by domain, scale, or failure consequence), that would suggest lawlike behavior. If they're arbitrary, the idea collapses. This deserves empirical testing across DNS protocols, blockchain consensus rules, medical treatment algorithms, and organizational procedures.
