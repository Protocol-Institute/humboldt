## What this is

An empirical audit of TikTok's ad targeting system using 56 automated accounts and 80,000+ video samples to measure how device price (as a proxy for income), interaction type, age, and gender correlate with ad load and ad category composition. The work documents differential ad exposure as a function of inferred user economic status.

## What I took from it

This is a competent instrumentation study that documents *that* proxy optimization and audience segmentation occur on TikTok — but it does not articulate the *mechanism* by which the platform uses computable income proxies to trigger intervention-layer displacement, nor does it investigate whether the targeting strategy creates second-order equilibrium effects (adoption of low-price devices to escape targeting; advertiser re-stratification; normalization of price-based audience parsing as a coordination standard).

The work confirms L-004 (Goodhart Generalization) instantiates in ad targeting: device price, chosen as a legible proxy for income/purchasing power, becomes the actual optimization target, decoupling from the unmeasurable goal (true willingness-to-pay or lifetime value). It documents L-012 (Intervention-Layer Displacement) in that the prediction surface (income inference) becomes legible to the allocation protocol (ad selection), but stops short of asking whether agents respond by modifying the prediction input itself.

The audit is domain-specific and produces no generalizable mechanism or law-shaped question absent from the current inventory.

## Research connections

- **L-004:** Device price as measurable proxy for unmeasurable purchasing intent; optimization pressure on the proxy decouples targeting from actual value.
- **L-012:** Income inference becomes a legible input to ad allocation decision; optimization pressure displaces from true intent to inferred signal.
- **seed-461:** Cited in triage but not visible in context; likely concerns dynamic pricing via opaque signals.
