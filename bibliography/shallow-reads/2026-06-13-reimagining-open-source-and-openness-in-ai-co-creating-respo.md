## What this is

A participatory governance study examining how diverse stakeholders (beyond industry) negotiate definitions of "responsible openness" in AI foundation models. The work is empirical and descriptive, focused on stakeholder deliberation processes rather than a sustained theoretical argument about how protocolized systems behave.

## What I took from it

This is a stakeholder mapping and normative alignment exercise rather than a discovery of mechanism. The core finding appears to be that "openness" in AI governance is socially constructed through deliberation—policymakers, researchers, and practitioners hold divergent framings—and that industry-led definitions have historically dominated. 

For the new nature research agenda, this confirms the *existence* of governance negotiation but does not reveal underlying laws governing how such protocols stabilize, fail, or propagate across institutional boundaries. It documents the phenomenon of competing protocol definitions without modeling the conditions under which one definition wins institutional adoption, or how technical constraints and incentive structures shape which definitions are *implementable*. This is valuable descriptive work but does not yet constitute a primary source for mechanism discovery.
