## What this is

A mathematical treatment of principal-agent contracting (Holmström-Milgrom continuous-time model) extended to include limited liability constraints and finite horizon. The paper develops PDE-based solutions to characterize the principal's optimal value function under these bounded enforcement conditions, using probabilistic methods to establish existence, uniqueness, and regularity properties.

## What I took from it

This is technical economics work on a well-established problem class — it extends a canonical model with one additional realistic constraint (limited liability) and solves it formally. The constraint is real and consequential (agents cannot be forced to pay arbitrarily large penalties), but the paper's contribution is solution technique rather than mechanism discovery or law-candidate identification.

The connection to L-005 (Gall Generalization) and seed-241 is real but shallow: the paper implicitly demonstrates that adding a legible enforcement bound (limited liability) forces restructuring of the optimal contract design, which is consistent with the claim that complex functional systems resist naive modification. However, the paper does not investigate *how* or *why* this occurs, what the costs of the restructuring are, what unintended consequences emerge, or whether the new system is genuinely safer or merely compliant. It is a solution to a constrained optimization problem, not an examination of the protocol system's response to constraint imposition.

## Research connections

- **L-005:** Confirms indirectly that adding a legible enforcement bound requires contract restructuring, but does not examine system resilience, side-effects, or cost displacement.
- **seed-241:** Limited liability is a formalized duty constraint. The paper solves for optimal behavior under it, but does not investigate whether agents find legible workarounds or whether the constraint shifts optimization pressure to unregulated margins.
