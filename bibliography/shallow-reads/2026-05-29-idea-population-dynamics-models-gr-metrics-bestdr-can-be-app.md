## What this is

The idea proposes that strangers-to-insiders ratio (as measured via population dynamics models like GR metrics or BESTDR) can serve as a quantitative proxy for adoption pressure and community maturation, thereby providing a measurable readout for the dynamics already captured in L-001 and L-004.

## What I took from it

This is a *measurement proposal* rather than a new law. It accepts L-001 (Protocol Ossification) and L-004 (Goodhart Generalization) as established and suggests an operationalization: instead of inferring ossification and metric capture retroactively from protocol change resistance, one could track them *prospectively* via compositional drift in the protocol community.

The value lies in potential early detection—if strangers-to-insiders ratio correlates with ossification *before* formal protocol resistance appears, it could become a leading indicator. However, the idea does not yet establish *why* this ratio should drive ossification (mechanism), nor does it clarify whether composition change is a cause, an effect, or a symptom of deeper coordination cost constraints (the substance of H-001).

This opens a useful clarification question: Is community composition change a driver of formalization and metric capture, or merely a marker?

## Research connections

- **L-001:** Strangers-to-insiders ratio may be a measurable proxy for the adoption pressure that drives protocol ossification, enabling earlier detection.
- **L-004:** Community composition turnover (influx of strangers) could mechanically drive metric capture if newcomers lack access to informal coordination norms and force formalization.
- **L-003:** The Formalization Ratchet may be *triggered* by demographic instability in protocol communities; this idea suggests a measurement pathway.
- **H-001:** Population dynamics framing is orthogonal to whether coordination cost is *conserved* across layers; methodologically useful but does not resolve the hypothesis.

## Candidate laws or signals

**none** — The idea is methodological scaffolding for existing laws. Promote to hypothesis only if empirical testing shows strangers-to-insiders ratio *predictively* precedes ossification independently of other factors.
