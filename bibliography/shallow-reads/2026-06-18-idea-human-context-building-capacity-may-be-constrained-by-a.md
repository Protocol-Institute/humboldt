## What this is

A claim that human cognitive ceilings may be partially circumventable through external (social/institutional) augmentation rather than biologically absolute.

## What I took from it

This idea introduces a **scaffolding hypothesis**: that biological constraints on processing speed or context window may not translate directly to constraints on *effective cognitive capacity* if humans embed themselves in institutional or social systems that distribute, cache, or sequence cognitive work.

The claim is **not new in principle** (distributed cognition, transactive memory, and institutional amplification are established in cognitive anthropology and STS), but it is **newly precise** in the research context: it asks whether the hypothesized 2.7 Hz ceiling can be functionally bypassed rather than merely worked around. This opens a critical question: *What is the unit we're measuring?* If the constraint is on individual synchronous processing, but humans operate in asynchronous, relay-based institutional pipelines, the constraint may not bind the system-level outcome.

This also **challenges a lurking assumption** in our research: that human-AI capability gaps are fixed by human neurobiology. If scaffolding works, the gap is plastic and negotiated at the system level, not predetermined.
