## What this is

An experimental economics paper comparing tournament entry decisions across three populations (South Korean, North Korean refugee, and Korean-Chinese) with different institutional histories. The work finds that NK refugees enter tournaments less frequently than SK and KC participants, but this difference becomes statistically insignificant when cognitive ability (Raven score) is controlled for, suggesting ability rather than institutional environment drives the effect.

## What I took from it

The paper is competent but operates at the level of confirming an existing behavioral economics mechanism (ability-driven risk aversion in competitive settings) rather than surfacing a novel regularity about protocol-based systems. The institutional variation is interesting as a natural experiment, but the paper does not develop how institutional memory shapes *coordination* behavior in the sense relevant to the research agenda — i.e., how agents signal willingness to participate in shared protocols, how institutional context alters interpretation of mechanism design, or how protocol adoption is conditioned on observable signals from peer populations. 

The finding that demographic/cognitive variables collapse the institutional effect is consistent with L-010 (Coordination Adoption Nonmonotonicity) only if we treat "institutional environment" as a coordination signal that gates adoption. But the paper does not examine whether NK refugees enter tournaments *together* (correlated adoption) or independently (no coordination signal learned). Without that distinction, it remains a study of individual risk preference, not protocol coordination.

## Research connections

- **L-010:** The paper treats institutional environment as a predictor of tournament entry, but does not investigate whether entry decisions are coordinated (conditional on observing peer signals) or independent. Without evidence of adoption nonmonotonicity (clustering, threshold effects, or signal-dependent timing), the connection remains latent.
- **seed-048:** Confirms that institutional context correlates with economic behavior, but does not uncover the *mechanism* by which institutions reshape how agents interpret or trust shared protocols.
