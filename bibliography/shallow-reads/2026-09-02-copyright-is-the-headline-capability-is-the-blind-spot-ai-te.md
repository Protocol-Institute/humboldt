## What this is

A rapid evidence review (89 articles, 9 languages, Nov 2025–Aug 2026) of trade and mainstream press coverage of AI in book publishing, coded for framing, stance, technical depth, and dominant voices. The work documents what institutional sensemaking systems *attend to* versus *overlook* in real time.

## What I took from it

This is a diagnostic of **institutional anomaly tolerance in action**. The finding that 30% risk-framed, 42% mixed, 28% opportunity-framed coverage coexists with a headline-capture on copyright (legal/rights) rather than capability (technical/systemic) suggests that established publishing institutions are perceiving and articulating *threats to their existing contractual and IP frameworks* while remaining largely blind to *capability gaps that could undermine protocol assumptions* — e.g., reproducibility, attribution, contamination, or model behavior under distribution shift.

The pattern fits L-013 (Paradigm-Locked Anomaly Tolerance): the publishing trade press is tolerating evidence of AI capability limits, training data problems, and output quality variance without triggering institutional restructuring, because these anomalies fall *outside* the paradigm's primary threat-detection apparatus (which is calibrated to copyright infringement, market disruption, and labor displacement). The copyright framing *insulates* the system from perceiving deeper protocol failures in verification, trust, and attribution — which are orthogonal to copyright law but central to sustainable adoption.

This suggests the blind spot is not accidental but **structural to the paradigm**: when a system's risk governance is built around legible, precedent-bound hazards (IP law), it systematically underweights illegible, novel hazards (capability boundaries, emergent behavior). The trade press is faithfully reflecting institutional sensemaking, not failing to report.

## Research connections

- **L-013:** Paradigm-locked institutions tolerate accumulating capability anomalies precisely because those anomalies are not legible through existing institutional threat models (copyright, labor, market share).
- **seed-030:** (no description provided in context, but likely concerns paradigm-bounded anomaly detection)
- **seed-062 (Formalization Opacity Collapse):** Copyright law is a *formalized* governance layer; capability gaps remain *informal*, distributed across technical documentation, user experience, and empirical failure — the press cannot easily legibilize them.
- **seed-072 (Explanation-Marker Decoupling):** Trade press articles can explain copyright risk without being able to articulate or mark capability risk because capability defects are not yet socially or legally *actionable*.

## Method note

This work reveals a methodological opportunity: **anomaly tolerance in institutional sensemaking is legible through media corpus analysis before it manifests as protocol failure**. Rather than waiting for systems to break, studying what established institutions systematically *fail to cover or frame* can diagnose which protocol assumptions are running unexamined. The multilingual, multinational approach is important — paradigm-locked anomaly tolerance should be observable across institutional contexts, and divergence would suggest institutional variation rather than structural inevitability. This supports treating press corpora as early-warning sensors for protocol fragility.
