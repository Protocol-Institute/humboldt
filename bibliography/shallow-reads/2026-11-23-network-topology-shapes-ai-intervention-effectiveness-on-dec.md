## What this is

An agent-based evolutionary game-theoretic study of deception dynamics in networked populations, examining how deploying AI agents (zealots) fixed to truth-telling or investigative strategies affects strategy adoption across different network topologies. The work extends the Deceiver's Game to structured networks and measures intervention effectiveness as a function of network architecture.

## What I took from it

This is a competent domain-specific application of evolutionary game theory with clear technical execution but narrow scope. The finding that "AI intervention effectiveness varies by network topology" is a **topology-dependence observation** rather than a law-shaped claim — it documents that the same intervention produces different outcomes under different network structures, but does not establish the mechanism or identify a generalizable regularity.

The work does not substantially engage with the core question of L-016 (Normative Intervention Algorithmic Retraining Effect), which concerns *how the adaptive algorithm itself* is modified by the presence of normative intervention. This paper treats AI agents as fixed zealots, not as learning systems that reorient in response to interventions. The network topology effect is real but orthogonal to the retraining hypothesis — topology shapes *diffusion*, not *adaptation*.

No tension with existing laws, no mechanism absent from the inventory, and no pattern that generalizes beyond deception games in social networks.
