## What this is

This is a mechanism design paper proposing a harmonic quota system for submission limits in research evaluation (journals, conferences, funders). The core argument: standard per-author submission caps treat solo and collaborative submissions identically, ignoring that reviewing burden is jointly attributable—the paper proposes allocating submission cost inversely by coauthor count (harmonic weighting) to align incentives with actual evaluation demand.

## What I took from it

This work identifies a fundamental misalignment in how protocolized research systems distribute resource constraints under collaboration. The harmonic quota scheme is a concrete instantiation of a broader principle: **cost allocation in collaborative evaluation systems should track the actual distributed demand they generate**. This matters because submission limits are increasingly primary control mechanisms in artificial research systems, and they currently create perverse incentives (either discouraging collaboration or concentrating quota on high-authorship papers). The harmonic approach is elegant but its adoption would reveal whether evaluation systems optimize for throughput efficiency or other values (visibility, author equity, subfield representation). This is a signal about how artificial systems handle multi-agent contribution attribution under resource scarcity.
