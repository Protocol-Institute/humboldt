## What this is

An empirical econometrics paper estimating price elasticity of computational resource demand in blockchain protocols using instrumented panel regression. The work quantifies how transaction volume responds to gas fees on Ethereum L1 and Arbitrum L2, addressing endogeneity bias from congestion feedback.

## What I took from it

This is domain-specific resource economics rather than a study of *protocolized system laws*. The paper solves a well-defined calibration problem—measuring demand sensitivity to fee signals—using standard econometric techniques (IV-2FE with lagged instrument). The finding of very low elasticity (-0.006) is substantive for fee mechanism design, but it is fundamentally an observation about user behavior under specific pricing rules, not a discovery about how artificial systems self-organize or constrain themselves.

The work does not propose a mechanism absent from protocol theory (fee responsiveness is expected), does not challenge established laws of resource allocation, and does not generalize beyond the specific L1/L2 fee structure. It is well-executed applied work within the existing frame of mechanism design, not a breach of it.
