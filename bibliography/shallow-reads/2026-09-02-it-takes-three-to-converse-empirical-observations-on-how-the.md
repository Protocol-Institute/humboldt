## What this is

An empirical study of Polis, a platform for asynchronous democratic deliberation through atomic statement voting, analyzing 119 large conversations to identify which design, facilitation, and participant factors shape outcomes. The paper treats the protocol system as a three-actor composition: platform developer, conversation convener, and participant population.

## What I took from it

The paper documents constraint and control distribution across three nested protocol layers, but does not develop a sustained theoretical argument about the mechanism or generalize the pattern. It confirms that formalization (L-003) occurs—Polis atomizes deliberation into discrete, votable propositions—but the work reads as outcome inventory rather than mechanism archaeology. The finding that "three parties shape the result" is observational; it does not explain *why* this tri-partite structure is stable, whether it generalizes to other coordination systems, or what happens when control incentives misalign between developer, convener, and participants. The paper documents *that* ossification and constraint propagation occur within democratic protocols, but does not isolate a generalizable law about how or under what conditions they do so. Connection to L-006 (coordination cost conservation) is potential but underdeveloped—there is no analysis of whether costs shift across layers or are truly conserved.
