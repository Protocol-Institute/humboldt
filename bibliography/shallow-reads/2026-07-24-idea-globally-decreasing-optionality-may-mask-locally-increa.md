## What this is

The claim that standardization or protocol enforcement can *simultaneously* reduce choice at the system level while increasing behavioral divergence *within subgroups*, suggesting variance is redistributed rather than eliminated.

## What I took from it

This idea challenges a naive model of protocolization as homogenizing and adds a useful complication: that global constraint and local heterogeneity are not opposites but can be structurally coupled. The observation speaks to how rigid coordination boundaries (enforced protocol adherence) might *force* adaptation and specialization within permitted zones, creating "pockets" of higher variance. This refines rather than contradicts CL-002 if CL-002 addresses cost conservation; here the mechanism is more specific—*optionality space* is compressed globally but reorganized locally.

The idea is generative but currently under-specified. What counts as "global" vs. "local"? How do we measure variance at each scale? Is this a prediction about agent behavior, organizational structure, or information flow? Without these anchors, it remains a useful intuition rather than a testable pattern.
