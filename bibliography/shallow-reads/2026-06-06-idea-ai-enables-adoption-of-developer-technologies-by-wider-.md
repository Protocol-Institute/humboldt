## What this is

AI intermediation can lower the technical entry cost to adopting protocols and distributed systems, potentially expanding the population capable of coordinating through them.

## What I took from it

This idea is a **mechanism proposal** rather than a law claim. It assumes technical barriers are a primary constraint on protocol adoption and proposes AI as a reduction lever. This is consistent with H-001's framing of coordination cost dynamics, but it introduces a specific causal pathway: *technical literacy → adoption velocity → coordination availability*.

The idea opens a useful refinement question: **Is barrier reduction equivalent to cost displacement?** If AI lowers technical barriers but increases dependency on AI intermediaries (or creates new protocol layers), has coordination cost truly decreased or merely moved? This tests whether H-001's hypothesis about conservation of coordination costs holds even when entry barriers fall.

The idea also suggests a testable prediction space: *wider adoption ≠ cheaper coordination* if the new population requires more mediation or generates new coordination overhead.

## Candidate laws or signals

**CL-drwip-026-001:** Barrier reduction via intermediary does not lower coordination cost; it distributes cost across new layers (intermediary dependency, AI literacy gatekeeping, abstraction fragility). Adoption breadth and coordination efficiency may decouple.

*Rationale: Worth tracking as a counter-signal to the claim. Promotes investigation of whether "accessibility" and "coordination efficiency" are actually orthogonal properties in protocolized systems.*
