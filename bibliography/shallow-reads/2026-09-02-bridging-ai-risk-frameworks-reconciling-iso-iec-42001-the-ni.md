## What this is

A comparative taxonomy paper mapping three heterogeneous governance instruments (ISO/IEC 42001, NIST AI RMF, EU AI Act) onto a unified conceptual framework. The work is primarily a reconciliation exercise — identifying structural incommensurabilities and proposing alignment pathways — rather than presenting a novel empirical or theoretical claim about protocol behavior itself.

## What I took from it

The paper appears to document a live instance of **L-001 (Protocol Ossification Under Adoption Pressure)** in real time: three independently designed governance protocols are achieving adoption momentum simultaneously, creating coordination pressure to harmonize or at least render them mutually interpretable. The incommensurability the authors document — legal status (binding vs. voluntary vs. certifiable), subject scope, and enforcement architecture — reflects the difficulty of retrofitting governance protocols post-deployment.

This surfaces a meta-level observation: frameworks designed to govern the same problem space but originating in different institutional contexts (ISO standardization, US voluntary guidance, EU regulatory law) tend to crystallize around incompatible vocabularies and verification models before alignment pressures become acute. The paper's existence is itself evidence that the cost of post-hoc unification is being paid *after* adoption has begun, not before. This fits the ossification pattern but does not directly generalize beyond the specific domain of AI governance frameworks themselves.

## Research connections

- **L-001:** Documents a live case of three governance protocols reaching adoption simultaneously while structurally heterogeneous; shows ossification pressure manifesting as taxonomy-bridging labor rather than protocol redesign.
- **seed-026:** Directly addresses the incommensurability cost of formalizing heterogeneous standards.
- **L-003 (Formalization Ratchet):** The three frameworks represent formalization at different stages; comparing them illuminates ratcheting dynamics in governance.
- **L-006 (Coordination Cost Conservation):** The paper's bridging work may itself be evidence that coordination cost is being displaced to the meta-layer rather than reduced.

## Method note

This paper exemplifies a category of work that is valuable for *documenting the state of a protocol ecosystem* but limited as primary evidence for laws of protocol behavior. It performs important boundary-mapping and incommensurability diagnosis, which should feed into case selection and framing for deeper empirical work. However, taxonomic reconciliation papers tend to assume the protocols under study are static objects, when the generative question is *why* the protocols diverged and *what mechanisms* prevent convergence or force costly bridging. Future work should treat such papers as problem statements, not solutions, and use them to identify sites where deeper causal analysis is needed.
