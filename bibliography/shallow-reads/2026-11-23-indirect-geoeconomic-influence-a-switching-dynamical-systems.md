## What this is

A game-theoretic mechanism design paper applying switching dynamical systems to model how one state can indirectly influence another by restructuring its internal political economy rather than applying direct coercive pressure. The work formalizes the transition between "permissive" and "constrained" modes of political-economic operation, where indirect pressure emerges from citizens and institutions rather than state-to-state action.

## What I took from it

The paper operationalizes formalization as a mechanism for indirect coordination capture—a domain-specific instantiation of L-003 (Formalization Ratchet). By rendering political economy legible through mechanism design protocols, the sender creates conditions under which internal agents optimize toward compliance without explicit instruction. This is mechanistically adjacent to L-012 (Intervention-Layer Displacement): the locus of optimization pressure shifts from policy makers to citizens conditioned by restructured incentive architectures.

However, the work treats the mode-switching dynamic as exogenously controllable and treats legibility as instrumentally unidirectional (sender sees target; target equilibrates under new rules). It does not examine what happens when the target nation's institutions begin to optimize *around* the formalized pressure surface, nor does it model the accumulation of interpretive burden that occurs when political economy must be held in dual-legibility (formal mechanism + resilient informal norms). The framework assumes stable equilibria within modes; it has no account of how formalization itself might trigger cascading re-formalization or norm-flight.

## Research connections

- **L-003:** Directly instantiates formalization ratchet in geoeconomic domain—pressure toward formalizing political economy to enable indirect control.
- **L-012:** Mode-switching maps onto intervention-layer displacement: optimization pressure moves from policy layer to citizen behavior layer once incentive architecture is formalized.
- **seed-553:** Coordination infrastructure formalization as legibility-without-cost-reduction—the paper assumes formalization *reduces* coordination cost for the sender, but does not measure total system cost.
- **seed-544:** Metalinguistic displacement under formalization pressure—implicit: what happens to informal political language and contestation norms when economy becomes formally legible?

## Seed

**Seed title:** Formalization as Compliance Architecture in Asymmetric Systems

**Seed type:** insight

**Seed text:** When a superior agent restructures a subordinate system's decision environment via formalization (rendering previously informal coordination legible and rule-bound), the subordinate system's own internal agents become compliance engines without direct intervention. This mechanism succeeds precisely when formalization is robust enough to constrain local optimization but not so rigid that it triggers norm-evasion or institutional workaround cascades. The generalizable pattern: *formalization transfers coordination burden from inter-agent negotiation to intra-agent constraint satisfaction*—and the stability of this transfer depends on whether the formalized rules remain interpretively stable as local agents probe their boundaries. Worth tracking as a subclass of L-003 and as a potential mechanism explaining L-013 (paradigm-locked anomaly tolerance).
