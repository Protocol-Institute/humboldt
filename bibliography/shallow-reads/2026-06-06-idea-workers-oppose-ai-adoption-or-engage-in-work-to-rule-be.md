# Idea: Workers oppose AI adoption or engage in work-to-rule behavior producing useless output to demonstrate AI's lack of value, framing adoption as a job risk rather than capability enhancement

## What this is

Workers deliberately degrade or withhold cooperation with AI systems to manufacture evidence of their incapacity, using performance sabotage as a trust-rejection signal and job-preservation strategy.

## What I took from it

This idea captures a second-order trust dynamic absent from simple technical-correctness models: adoption resistance may not stem from *actual* AI failure or genuine safety concern, but from *strategic framing* of adoption as existential threat. The work-to-rule behavior is performative—it creates the very incompetence narrative it claims to expose.

This complicates H-002 (trust-safety hypothesis) usefully. H-002 likely assumes workers assess safety based on observed system performance; this idea suggests workers may *engineer* poor performance to falsify that assessment. That inverts the causal chain: safety concerns don't drive resistance → resistance manufactures safety evidence → that evidence appears to validate the concern. It's a self-fulfilling deflection.

The idea also suggests that protocol adoption success depends not just on technical merit or actual risk, but on *whose narrative controls the interpretation of system behavior*—a governance and epistemic claim, not purely a safety one.
