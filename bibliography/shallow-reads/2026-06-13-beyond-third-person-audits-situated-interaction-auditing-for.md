## What this is

A methodological critique of LLM bias auditing that argues third-person audit frameworks miss a critical dimension: differential model behavior contingent on user identity/context during interaction. The work proposes "situated interaction auditing" as an alternative evaluation paradigm that treats the user as an active variable rather than external observer.

## What I took from it

This is a valid observation about audit methodology—that static, demographic-as-object testing misses conditional behavior emergence in dialogue. However, it remains within the bias/fairness evaluation domain and does not articulate a deeper law about how protocolized systems *systematically generate* different outputs based on contextual state. The insight is sound but local: it flags that bias audits need to be relational, not monolithic. This does not generalize to a structural principle about artificial system behavior or reveal a mechanism absent from current protocol theory. It is also primarily diagnostic (what we're missing in audits) rather than mechanistic (why systems produce these conditional behaviors at a fundamental level). No novel law about protocolization, emergence, or system behavior under interaction emerges here.
