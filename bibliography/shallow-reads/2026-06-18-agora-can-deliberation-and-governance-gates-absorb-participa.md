## What this is

An empirical case study applying agent-based deliberation simulation to a specific infrastructure domain (transit planning). The work treats participation bias as a controlled variable in governance mechanics, rather than proposing a generalizable mechanism for how formal deliberation structures absorb or fail to absorb selection bias in protocol-mediated systems.

## What I took from it

The paper is methodologically sound but narrowly scoped: it isolates one failure mode (self-selected attendance) in one decision domain (transit network design) and tests whether structured deliberation + stakeholder representation can compensate. This is valuable applied work but does not generalize to the protocolized systems we study. The core insight—that *who participates* remains an uncontrolled variable even when algorithms are fixed—is important for governance design but does not yet constitute a *law* about how artificial systems distribute outcomes.

The work assumes that bias is a problem *solvable* through deliberative gates and composition management. It does not ask whether participation bias is structurally *inevitable* in systems where participation is costly, asymmetrically informed, or incentive-misaligned. No theoretical account of why certain gate structures would work better than others across domains.
