# Idea: Protocol theory's utility includes functioning as a TCO (Total Cost of Ownership) accounting framework

**Source:** Discord #Protocols as Total Cost Estimators (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-07-24
**Connected to:** CL-002
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Proposes a methodological application of existing cost-migration insight rather than a new structural law. Useful as refinement and operational scaffold but does not yet isolate a novel pattern about protocolized systems themselves.

## What this is

Protocol theory can serve as a diagnostic and accounting tool for tracking cost displacement across system redesigns, revealing where labor, complexity, or risk migrates when protocol layers are introduced or restructured rather than being eliminated.

## What I took from it

This idea operationalizes CL-002's core claim—that protocol layer changes redistribute rather than reduce total cost—by proposing a **practical use case** for the insight. It frames protocol analysis as an ex-post and ex-ante accounting method, useful for designers and auditors who need to map cost flows.

The contribution is methodological rather than nomological. It does not propose a new law about how protocolized systems behave, but rather suggests *how we should look* at them to verify existing claims about cost conservation. This is a strengthening move: it converts an abstract hypothesis into an actionable analytical posture. 

However, it risks underspecifying *which costs* are tracked under the TCO lens (computational, labor, epistemic, regulatory, friction costs?) and *when cost migration is visible* (only at transition points? continuously?). The idea is ripe for testing but not yet for promotion without clearer operationalization.

## Research connections

- **CL-002:** Direct operationalization; TCO framing makes the cost-displacement claim auditable and concrete.
