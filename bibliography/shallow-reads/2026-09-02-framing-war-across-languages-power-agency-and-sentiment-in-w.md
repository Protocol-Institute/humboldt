# Framing War Across Languages: Power, Agency, and Sentiment in Wikipedia's Multilingual War Narratives

**Date read:** 2026-09-02
**Connected to:** L-003, L-015
**Kind:** empirical study
**Escalation:** store-only

## What this is

A comparative content analysis of how 20 Wikipedia language editions frame historical wars (158 conflicts since 1900) using connotation frames to measure power, agency, and sentiment attribution to combatants. The work documents systematic variance in how linguistic communities narrate the same historical events.

## What I took from it

This is a case study in **interpretive continuity decay** (L-015) across a distributed governance protocol — Wikipedia's multilingual namespace — but it remains domain-specific and does not establish the mechanism itself. The paper shows that formal audit trails (the article text, edit history, talk pages) survive intact across language editions while the institutional **meaning** of "neutral point of view" decays: the same war produces systematically different power/agency/sentiment framings depending on the language edition's author community.

The study confirms that formalization pressure (NPOV as a rule) does not eliminate cultural-linguistic heterogeneity; it merely displaces it into connotation space. This aligns with L-003 (Formalization Ratchet), but the paper does not investigate whether the ratchet *causes* this displacement or merely fails to prevent it. It is descriptive of variance, not mechanistic about causation. The finding is robust within Wikipedia's domain but offers no evidence that the pattern generalizes to other protocol systems or artificial coordination layers.

## Research connections

- **L-003:** The NPOV formalization did not eliminate interpretive variance; it shifted it from structure to frame semantics. Confirms that formalization under pressure does not reduce heterogeneity, only relocates it.
- **L-015:** Direct example of interpretive continuity decay — formal records (Wikipedia text) intact, institutional meaning (how combatants are framed) divergent across language editions. Documents the phenomenon but does not propose mechanism.
- **seed-069:** Tangential — NPOV functions as a trust proxy substitution in asymmetric-knowledge contexts (editor communities have asymmetric cultural/linguistic grounding), but the paper does not foreground this dynamic.
