## What this is

A design proposal for collaborative, wiki-like web platforms using AI-assisted editing to lower barriers to participation. The work reimagines social media sites (microblogging, messaging, dating, gaming) as collectively produced rather than owner-controlled, drawing structural analogy to Wikipedia's model.

## What I took from it

The paper is a conceptual and architectural proposal, not an empirical study or theoretical derivation. It presents an alternative governance model but does not provide sustained evidence of how formalization pressures would operate in this context, nor does it track what happens to coordination costs when wiki-style governance scales to real-time interactive systems (messaging, dating, ride-sharing) where immediate conflict resolution and state consistency matter differently than in encyclopedic content.

The connection to L-003 (Formalization Ratchet) is suggestive but underdeveloped: the claim is that AI-assisted editing reduces friction, but this could mask whether informal norms actually crystallize into formal rules under the stress of scale, conflict, or moderation disputes. Similarly, L-006 (Coordination Cost Conservation) is untested here — the proposal displaces ownership costs but does not demonstrate whether coordination costs actually decrease or merely relocate to moderation layers, consensus protocols, and conflict-resolution machinery.

The work does not challenge existing laws or introduce mechanism evidence. It is an aspirational redesign of a known class of systems (social platforms) using a known governance model (wiki-style collective production) applied to a new domain.

## Research connections

- **L-003:** Proposal assumes low formalization pressure in collective editing; no evidence whether real-time interactive systems (messaging, dating) exhibit different ossification dynamics than static-content wikis under adoption stress.
- **L-006:** Claims AI-assisted editing reduces coordination friction, but does not track whether total coordination cost is conserved or displaced into moderation, consensus, or arbitration layers.
- **seed-070:** Implicitly assumes coordination can scale without becoming infrastructure-binding; untested in synchronous multi-agent systems.
