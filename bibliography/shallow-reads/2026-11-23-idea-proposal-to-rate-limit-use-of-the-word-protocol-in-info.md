## What this is

A proposal that restricting terminology ('protocol') in informal spaces could prevent premature formalization pressure—suggesting that naming conventions themselves become targets of optimization and ossification as coordination scales.

## What I took from it

The idea observes that formalization pressure (L-003) may operate at the lexical layer: once a term ('protocol') becomes widely used to describe informal practice, it invites legibility, measurement, and standardization of the thing it names. This is a sharp meta-observation about how language can *accelerate* protocol ossification rather than merely describe it.

However, the idea conflates three distinct processes: (1) linguistic constraint as a coordination intervention, (2) formalization ratchet operating on terminology, and (3) ossification of the underlying coordination structure. Rate-limiting a word does not prevent formalization pressure—it may simply displace it to synonym spaces (procedure, pattern, norm, method). The proposal operates as a symptom-suppression rather than a mechanism intervention. It usefully highlights that naming conventions are not neutral, but does not establish whether lexical constraint breaks the causal chain or merely obscures it.

## Research connections

- **L-001 (Protocol Ossification Under Adoption Pressure):** The idea suggests ossification operates upstream at the naming layer, but provides no evidence that lexical constraint prevents structural ossification downstream.
- **L-003 (The Formalization Ratchet):** Strong connection—formalization ratchet does operate via legibility, and naming is a legibility gate. But restricting terminology may not block ratchet; it may hide it.
- **L-023 (Informality as Novelty Sink Under Legible Optimization):** The proposal treats terminology as a pressure point, but conflates informality-as-shelter with informality-as-naming-practice.
- **seed-461 (Proxy Opacity Under Nested Legible Enforcement):** Relates—rate-limiting language creates syntactic opacity, but does not establish whether this preserves functional informality or merely displaces optimization pressure to unseen synonym layers.
