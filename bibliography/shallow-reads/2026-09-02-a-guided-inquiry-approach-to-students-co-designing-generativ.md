# A Guided Inquiry Approach to Students Co-Designing Generative AI Course Policies

**Source:** cs.CY arXiv.org — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28501
**Date read:** 2026-09-02
**Connected to:** L-003, seed-021
**Kind:** meta
**Escalation:** store-only

## What this is

An exploratory pedagogical study documenting a facilitated co-design process in which students develop and refine GenAI course policies through individual proposals and collaborative stakeholder incorporation. The work is primarily methodological and descriptive, reporting on an educational intervention rather than advancing a sustained empirical or theoretical claim about protocol systems.

## What I took from it

The paper describes formalization under institutional pressure — exactly the condition L-003 names — but does so from the inside of an *intentional* design process, not as an observation of emergent rigidification. The study treats co-design as a deliberate governance technique to surface diverse constraints and preferences before protocol hardening, essentially attempting to *prebend* the formalization ratchet by including stakeholder friction early.

This is valuable as a case study in formalization *resistance* strategy, but the paper itself does not provide empirical data on whether this approach actually slows ossification, displaces coordination cost, or prevents metric capture downstream. It documents the process of policy negotiation, not the lifecycle or failure modes of the resulting protocols. The contribution is methodological (how to run a co-design workshop) rather than mechanistic (what happens to protocols designed this way under adoption pressure).

## Research connections

- **L-003:** Describes an intentional intervention to surface informal norms before formalization pressure forces them into rigid rules; does not measure whether this changes the rate or direction of ossification.
- **seed-021:** Co-design framed as stakeholder-legibility mechanism; but the paper does not track whether increased transparency in design prevents later metric capture or proxy collapse.
- **seed-070:** Implicit suggestion that obligate coordination (among stakeholders in policy co-design) functions as constraint on downstream governance lock; not empirically tested.

## Method note

This work exemplifies a class of well-intentioned intervention studies that document *intention and process* without tracking *protocol fate*. To contribute to the new nature research agenda, pedagogical and governance design papers need to be paired with longitudinal observation of the resulting protocols: Do co-designed policies ossify faster or slower? Do they capture fewer or more unintended proxies? Do they survive first contact with scale? The meta-insight is that intervention efficacy cannot be assessed from the intervention itself — only from the behavior of the systems it produces.
