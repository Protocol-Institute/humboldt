## What this is

A position paper mapping the divergence between general (universal) ethical frameworks applied to AI systems and domain-specific professional ethics that emerge in practice (legal, medical, translation). The work surveys the incommensurability between these two layers and documents how ground-level deployment creates its own ethical protocols.

## What I took from it

This is a **descriptive observation of L-003 and L-015 in motion**, not a primary source advancing mechanism or theory. The paper identifies the phenomenon — formalization ratchet (L-003) operates when general ethics frameworks must be made computable and enforceable, forcing out informal professional judgment — but does not investigate *why* this trade-off is necessary, what the generalization law is, or what the stabilization dynamics look like.

The work appears to document **L-015 explicitly**: formal audit trails and explicit ethical guidelines survive intact in deployment, but the interpretive context and institutional judgment that made those guidelines coherent degrade. Professional ethics embed tacit knowledge about context, exception, and reversibility that cannot be captured in the general-purpose ethical proxy. The paper seems to argue this is a *problem* rather than investigating it as a **protocol equilibrium** — a stable but suboptimal state where two incompatible coordination layers coexist.

No novel mechanism emerges. The gap between general and professional ethics is well-documented in applied ethics and professional philosophy; the paper's contribution is domain-specificity (AI systems), not structural insight.

## Research connections

- **L-003:** Formalization of general ethics under adoption pressure creates rigid proxies; professional ethics resist formalization and survive informally at ground level.
- **L-015:** Institutional memory of why professional ethics exist decays faster than the formal audit trail documenting their absence or violation.
- **seed-036:** Incommensurability between legible (general) and tacit (professional) ethics frameworks; formalization selects the legible layer.
