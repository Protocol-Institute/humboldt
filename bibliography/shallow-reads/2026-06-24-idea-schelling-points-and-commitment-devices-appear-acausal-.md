# Idea: Schelling points and commitment devices appear acausal but actually rely on present-time facts

**Escalation rationale:** Refines conceptual boundary of acausality claim; overlaps existing salience argument (item 14) but adds specificity to the causal substrate of apparent acausal coordination. Does not yet warrant candidate law—needs empirical cases and formalization of the "present-time fact" distinction.

## What this is

Apparent acausal coordination via Schelling points and commitment devices operates not through conditioning on futures, but through mutual recognition of present-time salience, credibility, or prominence—collapsing acausality into a correlation + prediction problem.

## What I took from it

This challenges the scope of acausality claims in protocol theory by proposing that what *appears* acausal is actually synchronous mutual inference on shared observables. If true, it reframes Schelling-style coordination as a special case of correlation-under-common-input rather than genuine counterfactual independence.

The idea is useful because it tests the boundary between:
- **True acausality:** agents condition on states they don't causally produce
- **Pseudo-acausality:** agents coordinate via real-time salience that both can perceive

This opens a question: can we operationalize "present-time fact" vs. "undetermined future" in protocol terms? And does the distinction hold when agents are themselves part of the system computing salience?

It also suggests that commitment devices (contracts, public pledges, reputation systems) may be less acausal and more "anchored coordination"—which has implications for how we model trust and credibility in protocolized systems.
