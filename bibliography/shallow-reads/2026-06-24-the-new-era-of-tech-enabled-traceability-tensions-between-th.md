## What this is

A governance and implementation study examining the gap between FDA's standardized data protocol vision for food traceability and the heterogeneous, materially-constrained operational contexts of supply chain actors. The work appears to be empirical/qualitative research documenting stakeholder resistance, adaptation, and workaround patterns rather than advancing a sustained theoretical argument about protocol-system dynamics.

## What I took from it

The paper documents a classic implementation failure mode in protocolized systems: centralized design assumes standardized data formats and harmonized reporting, but distributed stakeholders operate under resource constraints, legacy systems, and competing incentive structures that force deviation from protocol specification. This is recognizable as a "gap between formalism and practice" phenomenon but does not appear to isolate a *mechanism* about how such gaps propagate or stabilize, nor does it propose a generalizable law about protocol brittleness or stakeholder-induced protocol drift.

The framing ("tensions") suggests descriptive institutional analysis rather than causal investigation. Without access to full text, it is unclear whether the paper generates novel insight into *why* tech-enabled governance fails in heterogeneous domains or simply catalogs the failure modes we already recognize from principal-agent and organizational sociology literatures.
