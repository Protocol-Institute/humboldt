# "Are you an AI?" Analyzing Client Suspicion of AI Use in Crisis Counseling

**Source:** cs.CY updates on arXiv.org — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18261
**Date read:** 2026-06-24
**Connected to:** none
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** 

## What this is

Empirical analysis of 75,777 crisis counseling conversations on WhatsApp examining client detection of and response to suspected AI involvement in human-staffed helpline services. The work characterizes frequency, triggers, and mitigation patterns in a real-world high-stakes domain where trust and perceived authenticity are operationally critical.

## What I took from it

This is primarily a **perception and trust engineering study** rather than a contribution to the mechanisms governing artificial systems themselves. It documents the *symptoms* of AI suspicion in a constrained domain (text-based crisis support) but does not theorize why those triggers work, how counselor response patterns evolve under suspicion pressure, or whether suspicion itself follows predictable laws across different contexts.

The work is valuable for understanding **performance legitimacy** — the gap between functional competence and perceived authenticity — but it operates at the level of case observation, not mechanism discovery. It tells us *that* clients notice certain linguistic or behavioral patterns and *that* counselors have intuitive mitigation strategies, but does not isolate the underlying rules governing when suspicion arises or how it propagates.

## Research connections

- **Perception vs. substrate:** Confirms that users actively probe for ground-truth about system identity even when functional performance is adequate, suggesting perception management is a *separate* design problem from capability alignment.

## Candidate laws or signals

**CL-2606.18261-1:** In high-stakes, synchronous, intimate interactions, user suspicion of artificial mediation correlates with perceptual uncanny valley effects (deviation from expected linguistic/temporal patterns) rather than with stated system identity.
