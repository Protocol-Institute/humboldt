## What this is

The claim proposes that the ABCDE counseling model (Activating event, Belief, Consequence, Disputation, Energization) functions as a stigmergic protocol—where discrete, sequentially-ordered artifacts coordinate practitioner behavior without centralized instruction.

## What I took from it

This idea attempts to operationalize stigmergy in a familiar, institutionalized domain: therapeutic practice. Unlike abstract invocations of stigmergy, it anchors the concept to a named, widely-adopted framework with clear sequential steps. This is productive because it forces the question: *what is the unit of coordination here?* Is it the practitioner's attention? Client-practitioner dialogue? The artifact's own structure enforcing turn-taking?

The idea also surfaces a potential confusion worth tracking: whether stigmergy in protocols differs from simple *sequential workflow design* or *artifact-mediated instruction*. A protocol that guides behavior through structure is not automatically stigmergic—that term carries specific implications about emergent coordination from simple, local interactions. The ABCDE model may exemplify coordination, but whether it exemplifies *stigmergy specifically* depends on whether the coordination arises from the artifacts themselves or from the protocol's prescriptive design.

This suggests the need for a boundary-clarifying hypothesis about what distinguishes stigmergic protocols from instructional or sequential ones.
