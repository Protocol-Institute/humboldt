## What this is

An economic theory paper proposing constrained delegation as a mechanism to counteract agent information avoidance under reputational pressure. The core claim: when agent career outcomes depend on decision legibility, agents rationally avoid costly information acquisition and select politically safe actions; constraining the choice set reshapes reputational stakes and restores information-seeking incentives. Domain-specific solution to a principal-agent problem.

## What I took from it

The paper documents a real displacement effect under L-012 conditions (formalized, legible performance metrics creating optimization pressure). However, the contribution is a narrow intervention — constraining choice sets — rather than an explanation of the underlying mechanism or a generalization pattern. The work confirms that **when agent performance becomes precisely measurable, optimization pressure migrates away from task quality toward reputation management**, but offers no evidence that this pattern scales beyond delegated decision-making contexts or that constraint-based solutions work across protocol classes.

The paper does not engage with why constraints work mechanically (is it uncertainty reduction? reputational redistribution? anchoring?), whether the effect persists under different governance structures, or how constraints themselves become ossified or gamed. It reads as competent applied economics rather than a law-level contribution.

## Research connections
