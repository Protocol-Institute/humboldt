## What this is

Protocols formalize *asymmetric expectation structures* between role-bearers (clerks/attendants) and participants (guests), whereas procedures formalize only linear action sequences; this difference in semantic content is foundational to why protocols govern complex systems where participant behavior is contingent and interpretable.

## What I took from it

This refines L-001 by pinpointing *where* the protocol–procedure boundary lives: not in complexity alone, but in the explicit encoding of **what the system expects of participants**. A procedure says "do X, then Y, then Z." A protocol says "do X; *expect* the guest may do A, B, or C; *then* you do Y or Z accordingly."

The idea is valuable as a conceptual sharpening—it suggests that protocols are inherently *dialogical* in their formalism, building participant anticipation into the role description itself. This opens a useful line: protocols embed a **typed expectation space** (the set of moves a guest is modeled as capable of making), whereas procedures flatten this into a single canonical path.

However, this distinction is already the substance of L-001. The current idea is a useful *gloss* on why that distinction matters (expectation typing), but does not introduce a new empirical claim or a fresh hypothesis about how such systems behave or fail.
