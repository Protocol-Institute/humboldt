## What this is

A proof-of-concept agentic LLM system (EeVA) designed to scaffold ethical deliberation by applying multiple ethical frameworks to use cases rather than generating single normative answers. The work is a systems engineering contribution demonstrating workflow architecture for multi-framework ethical reasoning.

## What I took from it

This work sits at the intersection of prompt engineering and applied ethics but does not constitute a sustained theoretical or empirical investigation into the *laws* governing how agentic systems behave under constraint. The core contribution is pragmatic: showing that an LLM workflow can be structured to produce comparative rather than prescriptive outputs.

For the new nature agenda, this is instructive as a *negative case*. It demonstrates that agentic systems can be designed to *suppress* definitive output in favor of deliberative framing, but the paper does not investigate the underlying mechanisms: whether this is stable under adversarial use, how the system fails, whether the comparative mode is genuinely multi-perspective or collapses toward latent consensus, or whether the workflow architecture itself introduces systematic bias in which frameworks are "heard." These are questions about the *natural laws* of how protocolized systems handle goal-constraint conflicts.
