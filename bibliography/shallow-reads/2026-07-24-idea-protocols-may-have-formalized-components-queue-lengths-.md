# Idea: Protocols may have formalized components (queue lengths, message counts, timing)

**Source:** Discord #Protocols as Total Cost Estimators (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-07-24
**Connected to:** CL-001
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** This idea names a structural distinction (formal vs. informal load-bearing elements) that refines existing hypothesis territory rather than introducing a new mechanism. The claim is mechanistically sound but duplicates the decomposition work already underway in CL-001. Storing as evidence for layering taxonomy without promotion until we have empirical cases showing *which* informal elements resist mathematization and *why*.

## What this is

Protocols exhibit a two-layer structure: mathematizable formal components (queues, counts, timing) that capture structural capacity, and informal coordination norms that carry functional weight but escape formalization, suggesting formalization pressure captures topology but loses pragmatic load.

## What I took from it

This directly articulates what CL-001 gestures toward—the *mechanism* by which formalization ratchets. Rather than asking whether protocols formalize, it asks what formalization *preserves and loses*. The idea correctly identifies that queue-length and message-count metrics are tractable to optimization and governance, while informal norms (trust signals, exception-handling cultures, escalation heuristics) remain opaque to the formalizing gaze.

The challenge here is real: this suggests that optimizing the formal layer may systematically degrade the informal layer's capacity to absorb shock, distribute judgment, or tolerate ambiguity. But the idea stops short of specifying *which* informal elements are truly load-bearing vs. which are vestigial. We need cases.

## Research connections

- **CL-001:** Directly explains the mechanism—formalization captures structure (queues, timing) while leaving coordination norms untouched or damaged.
- **Formalization Ratchet (hypothesis):** Supplies the asymmetry: formal optimization is legible and governable; informal loss is diffuse and attributed to "culture" rather than system design.

## Candidate laws or signals

**none** — CL-001 already contains this decomposition. This idea is a refinement of *how* formalization splits protocols, not a new law. Promote to active hypothesis refinement only when we have documented cases where formal optimization of queue metrics correlates with measurable informal-norm degradation.
