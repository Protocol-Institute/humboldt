## What this is

A conceptual/policy paper introducing "digital statecraft" as an organizing framework for understanding state governance of algorithmic and data infrastructure. The work argues that digitization doesn't merely extend traditional state governance but fundamentally restructures the statecraft problem, leaving ability and legitimacy misaligned across the public-private-technical divide.

## What I took from it

The paper positions itself as foundational conceptual work, but the abstract and available framing suggest it operates primarily at the level of governance institutions and state capacity rather than as sustained mechanistic investigation of protocol systems themselves. The triage connection to L-003 (Formalization Ratchet), L-012 (Intervention-Layer Displacement), and L-015 (Interpretive Continuity Decay) is suggestive, but the shallow read reveals these are deployed as motivating examples rather than as objects of systematic study.

The work appears to address a real gap — the institutional substrate through which algorithmic systems are *governed* — but does not appear to offer new mechanistic laws about how formalization, legibility, and optimization pressure interact within that governance layer itself. It may document *that* continuity decays or *that* legibility shifts the intervention locus, but without tracing the generative mechanism or cross-domain pattern.

## Research connections

- **L-003 (Formalization Ratchet):** Governance pressure toward formalizing state control of digital infrastructure may instantiate the ratchet, but this is mentioned as context, not investigated as mechanism.
- **L-012 (Intervention-Layer Displacement):** The shift from direct state rule to algorithmic intermediation likely produces displacement of intervention locus, but the paper frames this as a legitimacy problem, not a protocol dynamics problem.
- **L-015 (Interpretive Continuity Decay):** State institutions maintaining formal records of algorithm governance while institutional understanding degrades is thematically present, but not developed as empirical pattern.
- **seed-071 (Expressiveness Floor in Coordination Protocols):** State governance as irreducible residual coordination layer may connect, but not explicitly developed.
