## What this is

A pedagogical intervention paper describing a course that teaches advocacy and organizational change skills to researchers using the UNICEF five-step advocacy cycle. The work addresses the gap between motivation and effective institutional action in the research community, focusing on sustainable digital research practices.

## What I took from it

This is a meta-level response to a coordination problem: researchers understand systemic issues but lack institutional literacy to execute change. The paper does not itself study protocolized systems or propose mechanisms of protocol behavior. However, it is worth noting that the framing assumes institutional change requires skill transfer rather than structural incentive alignment—a revealing assumption about the nature of coordination failure in research governance.

The pedagogical structure (UNICEF cycle) itself is a protocol for converting individual motivation into collective action. The implicit hypothesis is that the bottleneck is *capability*, not incentive. This may be testable against L-003 (Formalization Ratchet) and L-005 (Gall Generalization): if institutional systems resist restructuring, then teaching advocacy without changing the protocol architecture may produce capacity without effect. The paper does not investigate this risk.

## Method note

This paper reveals an assumption worth interrogating in protocol research: that institutional friction is primarily a skills or awareness problem rather than a structural one. Future work should empirically test whether advocacy skill training produces measurable institutional change in protocol-bound systems, or whether it simply accumulates frustration. This would inform whether L-005 (Gall Generalization) predicts stronger than L-003 (Formalization Ratchet) in research governance contexts.
