## What this is

Legible AI system outputs exhibit context-dependent instability (version, temporal, prompt, effort variations), creating hidden coordination overhead as organizations manage inconsistency that formal protocols were supposed to eliminate.

## What I took from it

This observation is a sharp symptom, not a novel mechanism. It documents what happens when you try to ossify (L-001) or formalize (L-003) a system with inherent output variance: the coordination cost doesn't disappear, it migrates to *managing the variance itself*—versioning strategies, prompt engineering, sampling, retry logic, human review gates.

The claim sits squarely inside L-006's prediction: when a protocol layer is replaced (formal model deployments replacing informal human judgment), coordination costs are conserved, not reduced. Here the cost shows up as temporal/contextual management overhead. This is a refinement of *where* the cost hides, not evidence of a new law.

The connection to seed-144 is direct: informality becomes a refuge under substitution pressure because *informality can absorb variance without formalization overhead*. The more you try to make model outputs legible and consistent, the more work you push into the layer below (prompt formulation, version management, contextual tuning). This is not a bug in L-006—it's the predicted mechanism.

## Research connections

- **L-006:** Direct instance of cost conservation under protocol layer transition. Model formalization does not reduce coordination cost; it redistributes it to variance management and contextual adaptation.
- **seed-144:** Informality (temporal/contextual tweaking, prompt engineering, selective deployment by version) becomes the refuge when formal protocol (frozen model version, standardized prompt) creates coordination friction.
- **L-003:** The Formalization Ratchet predicts this: as pressure mounts to standardize outputs, informal practices (version selection, time-of-day tuning, effort calibration) accumulate as hidden coordination shadow.
