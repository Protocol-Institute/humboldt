# What Prediction Markets Can See: Market Formation, Settlement Legibility, and the Geography of Tradable Uncertainty in Africa and Latin America

## What this is

An empirical study of prediction market formation across 6,047 Africa- and Latin America-topic contracts on Polymarket and Kalshi. Rather than evaluating market accuracy post-hoc, the authors shift focus upstream: which uncertainties *become tradable at all*? They construct and validate a measure of "settlement legibility"—the institutional workability of resolving a contract through credible third-party sourcing.

## What I took from it

This work identifies a critical filtering mechanism in protocolized systems: **legibility precedes tradability**. Markets do not simply price all uncertainties; they can only price those uncertainties that can be *written into resolvable contracts*. Settlement legibility acts as a hard institutional boundary. This suggests that the observable reality of a protocol-mediated system is not neutral—it is shaped by what can be formalized, sourced, and verified.

The geographic/topical scope (Africa and Latin America) is methodologically significant: by studying markets on these regions *from outside those regions*, the authors expose how institutional legibility itself is often externally constrained. A local uncertainty may be highly salient but *illegible* to remote settlement authorities, and thus invisible to the market. This hints at a deeper principle: **protocols encode geography and epistemic authority**. What a system can know depends on what its settlement layer can credential.

## Research connections

- **Protocol-reality coupling:** Settlement legibility is a material constraint on what artificial systems operationalize as "real" or tradable.
- **Institutional legibility hypothesis (adjacent):** Builds on the principle that formalized systems require legible boundaries; extends it to show these boundaries are *selective*, not neutral.

## Candidate laws or signals

- **CL-2606.17503-1:** *Legibility gatekeeping* — In protocolized markets, institutional legibility of settlement is a necessary precondition for contract formation; uncertainties fail to become tradable not because they are unpredictable, but because they are institutionally illegible.

- **CL-2606.17503-2:** *Epistemic asymmetry in protocol design* — The settlement authority's ability to verify an outcome determines which local uncertainties become visible to a distributed system; protocols thus encode distance and institutional hierarchy into what they can "see."
