## What this is

An empirical study of voting concentration in DAO governance mechanisms, decomposing support distribution across proposal choices into observable behavioral factors: approval stance, list position, and author preference. The work treats token-weighted voting as a proxy mechanism and maps behavioral biases that distort the mapping between voting power and actual choice distribution.

## What I took from it

The paper documents that token-weighted voting—a computable, legible proxy for "weighted collective preference"—produces systematically skewed outcomes driven by presentation and framing rather than underlying preference intensity. The three factors (approval orientation, position, authorship) are all *choice-presentation* variables, not preference variables. This confirms L-004 territory: the proxy (token weight) is now being optimized against by agents controlling *how choices are presented* rather than by improving the decision itself.

However, this is a **behavioral bias story**, not a mechanism story about protocol design or strategic incentive concentration. The paper appears to document choice-list framing effects and status-quo bias in voting interfaces—important for UX design, but the connection to L-014 (strategic boundary concentration under computable legality) is thin. L-014 predicts agents will concentrate optimization at *protocol boundaries* when obligations become machine-readable; this study shows agents optimize *presentation layer* within a fixed voting rule.

The work does not challenge or extend the law inventory. It is a competent domain study with limited generalization beyond DAO voting mechanics.

## Research connections

- **L-004:** Token-weighted voting is a measurable proxy for unmeasurable "legitimate collective preference"; the study shows optimization pressure has moved to choice *presentation* rather than voting mechanism reform.
- **L-014:** Weak connection—the paper does not study strategic agents concentrating optimization at protocol/legality boundaries; it documents behavioral biases in voting interfaces.
- **seed-052:** Tangential—the concentration pattern could relate to competition reversing homogenization, but the paper does not examine multi-DAO competition dynamics.
