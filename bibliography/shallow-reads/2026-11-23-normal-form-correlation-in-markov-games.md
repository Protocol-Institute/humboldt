## What this is

A game-theoretic paper establishing the first efficient algorithm for computing normal-form correlated equilibria (NFCEs) in finite-horizon Markov games with fixed player count. The work extends prior results on weaker correlated equilibrium concepts and closes a computational gap left open since Papadimitriou & Roughgarden (2008).

## What I took from it

This is a solid theoretical contribution to multi-agent coordination mechanics, but it operates entirely within the equilibrium-finding frame — it assumes agents can coordinate via a trusted mediator or correlating device and focuses on the computation problem given that assumption. It does not address how such coordination emerges, persists, or breaks down under adoption pressure, opacity, or strategic deviation incentives in real protocol systems. The paper has no engagement with protocol ossification, metric capture, trust ratchets, or the dynamics of formalization under pressure — all core concerns of L-001 through L-007. It is a tool paper, not a law paper.
