## What I took from it

The paper appears to offer a rigorous mathematical substrate for understanding how agents interact with protocols: perception is not direct access to reality but construction of measurable partitions over informational fields. This directly addresses a gap in existing protocol theory—namely, *how* agents actually implement protocols when perception itself is mediated by formal structure.

The emphasis on sigma-algebras (the mathematical formalization of measurable partitions) suggests the authors are translating older frameworks from probability theory and measure theory into a language that can describe artificial perception. If the core argument holds, it implies that all agentic interaction with protocolized systems is fundamentally an act of *distinction-drawing*—and that protocols constrain *which distinctions are available*. This could be foundational to understanding protocol capture, emergent agency, and the grammar of artificial cognition.
