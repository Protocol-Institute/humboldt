## What this is

An experimental study (N=181, factorial design) testing combined effects of three digital nudges (anchoring, salience, social proof) on stopping decisions in a secretary problem task. Reports counterintuitive results: anchors increased exploration rather than constraining it, suggesting nudges interact antagonistically in layered architectures rather than additively.

## What I took from it

The paper documents a localized phenomenon—nudge interaction effects in a constrained sequential decision task—but does not sustain a theoretical argument about *why* antagonism emerges, under what conditions it generalizes, or what this implies for protocol design more broadly. The finding that an anchor nudge *increased* rather than *decreased* search is empirically interesting but treated as an isolated anomaly rather than evidence for a mechanism. 

The work does not engage with the deeper question motivating L-012 and L-016: whether layered interventions systematically displace the locus of optimization pressure, or whether agents develop compensatory strategies that render stacked nudges mutually destructive. The experimental framing (secretary problem, synthetic choice task) is too narrow to support claims about how this scales to real protocol systems where agents have history, reputation stakes, and strategic awareness of nudging infrastructure itself.

## Research connections

- **L-012 (Intervention-Layer Displacement):** The paper observes that intervention effects reverse or cancel under combination, but does not investigate whether agents are shifting optimization target rather than merely experiencing noise. Unclear mechanism.
- **L-016 (Normative Intervention Algorithmic Retraining):** No engagement with adaptive agent response or learning-trajectory effects. Single-shot experimental design cannot detect retraining.
- **seed-023 (Informality as Novelty Sink):** No connection; the paper does not examine how agents escape formalized choice architecture through informal workarounds.
