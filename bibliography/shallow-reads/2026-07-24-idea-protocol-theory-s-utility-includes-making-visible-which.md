# Idea: Protocol theory's utility includes making visible which coordination layers bear

**Source:** Discord #Protocols as Total Cost Estimators (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-07-24
**Connected to:** CL-002
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** The claim operationalizes an existing hypothesis (CL-002) by naming a *method* (cost visibility across layer transitions) rather than proposing a new structural claim. It is methodologically useful but does not yet constitute a separable law. Storing preserves the practical framing for future protocol audits without creating conceptual duplication.

## What this is

Protocol theory's primary utility lies in making *visible* the distribution of coordination costs across layer boundaries—revealing hidden or externalized costs that simpler (non-layered) analyses cannot detect, thereby enabling total cost of ownership calculations across protocol transitions.

## What I took from it

This idea sharpens CL-002 by shifting from the abstract principle (coordination costs are conserved) to the *epistemic function* (protocol theory reveals where those costs live). The claim is not that costs are conserved—that is already CL-002's territory—but that *protocol-layered thinking is the discovery tool that makes conservation visible and measurable*.

This opens a methodological question: if protocol theory's value is primarily as a cost-accounting instrument, then its failure cases would be systems where cost distribution is already transparent (or where layer transitions don't occur). It also suggests that protocol theory is less a predictive tool and more a *forensic* or *audit* tool. This is a useful constraint on the scope of the hypothesis inventory.
