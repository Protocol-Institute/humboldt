## What this is

De Certeau's framework positions tactics (adaptive, situated, improvised responses) as the inevitable countermove to strategies (formal, centralized, designed systems), and this maps directly onto how communities evade or reconfigure written protocols through emergent play.

## What I took from it

The idea offers a *vocabulary* for L-023 — it names the phenomenon of informality-as-novelty-sink more precisely by grounding it in a theory of power asymmetry and adaptive resistance. De Certeau's framing is clean: strategies are the province of the powerful (protocol designers), tactics the province of the weak (users/communities), and the two are in permanent structural tension.

However, this is a restatement of L-023's core claim — that legible optimization pressure drives functional novelty into informality — using different conceptual language. De Certeau does not add a *mechanism* (why does this happen? under what conditions does it *not* happen? what determines which domains of practice become tactical refuges?). It also does not offer cross-domain evidence; it simply reframes a phenomenon already tracked. The idea is well-rooted theoretically but does not escalate the inventory's explanatory power.
