## What this is

The idea proposes that protocols contain detectable, systematic structural methods—specifically for *anticipating* future states and *measuring distance* (perhaps to targets, errors, or constraints)—embedded at the schema level rather than as emergent properties.

## What I took from it

This claim is genuinely generative: it suggests protocols are not merely reactive rule-sets but may encode forward-modeling and spatial/logical reasoning as *primitive components*. This opens a research direction toward protocol *intentionality*—not consciousness, but the formalization of lookahead and state-space geometry.

However, the claim is currently under-specified. It's unclear whether "anticipation" refers to: prediction, branching logic, constraint pre-checking, or temporal modeling. Similarly, "distance" could mean semantic divergence, execution steps, error magnitude, or logical separation. Without operationalizing these terms, the claim risks being too broad to falsify or test.

If substantiated, this would challenge a purely mechanistic reading of protocols and suggest they encode *epistemic structure*—methods for knowing and measuring, not just doing.
