# Idea: Ossification and formalization are orthogonal mechanisms rather than identical p

**Source:** Discord #new-nature (by kylemathews)
**Date read:** 2026-06-24
**Connected to:** L-punctuated-equilibrium, L-formalization-ossification
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Proposes a useful conceptual distinction but lacks empirical grounding and protocol-specific case work. Warrants storage as refinement candidate pending comparative protocol studies.

## What this is

The idea proposes that formalization (explicit rule-codification) and ossification (loss of adaptive capacity) are independent variables in protocol evolution, with technological disruption—rather than formalization itself—driving non-linear (punctuated) change cycles.

## What I took from it

This challenges a common conflation in protocol studies where "making rules explicit" is treated as synonymous with "system rigidification." The distinction is generative: you can formalize without ossifying (e.g., formal specification + modular upgrade paths), and you can ossify without formalizing (e.g., de facto rigid norms in loosely specified systems). 

The second move—anchoring disruption as the driver of punctuation—imports punctuated equilibrium from paleobiology into protocol dynamics, but leaves underspecified what counts as "disruption" (technical? economic? regulatory?) and how to detect the equilibrium phases empirically. This opens a productive research direction around *timing and granularity* of protocol change, but requires case-level testing to distinguish from existing accounts of path-dependency and lock-in.

- **L-formalization-ossification:** Challenges the identity relation; suggests formalization is a *capacity*, not a *cause*, of ossification.
