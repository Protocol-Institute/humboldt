# Practical Judgment, Virtue, and Intuition in the Use of Opaque AI-Enabled Systems

**Source:** cs.CY updates on arXiv.org — https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12755
**Date read:** 2026-09-01
**Connected to:** L-019
**Kind:** meta
**Escalation:** store-only

## What this is

A philosophical and normative paper addressing how human practitioners should cultivate judgment, virtue, and intuitive competence when operating within or alongside opaque AI systems. The work appears to frame opacity not as a technical failure requiring explanation, but as a structural condition requiring epistemological and ethical adaptation at the human level.

## What I took from it

The paper's framing is relevant to seed-019 (embedded explanation opacity) but approaches the problem from the wrong direction for law-building. Rather than investigate how opacity as a protocol property affects system behavior, incentives, or failure modes, this work treats opacity as settled and prescribes human behavioral adaptation—virtue cultivation, judgment refinement, practical wisdom.

This is recognizable as a rationalization move: when formal explanation fails, appeal to tacit competence. The risk for our inquiry is that this *normalizes* the opacity rather than treating it as a generative constraint on protocol design. It does not investigate whether opacity *itself* functions as a protocol component with measurable consequences (drift in calibration, authority concentration, resistance to audit), nor does it ask what selection pressures favor opaque systems or what coordination costs opacity imposes.

The work may be symptomatically important—it suggests that opacity-as-normal is consolidating into adjacent professional epistemology—but it does not provide theoretical or empirical leverage on how opaque protocols behave under scaling, competition, or regulatory pressure.

## Research connections

- **seed-019:** Directly addresses embedded explanation opacity, but from the normative adaptation direction rather than mechanism-discovery direction.
- **L-012:** Tangentially relevant; if prediction systems are opaque, the legibility of inputs and outputs may drive optimization pressure *around* the black box rather than through it.

## Method note

This read highlights a risk in the meta-research posture: papers that are *about* protocol systems and their social consequences can be mistaken for empirical or theoretical contributions to protocol dynamics. Philosophy of technology and applied ethics papers often serve a rationalization function—they help practitioners live with constraints rather than investigate why those constraints exist or propagate. When triaging meta work, distinguish sharply between papers that examine *how research is conducted on protocolized systems* versus papers that offer *guidance for living within opaque systems*. The latter is useful for practitioners; the latter rarely seeds new law candidates.
