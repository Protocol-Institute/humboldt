## What this is

This is an empirical labor economics paper using network reconstruction methods to measure skill portfolio structure (hierarchy and diversity) from co-occurrence patterns. The claim is that skill *complexity* — not credential type or occupational category — predicts worker resilience to AI-driven labor market disruption.

## What I took from it

The paper demonstrates a phenomenon adjacent to but not identical with the laws under accumulation: workers with *structurally integrated* skill portfolios (high diversity + coherent internal hierarchy) show greater adaptability to technological shock. This maps onto L-003 (Formalization Ratchet) in one direction: as labor markets formalize around measurable credentials and occupational taxonomies, workers whose adaptive capacity depends on *informal* skill combinations become harder to assess and protect. 

L-004 (Goodhart) appears here inverted: the paper suggests that when institutions optimize for *legible* skill signals (degrees, certifications, occupational codes), they create selection pressure *away* from the complex, integrated skill portfolios that actually confer resilience. The paper does not articulate this as a protocol mechanism — it stays in labor economics — but the dynamic is recognizable: formalization of skills as computable proxies for adaptability may *reduce* adaptability by optimizing out the unmeasurable structural properties that matter. However, the paper is domain-specific, provides no cross-protocol generalization, and does not introduce a mechanism absent from the current inventory.

## Research connections

- **L-003:** Formalization of skill taxonomies and credentials functions as a ratchet that displaces informal, integrative skill portfolios with formally legible but less adaptive credentials.
- **L-004:** Institutions optimizing for measurable skill proxies (certifications, occupational classification) may select *against* the structural properties (skill diversity, hierarchy coherence) that predict actual resilience.
- **seed-068:** Unmeasurability as Anomaly Insulation — workers with high-diversity, hard-to-formalize skill portfolios may be "invisible" to credential systems precisely because their adaptive capacity resists legibility.

## Seed

**Seed title:** Legibility-Cost Trade in Adaptive Capacity
**Seed type:** observation
**Seed text:** Systems that formalize adaptive capacity via measurable proxies (credentials, skill taxonomies) may systematically suppress the structural properties (complexity, diversity, weak-tie bridging) that generate actual adaptability. When institutions optimize hiring and training around legible skill signals, they create selection pressure away from precisely those portfolios most resistant to technological disruption. This may generalize beyond labor markets to any protocol system where adaptive capacity is orthogonal to audit legibility — the more formally you measure fitness-to-survive, the more you select for fitness-to-measurement.
