## What this is

A framework paper extending institutional resilience assessment methodologies to agentic AI deployment contexts. It proposes indicator-based evaluation of how companies can contribute to societal capacity to govern AI agents, positioning corporate design choices as structural levers on social resilience.

## What I took from it

The work operates primarily at the level of institutional normativity and design guidance — prescribing what companies *should* do to build resilience — rather than generating sustained empirical or theoretical claims about how protocols actually behave under adoption stress. The connection to L-001 (Protocol Ossification Under Adoption Pressure) is gestural: the paper identifies that widespread AI agent deployment creates "sociotechnical circumstances under which this technology integrates into existing structures," but does not examine the mechanism by which that integration *resists modification*, nor does it track how safety interventions become locked-in as deployment scales. Similarly, L-005 (Gall Generalization) is invoked but not tested: the paper assumes that safer AI agent implementations can be evolved incrementally, but provides no evidence about what happens when competing deployment pressures favor speed over safety-informed architecture, or when path dependency makes reversal impossible.

The framework is essentially an instrument for measuring and incentivizing corporate behavior, not an instrument for discovering laws of protocol behavior. It does not present a primary theoretical argument, empirical pattern, or generalizable mechanism.

## Research connections

- **L-001:** The paper identifies adoption-driven integration as real but treats it as a design problem rather than investigating whether ossification is inevitable regardless of corporate intent.
- **L-005:** Assumes incremental safety-informed evolution is possible; does not test whether complex deployed systems resist modification under pressure.
- **seed-035:** Mentioned in triage but not reviewed; likely connection is institutional insulation of agentic systems once deployed.
