## What this is

An econometric study exploiting quasi-experimental variation (telehealth parity laws, broadband rollout) to measure how price floors/ceilings and technological access jointly affect physician service quantity and spatial market structure in healthcare. Standard causal inference applied to a specific regulatory domain; contributes empirical material but not theoretical mechanism or generalized law.

## What I took from it

The paper documents a domain-specific interaction: price controls + technology adoption show nonlinear effects on market structure. Price floors amplify with broadband access; price ceilings converge toward floors under high connectivity. This is consistent with L-001 (ossification under adoption pressure — formalized pricing rules become harder to modify as technology locks in compliance infrastructure) and L-003 (formalization ratchet — telehealth parity laws are explicit formalization events under scaling pressure). However, the mechanism is economic/behavioral, not protocol-structural. The work shows *that* formalization and technology interact, but does not expose the underlying mechanism by which protocols harden, nor does it reveal anything about the generalized structure of this interaction that would hold beyond healthcare market structure.

## Research connections

- **L-001:** Price controls formalize as technology legitimizes and scales adoption; institutional resistance to modification increases. But this is shown through market effects, not protocol mechanism.
- **L-003:** Telehealth parity laws and broadband are explicit formalization events under adoption pressure. The paper documents the outcome, not the dynamic.
- **seed-082 (Additive Intervention in Overloaded Protocols Preserves Root Pressure):** Price floors may function as additive interventions that preserve underlying shortage/allocation pressure rather than resolve it. Relevant but speculative from this abstract.
