## What this is

A mapping paper surveying the ethical and societal dimensions of human-swarm teaming (HST) in crisis governance — drones coordinated with human operators in disaster response. The work identifies governance challenges (control, responsibility, surveillance, triage legitimacy, dual use) but does not present a sustained theoretical or empirical argument for a specific law or mechanism.

## What I took from it

The paper documents formalization pressure in safety-critical protocols (L-003 signal: crisis response pushes toward formal coordination norms) and touches on trust accumulation in operational systems (L-007 signal: HST legitimacy depends on track record, not technical capability alone). However, the piece remains largely a taxonomic exercise — it identifies tension surfaces (human oversight vs. speed, control vs. autonomy, accountability under opacity) without resolving or mechanically explaining any of them.

The domain is real and pressure-rich: crisis response is precisely where legibility becomes safety-critical, where informal coordination breaks down, and where dual-use risks concentrate. But the paper does not offer a law-shaped claim about *how* these tensions resolve or what stable equilibrium emerges. It maps territory without proposing a regularity that generalizes beyond HST.

## Research connections

- **L-003 (Formalization Ratchet):** Crisis governance under time pressure shows clear pressure toward formal protocols and legible decision rules, but the paper does not trace the mechanism or track what informal norms are displaced.
- **L-007 (Trust Ratchet in Safety-Critical Protocols):** HST legitimacy claimed to depend on operational history, but paper does not test whether trust follows age or stability rather than capability updates.
