## What this is

Technical standards for conductor sizing in electronics design locked into pragmatic compromise values through widespread adoption and manufacturing tooling, without ever being formally specified as a protocol or standard, demonstrating ossification through *de facto* rather than *de jure* adoption.

## What I took from it

This is a clean instantiation of L-001, but the idea does not reveal new conditions or exceptions that would extend or challenge the law. The electronics example shows that ossification operates equally on informally-adopted technical conventions as on explicit protocols—which is already captured in L-001's mechanism ("independent of formalization status" is implicit in the law's scope).

What *could* be interesting here: the idea hints at whether ossification rate differs between *formalized* vs. *pragmatic* adoption pathways. Do informally-adopted standards ossify faster (because they lack governance structures to manage change) or slower (because they lack formal governance to enforce stability)? The conductor example alone doesn't resolve this. This would be worth tracking if future examples start showing a pattern.

## Research connections

- **L-001:** Direct instance of adoption-driven ossification; conductor standards achieved lock-in through manufacturing adoption and path dependency, not specification authority.
- **L-003:** Inverse case worth noting—these standards remained *informal* under adoption pressure, rather than formalizing. Suggests L-003 may have boundary conditions (when does stress force formalization vs. entrench informality?).
