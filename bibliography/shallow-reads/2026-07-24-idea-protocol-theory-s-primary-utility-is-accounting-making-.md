## What this is

Protocol theory's core contribution is rendering *visible* the layered costs of coordination that holistic or black-box system models conceal, enabling distributed systems to be understood and estimated as Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) problems.

## What I took from it

This idea grounds CL-002 in concrete analytical practice: it shifts protocol theory from a descriptive framework ("protocols have layers") to a diagnostic/accounting method ("protocol analysis reveals hidden coordination costs"). The claim is that the value of protocol thinking is *clarity on where money, latency, or effort actually lives*—not in prescribing what protocols should do, but in making their real cost structure legible.

This is a useful refinement because it clarifies that protocol theory is not primarily a design tool or a predictive engine; it is an *accounting lens*. It opens a productive direction: testing whether protocol-layer disaggregation consistently outperforms flat or aggregated cost models in real systems. It also suggests that protocol theory's utility scales with system complexity and opacity—the more hidden the cost structure, the greater the epistemic gain from layering.

The idea does not contradict the current inventory; rather, it proposes the *mechanism* by which CL-002 operates.
