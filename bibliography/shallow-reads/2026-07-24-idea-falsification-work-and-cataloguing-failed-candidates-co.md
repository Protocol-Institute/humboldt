# Idea: Falsification work and cataloguing failed candidates constitutes the load-bearing research that distinguishes a law from an anecdote

**Source:** Discord #Protocols as Total Cost Estimators (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-07-24
**Connected to:** CL-001, CL-002, CL-003
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Epistemological metaclaim about validation standards. Belongs in inventory as methodological constraint on all three active candidate laws, not as independent hypothesis. Does not require M-003 deep read; triage note already flags duplication with [6], [9], [12].

## What this is

The idea claims that systematic documentation of *failed* cases and boundary conditions is the epistemic criterion separating empirical laws from post-hoc storytelling—not the accumulation of supporting cases.

## What I took from it

This is a useful *inversion* of validation logic. Rather than asking "what cases confirm CL-001/002/003?", it demands we ask: "what would falsify each? Have we catalogued those attempts?" This reframes the three candidate laws as incomplete until their *failure modes* are explicitly mapped.

The idea does not propose new content; it argues for a validation *standard* that should apply retroactively to all three. It clarifies that CL-001, CL-002, and CL-003 are currently supported by positive cases but lack the negative case inventory that would elevate them from interesting patterns to defensible laws. This is a legitimate gap—one that [6], [9], [12] likely identified but this formulation sharpens as a procedural requirement.

It opens: a systematic falsification audit of each candidate law. It challenges: treating agreement/confirmation as sufficient evidence of lawfulness.
