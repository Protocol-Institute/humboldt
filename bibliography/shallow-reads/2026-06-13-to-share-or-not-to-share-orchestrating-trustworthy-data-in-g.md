## What this is

A RegTech architecture paper applying the IDSA framework to semiconductor supply chain compliance, addressing tensions between EU regulatory transparency (CBAM) and corporate data sovereignty. Domain-specific case study in protocol design for cross-border environmental reporting.

## What I took from it

The paper documents a real implementation problem: mandatory disclosure protocols (CBAM) and voluntary disclosure protocols (SBTi) create conflicting data-sharing incentives within a single value chain. The proposed solution—a trusted intermediary architecture using IDSA—is an engineering response to what appears to be a *protocol stack collision*: two governance layers operating on the same actors with incompatible transparency requirements.

However, the work remains implementation-focused rather than theoretical. It does not explain *why* such collisions occur systematically in protocolized systems, nor does it generalize the mechanism beyond environmental compliance. The IDSA framework itself is presented as already established; this is an application, not a foundational analysis. The "additive" concept mentioned in the abstract cutoff suggests a distinction between mandatory and voluntary overlays, but the abstract fragment does not clarify whether this is a novel theoretical contribution or standard regulatory design vocabulary.
