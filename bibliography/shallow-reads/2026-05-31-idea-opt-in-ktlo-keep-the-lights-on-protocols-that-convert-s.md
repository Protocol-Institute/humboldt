# Idea: Opt-in KTLO (Keep The Lights On) protocols that convert surplus energy into maintenance

**Source:** Discord #new-nature (by 4umd)
**Date read:** 2026-05-31
**Connected to:** L-001
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Proposes a design pattern rather than a law; challenges an existing law through counter-example but does not yet articulate a generalizable mechanism for *why* the pattern works or under what conditions it fails.

## What this is

A protocol design pattern in which participants voluntarily contribute surplus resources (energy, capacity, capital) to maintenance operations, avoiding the adoption-pressure deformation described in L-001 by decoupling system stability from growth-driven metric capture.

## What I took from it

The idea gestures toward a real tension in L-001: ossification is described as pressure-independent ("independent of the quality of proposed changes"), but the mechanism may actually depend on *who wants to change the system and why*. An opt-in KTLO model suggests that if maintenance funding is decoupled from adoption metrics—if the protocol does not need to grow to survive—then the typical drivers of ossification (defending against change to protect adoption numbers, investor expectations, or installed-base lock-in) lose force.

However, this is more a *design choice* that might reduce ossification *pressure* than a refutation of L-001 itself. The idea doesn't yet explain whether opt-in KTLO protocols still face the underlying constraint: that once a protocol is widely deployed and trusted, stakeholders develop asymmetric interests in stability that resist modification regardless of funding model. It opens a useful question—does the *source* of survival pressure matter?—but doesn't yet provide a law-level answer.

## Research connections

- **L-001:** Directly engages; proposes that surplus-funded, adoption-decoupled maintenance may avoid ossification, but mechanism remains unclear.
- **L-003:** Tangential; opt-in KTLO may reduce formalization pressure if the protocol doesn't need explicit coordination rules to survive, but this is speculative.
- **H-001:** Potentially relevant; does opt-in KTLO represent a cost shift rather than a cost reduction across protocol layers?

## Candidate laws or signals

**CL-4umd-001:** Protocols funded by voluntary surplus contribution rather than adoption-dependent capture may exhibit lower ossification under stable-state conditions—*but requires: (a) empirical cases; (b) clarity on whether consensus/safety requirements still enforce de facto stability; (c) test of whether "voluntary" funding itself becomes a new coordination bottleneck.*

**Recommendation:** Store as pattern candidate. Revisit if concrete protocol implementations of opt-in KTLO emerge, or if H-001 (coordination cost conservation) yields data on whether funding shifts simply redistribute rather than eliminate the pressure to resist change.
