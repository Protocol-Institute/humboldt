A conceptual/definitional paper mapping systemic risk framings in AI to emergence, collective action, and externalities. ## What I took from it

The paper appears to be primarily taxonomic—organizing existing risk framings rather than deriving new laws. The connection to L-009 (Catastrophic Risk Cancellation in Symmetric Racing Protocols) and L-003 (Formalization Ratchet) is suggestive but underdeveloped in the abstract. The framing of systemic risk through *collective action problems* and *externalities* is competent but well-established in economics and coordination theory. The application to AI integration risks does not appear to introduce a generative mechanism for how AI protocols specifically amplify or transform these classical coordination failures—it applies existing frameworks to a new domain.

The triage note hints at "coordination cost and formalization dynamics in multi-agent AI integration," but without seeing the full paper, the abstract does not establish whether this is novel theoretical work or a case study applying existing coordination theory to contemporary AI deployment patterns.
