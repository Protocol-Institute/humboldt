## What I took from it

The relevance annotation's phrase "models and artefacts coordinate multi-actor behavior" suggests this work operationalizes a key hypothesis: that in protocolized systems, coordination is not achieved *by* agents but *through* persistent, shared artefactual structures. This directly grounds stigmergy (originally Grassé's chemical trails, now generalized) as a law governing how artificial and computational collectives maintain order without central control.

The submission source and Bologna institutional affiliation indicate this is part of a sustained research program (E4MAS workshops, artifact-oriented computing) that has been refining these concepts since the early 2000s. The annotation's specificity ("models and artefacts") suggests the paper makes explicit what remains implicit in much multi-agent literature: that the *representational layer* (shared models, data structures, protocol artefacts) is the primary locus of coordination, not the logic of individual agents.
