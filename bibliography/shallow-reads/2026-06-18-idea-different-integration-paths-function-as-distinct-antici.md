# Idea: Different integration paths function as distinct anticipatory stances, where selecting a projection over a system's state-space is analogous to selecting a possible future

## What this is

Integration path selection is not merely a collapse of degrees of freedom, but an act of anticipatory commitment—choosing which possible futures a system will "attend to" through its projection onto a reduced state-space.

## What I took from it

This idea reframes the mechanics of integration hierarchies from a passive filtering model (constraints → compression) to an active predictive model (selection → anticipation). It suggests that the ontological ordering between integration levels is not just about information loss but about *which futures are made available to interact with*. This is a useful pivot: rather than asking "which variables are retained?", it asks "which possible trajectories does this integration path permit or foreclose?"

The idea also hints at a metric—distance between integration paths as distance between anticipatory frames—which could ground comparisons across heterogeneous systems (biological, computational, organizational). However, the claim remains phenomenological rather than mechanistic. It identifies a pattern (selection = anticipation) without specifying how that anticipatory stance is *enacted* or *evaluated* in protocolized systems, where futures are often explicitly modeled or constrained.
