# Idea: Electrification (Devine) and steam power (Hunter & Atack) transitions involved AI/mechanization changing internal work organization

**Source:** Discord #new-nature (by rafa_0x)  
**Date read:** 2026-05-29  
**Connected to:** L-005  
**Escalation:** store-only  
**Escalation rationale:** This idea provides historical grounding for an existing law but does not reveal new dynamics of resistance or restructuring. It strengthens confidence in L-005's generalizability across technological regimes but does not isolate a novel mechanism or boundary condition.

## What this is

Historical technology transitions (steam, electrification) show that mechanization forces organizational restructuring (vertical-to-horizontal work layout, geographic/scale expansion) precisely *because* incumbent protocols resist adoption of the new technology—suggesting that resistance itself may be the driver of organizational innovation.

## What I took from it

This idea treats organizational resistance not as friction to overcome but as a causal engine: working systems resist restructuring (L-005), but sufficiently powerful exogenous technologies force the issue by making the old organizational form incompatible with the new capability. The idea enriches L-005 by implying that *resistance is a selection pressure*, not merely inertia.

However, the idea stops short of isolating *why* new technologies succeed in forcing restructuring when L-005 predicts they shouldn't. It provides historical analogy rather than mechanism. The claim also risks conflating two dynamics: (1) technologies that make old protocols *technically infeasible*, and (2) technologies that create *economic incentives* strong enough to overcome adoption pressure. These may be distinct.

## Research connections

- **L-005:** Historical parallel confirming that complex working systems do resist restructuring even under technological pressure; suggests the generalization holds across electromechanical and digital regimes.
- **L-003:** Implies inverse relationship—as informal work organization ossifies, new technologies may force formalization by removing the option to maintain old norms.
- **H-001:** Raises question: does mechanization-driven restructuring conserve coordination cost, or does it simply shift who bears it?

## Candidate laws or signals

**None.** The idea is a historical illustration of L-005 rather than a new pattern. To become a candidate law, it would need to identify the *mechanism* by which exogenous technological capability overcomes organizational resistance—e.g., a threshold of capability-mismatch that triggers forced restructuring, or a cost model showing when restructuring becomes cheaper than retrofitting old forms.
