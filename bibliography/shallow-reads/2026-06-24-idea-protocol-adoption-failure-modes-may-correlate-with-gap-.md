# Idea: Protocol adoption failure modes may correlate with gap between technology expectations and actual capabilities

**Source:** Discord #(Protocol) adoption failure (by ncc1031)  
**Date read:** 2026-06-24  
**Connected to:** none  
**Escalation:** store-only  
**Escalation rationale:** Identifies a plausible failure mechanism within adoption contexts but lacks specificity on measurement, scope, and causal directionality. Warrants hypothesis development before law candidacy, but core insight is sound enough to preserve and refine through future observations.

## What this is

The claim proposes that protocol adoption failures correlate with a gap between stakeholder expectations of technology capability and its actual performance—suggesting that inflated expectations function as a failure driver rather than mere symptom.

## What I took from it

This idea moves beyond describing *what* fails (adoption rates drop) to proposing a *why* rooted in expectation management. It assumes that adoption decisions are partially contingent on perceived value relative to real value, and that misalignment produces friction, resistance, or abandonment.

The insight is generative: it suggests adoption failure is not purely technical or political, but perceptual—rooted in the narrative and marketing frames around a protocol *before* implementation. This opens investigation into:
- How expectations are set (vendor claims, peer influence, regulatory framing, conference discourse)
- Timing of expectation-reality collision (at deployment, scaling, compliance check)
- Recoverable vs. terminal misalignment (can trust be rebuilt, or is adoption damage permanent?)

It does *not* contradict current inventory (none yet exists), but it does suggest adoption study should systematically track expectation metrics alongside adoption metrics—currently a gap.
