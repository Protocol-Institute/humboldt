## What this is

Empirical labor economics study analyzing ~30M job postings (2018–2024, US/UK/Australia) to measure how AI adoption affects demand for complementary human skills (analytical thinking, resilience, ethical judgment). Distinguishes internal effects (within AI roles) from spillover effects (beyond AI roles).

## What I took from it

This work is oriented toward human labor market dynamics rather than the formal properties of artificial systems themselves. It provides useful empirical grounding for *demand-side* complementarity claims, but operates at the level of skill taxonomy and job posting signals—not the computational or architectural laws governing how AI systems interact with human agents or institutions.

The finding that AI adoption increases demand for non-technical human skills is economically important but does not immediately illuminate how protocolized systems scale, fail, or reorganize under constraint. The work sits in human capital theory, not in the study of artificial system behavior.
