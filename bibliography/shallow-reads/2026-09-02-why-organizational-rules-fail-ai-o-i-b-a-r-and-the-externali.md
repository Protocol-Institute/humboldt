# Why Organizational Rules Fail AI: O-I-B-A-R and the Externalization of Decision Boundaries

## What this is

A sociotechnical analysis of why AI systems deployed under formal organizational rules (policies, playbooks, prompts) fail in practice. The argument centers on a knowledge representation asymmetry: the AI receives the procedure as stated, while human operators navigate the procedure *plus* a corpus of negative boundaries and contextual know-how that remain implicit and unformalized.

## What I took from it

The paper identifies a real failure mode — the gap between procedure-as-written and procedure-in-practice — but frames it primarily as a knowledge representation problem rather than as a protocol-level mechanism. The O-I-B-A-R framework (Observed, Informal, Boundary, Action, Rule) appears to be a descriptive taxonomy of this gap, not a predictive law.

The work is competent and addresses a genuine pain point in AI deployment. However, it does not sustain a theoretical argument about *why* this gap is structurally inevitable under formalization pressure, or under what conditions it generalizes beyond the human-AI organizational interface. It reads as a case study with applied value, not as a foundation for induction into a broader regularity.

The connection to L-012 (Intervention-Layer Displacement) and L-015 (Interpretive Continuity Decay) is intuitive but underdeveloped. The paper does not demonstrate that the mechanism here is the same as in other domains (e.g., governance protocols, computable legality, distributed audit systems) — it remains localized to the AI-organizational rules boundary.
