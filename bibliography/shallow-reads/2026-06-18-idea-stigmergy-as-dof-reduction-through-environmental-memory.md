## What this is

Stigmergy operates as a degrees-of-freedom reduction mechanism by substituting direct agent-to-agent signaling with protocol-mediated environmental traces, where action triggers emerge from accumulated integrated signal (∫signal dt > θ) rather than explicit coordination.

## What I took from it

This idea sharpens the operational model of how stigmergy achieves coordination without central control—a key mechanism in protocolized systems. Rather than treating stigmergy as a separate phenomenon, it frames it as *instantiation* of L-DOF-reduction: the environment (medium/protocol) becomes the substrate for memory, and thresholds become the constraint mechanism. 

The integral formulation (∫signal dt > θ) is a useful formal anchor, suggesting that stigmergic triggers depend on *accumulated* rather than instantaneous signal—this distinguishes it from reactive threshold mechanisms and implies temporal integration as a built-in DOF constraint. It opens a question: does protocol design determine both the integration window and the threshold, making stigmergy a *designed* property rather than emergent? This connects to questions about protocol-as-ontology.
