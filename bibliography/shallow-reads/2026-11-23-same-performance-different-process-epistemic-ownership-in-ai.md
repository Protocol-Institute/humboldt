# Same Performance, Different Process: Epistemic Ownership in AI-Mediated Education

## What this is

An empirical study of 150 student-AI conversations examining how generative AI decouples observable cognitive process from assessed performance in educational contexts. Uses "epistemic ownership" (Direction, Integration, Evaluation) as an interpretive frame to show that identical performance metrics can mask substantially different underlying cognitive traces.

## What I took from it

This is a domain-specific instantiation of L-012 (Intervention-Layer Displacement) and L-023 (Informality as Novelty Sink), but does not generalize the mechanism itself or introduce evidence that settles either law's conditions. The paper documents a symptom: when performance becomes the legible, assessable signal in an AI-mediated system, the locus of optimization pressure shifts away from the cognitive processes that performance was originally meant to reflect. Students optimize for assessed output, not for the epistemic work the assessment was designed to capture.

However, the paper does not investigate why this happens *structurally* (what about formalized assessment creates this displacement?), nor does it establish whether this is specific to education or a general property of systems where intermediate process becomes invisible while final output remains legible. It observes the phenomenon without exploring the boundary conditions or the mechanism driving reoptimization.

## Research connections

- **L-012:** Legible performance metrics (assessed output) become the optimization target; the original epistemic process becomes invisible and thus loses optimization pressure. Confirms the displacement pattern but does not advance mechanism understanding.
- **L-023:** When educational assessment formalizes performance as a computable signal, the informal cognitive work and exploratory novelty that assessment was meant to incentivize migrate underground or disappear entirely.
- **seed-545:** Functionality Opacity Stratification — students demonstrate competent performance while the cognitive process that generates it becomes opaque to both assessor and potentially to the student themselves.

## Seed

**Seed title:** Process-Legibility Collapse Under Output Formalization
**Seed type:** observation
**Seed text:** When a protocol system formalizes and measures an output signal (e.g., assessed performance), the intermediate processes that produced that output become invisible to the system's feedback loops. Optimization pressure then concentrates on the output itself rather than the process; agents learn to decouple process quality from output legibility. In educational AI, this creates a stable state where two students with identical assessed performance have executed radically different cognitive processes — one genuine, one surface-compliant. This may generalize to any system where intermediate work is expensive to audit and final output is cheap to measure.
