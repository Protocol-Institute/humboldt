## What this is

An economic model of tax compliance grounding fiscal capacity in moral reasoning rather than enforcement. Citizens partially internalize concealment consequences via universalization (imagining world-states where everyone behaves as they do), creating a self-reinforcing link between perceived public goods effectiveness and voluntary compliance. The elite chooses between public spending and private rents, taking compliance as endogenous to their allocation choices.

## What I took from it

The paper instantiates L-003 (Formalization Ratchet) in reverse: rather than showing how informal norms formalize under stress, it shows how *moral reasoning* (universalization) can substitute for formalization when it becomes a legible coordination substrate. This is interesting but operates at the individual psychology level, not the protocol system level where we're tracking ossification and hardness asymmetries.

It also touches L-006 (Coordination Cost Conservation) obliquely — the model suggests that shifting from enforcement-based to norm-based compliance redistributes the *visibility* of coordination cost (from policing/audit to moral inference), but doesn't show that total cost is conserved, only that it moves. The paper doesn't track what happens when universalization reasoning becomes the bottleneck or fails at scale.

The mechanism is genuinely about protocol *belief formation* as a compliance input, which sits at the edge of L-008 (Proxy Optimization Under Computable Enforcement), but the universalization framework is too abstract and psychology-embedded to generate cross-domain protocol laws. It's a domain-specific model, not a mechanism hint.

## Research connections

- **L-003:** Formalization Ratchet operates here in reverse — moral reasoning *prevents* need for formalization by internalizing collective consequences. But this is a stability condition in low-scale settings; no evidence it generalizes to protocol systems at adoption pressure.
- **L-006:** Coordination cost may be displaced from enforcement to moral reasoning, but paper doesn't measure total cost conservation.
- **seed-068:** Unmeasurability as Anomaly Insulation — the universalization reasoning depends on *perceived* public goods effectiveness, which is inherently unmeasurable and plastic; this could be a protective feature, but paper doesn't explore degradation under metric pressure.
