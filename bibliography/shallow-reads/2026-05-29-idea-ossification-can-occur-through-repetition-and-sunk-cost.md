# Idea: Ossification can occur through repetition and sunk costs without adoption pressure

**Source:** Discord #new-nature (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-05-29
**Connected to:** L-001
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Mechanistic refinement of an established law; does not yet warrant deep investigation but should condition future L-001 empirical work.

## What this is

Protocols can become resistant to modification through accumulated operational inertia, switching costs, and institutional dependency—independent of whether they were widely adopted or whether adoption pressure was the proximate cause of that resistance.

## What I took from it

L-001 proposes adoption pressure as the driver of protocol ossification. This idea suggests that ossification may be overdetermined: even protocols with modest adoption can become locked-in if their operational footprint creates switching costs, procedural habituation, or sunk training/infrastructure investment. This would mean L-001 identifies *one sufficient condition* but not the only one.

The idea is valuable because it decouples ossification from adoption velocity—suggesting that slow, deep entrenchment (through repetition and operational dependency) may be as powerful as rapid scaling. It also connects to economic and organizational lock-in literature (Arthur, David) that may have been underweighted in L-001's formulation. The challenge is real: a protocol used by 100 organizations for 10 years may be harder to modify than one used by 10,000 for 6 months, depending on switching costs.

However, this may be a *refinement* rather than a contradiction. L-001 could be broadened to "Adoption Pressure and Operational Entrenchment" without invalidating the core claim.

## Research connections

- **L-001:** Directly proposes an alternative or complementary mechanism for ossification; suggests L-001's stated driver (adoption pressure) may be insufficient or not primary in some domains.
- **L-005:** Related: Working Systems Resist Restructuring likely captures some of the same dynamic (sunk operational correctness), but L-005 is about *functional dependency* whereas this idea emphasizes *switching cost and habituation*.
- **H-001:** If coordination cost is conserved, then ossification through sunk costs may simply displace modification friction rather than create it—worth testing jointly.
