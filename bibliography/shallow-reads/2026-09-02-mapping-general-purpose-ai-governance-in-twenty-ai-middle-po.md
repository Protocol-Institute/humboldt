## What this is

A descriptive mapping study surveying GPAI governance provisions across 20 non-frontier jurisdictions, charting what rules exist across four accountability domains. This is a horizontal cross-sectional inventory of enacted provisions, not a theoretical or mechanistic argument about why those provisions emerge or how they fail.

## What I took from it

The paper documents the *outcome* of L-003 (Formalization Ratchet) in action: jurisdictions without frontier AI capacity are formalizing governance at scale under distributed pressure. However, the paper itself does not investigate *mechanism* — it does not explain why provisions converge or diverge, what coordination failures drive heterogeneity, or how formalization pressures interact with adoption cycles. It is a landscape snapshot, not a causal inquiry.

The work confirms that formalization is occurring (L-003 signal), but does not advance the mechanistic understanding of when formalization *hardens into ossification* (L-001), or what role asymmetric deployment power plays in locking governance shape. The paper notes that GPAI risk lands globally but builders are concentrated in two jurisdictions — this is the *structural condition* that should generate ossification pressure, but the paper does not track or measure it.

## Research connections

- **L-001:** Confirms adoption-pressure conditions exist (distributed risk, concentrated building). Does not measure ossification or track provision stability over time.
- **L-003:** Documents formalization across governance domains under scaling/coordination stress. Does not explain mechanism of norm replacement or causal drivers.
- **L-015:** Could relate to governance record survival vs. institutional continuity decay, but paper does not address interpretive drift or audit-trace abandonment.
- **seed-062:** Relates to formalization opacity — does mapping provisions reveal what is actually *legible* to automation and enforcement, or only what is *written*?

## Seed

**Seed title:** Governance Legibility Asymmetry Under Asymmetric Deployment

**Seed type:** question

**Seed text:** When AI capability deployment is concentrated geographically but governance is distributed across jurisdictions, the legibility and enforceability of governance rules may become asymmetrically coupled to builder location rather than jurisdiction of rule origin. Jurisdictions without frontier builders may formalize rules that are unobservable and unenforceable against the entities they target, while simultaneously creating legible compliance signals for actors *outside* the primary capability chain. This may create a ratchet where formalization increases (observable compliance burden) while effectiveness decreases (unobserved evasion by primary actors). Worth tracking whether provision heterogeneity correlates with builder proximity or whether formalization is itself the coordination goal regardless of enforcement.
