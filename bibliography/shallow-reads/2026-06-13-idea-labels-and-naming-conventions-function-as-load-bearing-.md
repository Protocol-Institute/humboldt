## What this is

The claim that naming and labeling systems operate as functional infrastructure components—not merely symbolic—and that their design choices materially shape coordination, trust allocation, and institutional outcomes.

## What I took from it

This idea restates the core load-bearing function already captured in H-004, emphasizing that nomenclatural choices are *active protocol decisions* rather than post-hoc descriptive acts. The framing ("decorative elements" vs. infrastructure) is rhetorically clarifying but does not introduce new mechanistic insight: we already model naming systems as having epistemic weight and institutional consequences.

The idea's value lies in its potential to flag *when* nomenclature becomes a strategic research target—i.e., when naming conflicts, standardization failures, or legitimacy cascades become visible. However, without a specific case, empirical anchor, or mechanistic prediction about *how* naming shapes outcomes under different protocol conditions, it remains a restatement of existing awareness. The phrase "materially affect coordination outcomes" is true but not yet operationalized in this form.
