# Mathematical Modelling of Ethical AI Use in Higher Education: A Coordination Game Framework for Future-Facing Learning

**Source:** cs.MA updates on arXiv.org — https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27400
**Date read:** 2026-05-29
**Connected to:** L-003, H-001
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** 

## What this is

A game-theoretic model applying coordination game theory to analyze how norms around AI use (responsible vs. opportunistic) emerge and stabilize within student cohorts in higher education assessment contexts. The work frames institutional AI governance policy as an intervention in peer-expectation-driven coordination dynamics rather than as direct behavioral enforcement.

## What I took from it

The paper appears to formalize one mechanism consistent with L-003 (formalization ratchet under stress): institutional responses to AI proliferation are moving from informal peer norms toward explicit policy structures. However, the coordination game framing introduces a potentially important wrinkle—it suggests that *formal policy alone may not stabilize desired equilibria* if peer-level coordination incentives remain misaligned. This hints at a distinction between formalization *as prescription* (top-down) versus formalization *as solution to coordination failure* (bottom-up).

On H-001 (coordination cost conservation), the work is suggestive but underdeveloped: if formal AI governance policies succeed, do they simply displace coordination costs from students to institutions, or genuinely reduce system coordination overhead? The paper does not appear to track costs across layers.

The triage note's intuition is sound—this is observing L-003 in action—but the contribution feels exploratory rather than mechanistic. It documents the phenomenon of norm formalization under pressure without isolating what makes some formalizations stable and others fragile.

## Research connections

- **L-003:** Confirms that scaling pressure (rapid AI adoption) triggers formalization of informal norms; adds that peer-coordination incentives complicate adoption of formal rules.
- **H-001:** Raises but does not resolve whether institutional policy shifts coordination burden or reduces it globally.

## Candidate laws or signals

**CL-CS.MA-001:** Formalized protocols imposed against misaligned peer incentives tend to bifurcate into surface compliance and shadow norms, unless the formalization also restructures the payoff structure that drives peer coordination.
