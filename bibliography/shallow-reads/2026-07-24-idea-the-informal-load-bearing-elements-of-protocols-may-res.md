# Idea: The informal load-bearing elements of protocols may resist full mathematical formalization; Fermi-style back-of-envelope accounting may better capture protocol dynamics than formal mathematical language

**Date read:** 2026-07-24
**Connected to:** CL-001
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** This is a methodological refinement that strengthens the formalization tension already captured in CL-001, but does not yet constitute a separable law. The claim about *which tools work better* is subsidiary to the core claim about formalization limits. Promotes to candidate hypothesis only if empirical tests of Fermi vs. formal accuracy emerge.

## What this is

The claim that protocol function depends on elements resistant to formal mathematical capture, and that approximate/heuristic accounting methods may better predict actual behavior than rigorous formalism.

## What I took from it

This idea doesn't contradict CL-001; it *specifies* what happens at the formalization boundary. CL-001 posits that protocols ratchet toward formalization and that this generates new opacity. This idea suggests that some load-bearing informal elements—tacit coordination, heuristic thresholds, implicit consensus—may *structurally resist* formalization, and that researchers may capture protocol dynamics more accurately by accepting and instrumentalizing this resistance (via Fermi estimation, order-of-magnitude reasoning, scenario accounting) rather than chasing completeness.

This opens a methodological question: *Are there protocol components for which formal models are *worse* predictors than heuristic ones?* If so, CL-001 needs refinement—formalization ratchets not toward total capture, but toward a stable asymptote of formal + irreducible-informal systems. This also challenges the assumption that "more formalization" = "more understanding."
