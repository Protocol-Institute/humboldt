## What this is

A position/agenda paper arguing that automated multi-agent resource allocation systems (highway lanes, energy grids, attention markets) currently embed welfare assumptions via industry convention rather than explicit design. The work advocates for "welfarist control design"—a framework to make societal welfare objectives legible and deliberate in protocol engineering.

## What I took from it

The paper identifies a genuine lacuna: that protocolized systems managing scarce resources have *implicit* welfare functions but lack formal methods to surface, audit, or modify them. This is less a technical contribution than a framing problem—it highlights that automation doesn't eliminate normative choice, it obscures it.

This is relevant to our inventory insofar as it suggests a structural vulnerability in multi-agent control systems: **design by convention is indistinguishable from unexamined bias at scale**. However, the paper appears to be programmatic (defining a research direction) rather than presenting sustained theoretical or empirical support for a specific mechanism or law. The core observation—that allocation protocols embed hidden welfare assumptions—is sound but not novel; the solution space (how to *design* welfarist systems) remains open.
