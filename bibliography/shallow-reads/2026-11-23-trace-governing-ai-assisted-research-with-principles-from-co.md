## What this is

A meta-governance paper proposing formal coordination protocols for research teams using AI assistants, drawing analogies from software engineering and security practices. The core argument is that AI-assisted research requires active organizational structure — not new principles, but application of established team-coordination patterns to a novel capability context.

## What I took from it

The paper frames AI capability adoption as a *coordination problem*, not a capability problem — a classic signal that L-003 (Formalization Ratchet) is operating. The move from informal research norms ("how we've always done papers") to formalized protocols ("TRACE governance rules") under adoption pressure is exactly the mechanism L-003 targets. 

However, the paper does not appear to investigate the *cost* side of that formalization, nor does it model what gets lost or displaced when informal coordination structures are replaced with legible, enforceable rules. It treats formalization as solution rather than tradeoff. This suggests the paper is *applying* a governance pattern rather than studying the deeper dynamics of why formalization creates new failure modes in research contexts. The analogy to software engineering may also obscure domain-specific differences: research coordination involves novelty-generation and contestation in ways that differ from maintaining deployed systems.

## Method note

This paper exemplifies a common meta-research pattern: identifying an institutional problem (how do teams coordinate under new tool adoption) and reaching for domain-analogues (software engineering best practices) without investigating whether the analogy preserves the dynamics that made those practices work. For the new nature research agenda, this suggests that meta-governance papers should be evaluated not on whether they propose *better* formalization, but on whether they measure what formalization *displaces* — attention, flexibility, contestation, interpretive diversity — and whether the tradeoff is favorable in the specific domain. Store for reference in behavior-pattern analysis, but do not seed further law induction without evidence that the paper models failure modes of its own proposed protocols.
