# Idea: Models or frameworks can function as stigmergy for groups of humans, where discrete steps or artifacts within the model coordinate group behavior through environmental modification rather than direct communication.

**Source:** Discord #Cognitive stigmergy (by ncc1031)  
**Date read:** 2026-06-18  
**Connected to:** none  
**Escalation:** store-only  
**Escalation rationale:** Proposes a generative linking mechanism (stigmergy → formal models) but lacks concrete instantiation or boundary conditions. Warrants observation and collection of empirical cases before hypothesis promotion.

## What this is

The claim that structured models or frameworks can serve as coordination mechanisms for human groups by encoding behavioral instructions into artifacts that modify the informational/social environment, rather than requiring direct synchronous communication.

## What I took from it

This idea bridges two separable domains: stigmergy (traditionally a biological/swarm concept describing coordination via environmental traces) and epistemic/procedural artifacts (models, frameworks, protocols). The proposal is non-trivial because it suggests models don't merely *represent* coordination—they *perform* it through their structure.

The idea opens a direction: instead of asking "how do humans coordinate?" we ask "how do formal structures encode and distribute coordination signals?" This is generative for studying protocolized systems. However, the claim is currently at the level of analogy. It needs grounding in specific cases—e.g., how does a project management framework (Kanban board, sprint structure) function differently than pheromone trails, and where does the stigmergic mechanism actually live (the artifact, the interpretation rules, the feedback loop)?

This does *not* directly contradict any established law, but it also doesn't yet explain mechanisms. It's a promising direction, not yet a testable hypothesis.
