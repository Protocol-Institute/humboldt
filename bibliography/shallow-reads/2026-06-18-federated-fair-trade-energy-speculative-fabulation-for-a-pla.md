## What this is

A speculative/design paper examining co-evolution of decentralized renewable energy grids and computational infrastructure under conditions of local governance and resource scarcity. The work treats electricity and computation as tightly coupled systems rather than separate layers, and explores what happens to "permacomputing" assumptions when energy becomes locally distributed and politically contested.

## What I took from it

The paper identifies a genuine systems coupling: computational demand profiles and renewable energy availability operate on mismatched temporal/spatial scales, creating mutual constraints. This pushes against siloed infrastructure thinking. However, the work appears primarily **speculative and normative** (exploring what *should* happen under fair-trade energy models) rather than presenting empirical laws or sustained theoretical grounding for how such coupled systems actually behave under constraint.

The relevant signal is the **infrastructure interdependency problem**: when you cannot decouple computation from energy governance, optimization strategies that work for each system independently become unstable. But the paper seems to address this through design fiction and governance proposals rather than mechanistic analysis. Without seeing the full text, it's unclear whether a sustained causal argument or generalizable pattern emerges.
