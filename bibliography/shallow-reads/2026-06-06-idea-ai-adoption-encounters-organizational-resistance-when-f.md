# Idea: AI adoption encounters organizational resistance when framed as job displacement

**Source:** Discord #Protocols for Business [01-06-26] -- Summer 2026 Direction Discussion (by sachbenny)
**Date read:** 2026-06-06
**Connected to:** H-002
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Idea contains a concrete behavioral mechanism (work-to-rule sabotage) that refines H-002 but does not yet constitute a freestanding law. Warrants formalization as candidate hypothesis once additional cases are collected.

## What this is

When AI systems are introduced with a narrative of labor displacement, organizational actors may deliberately degrade output quality or adopt defensive work practices to demonstrate AI ineffectiveness, thereby undermining trust in the system independent of its actual technical performance.

## What I took from it

This idea sharpens H-002 by proposing a *causal mechanism* linking social framing to technical trust erosion. Rather than simply claiming that "trust accumulates through stability and age," sachbenny identifies an active social response—deliberate low-quality output as a form of resistance—that *manufactures* instability and apparent technical failure. This is not passive distrust; it is instrumental sabotage disguised as legitimate feedback.

The idea opens a productive tension: technical correctness becomes decoupled from perceived correctness when adoption narratives activate identity or livelihood concerns. This suggests trust in protocolized systems is not purely a function of their design maturity, but also of the *social context in which they are deployed*. It also implies that adoption failure might be misread as technical failure when it is actually social rejection performing as technical critique.

## Candidate laws or signals

**CL-sachbenny-001:** *When a protocolized system is introduced into an organization with displacement framing, actors have incentive to produce outputs that demonstrate system failure, creating a self-fulfilling prophecy of untrustworthiness regardless of underlying technical capability.*
