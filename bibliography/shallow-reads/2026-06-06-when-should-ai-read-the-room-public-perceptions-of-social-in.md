# When Should AI Read the Room? Public Perceptions of Social Intelligence in AI Agents

## What this is

Empirical survey study (N=200, mixed methods) examining how lay publics perceive and evaluate social intelligence in AI agents. The work is descriptive of user attitudes toward socially-capable systems in deployment contexts, not a theoretical or mechanistic investigation of how social intelligence functions as a protocolized system property.

## What I took from it

This appears to be a perception/acceptability study rather than a work probing the laws governing how AI systems actually perform social reasoning or how social protocols stabilize in human-AI interaction. The framing around "should AI read the room" suggests normative questions about design choice rather than causal or structural claims about social intelligence itself.

The research may document friction points between deployed social-AI capabilities and public expectations—useful for design iteration—but does not appear to present a primary theoretical argument about *how* social intelligence operates as a system property, *what mechanisms* enable or constrain it, or *how* those mechanisms generalize across contexts. It reads as a requirements elicitation or acceptance study, not a candidate for the laws-of-artificial-systems inventory.
