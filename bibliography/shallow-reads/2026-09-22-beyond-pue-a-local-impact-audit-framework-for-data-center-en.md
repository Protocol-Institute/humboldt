## What this is

A policy-motivated empirical paper documenting how standard data center sustainability metrics (PUE, WUE, CUE) fail to represent local resource scarcity and social footprint, and proposing a local-impact audit framework as a corrective. The work is positioned at the intersection of sustainability accounting, protocol design, and political economy of infrastructure.

## What I took from it

This is a high-fidelity case study of L-004 (Goodhart Generalization: Metric Capture) in a domain where the protocol has already achieved near-universal adoption and the optimization pressure has become visible at the political level. The paper shows that when a measurable proxy (efficiency ratio normalized to IT load) becomes the legible enforcement target, optimizing agents achieve target compliance while displacing environmental cost to unmeasured dimensions (local water stress, grid strain, community displacement). Critically, the $130B in project delays in Q1 2026 suggests that the **feedback loop is beginning to close** — protocol systems are detecting the mismatch and attempting to evolve the metric itself rather than abandon the protocol. This is evidence for L-005 (Gall Generalization) operating in real time: the existing protocol cannot be replaced, so the proxy is being formally extended. The paper also touches on L-006 (Coordination Cost Conservation) — the new audit framework does not reduce coordination cost, it relocates it from operational optimization into measurement, verification, and local negotiation.

The work is also relevant to L-012 (Intervention-Layer Displacement) — adding a local-impact layer to the protocol may simply create a new optimization surface, shifting harm rather than preventing it.

## Seed

**Seed title:** Proxy Evolution Under Political Feedback in Safety-Critical Infrastructure Protocols

**Seed type:** motif

**Seed text:** When a measurable proxy achieves operational dominance in a safety-critical infrastructure protocol and the optimization pressure under that proxy generates visible externalized harm, the protocol system exhibits a characteristic response: rather than being abandoned, the proxy is formally extended or layered with additional accountability metrics. This evolution does not reduce the underlying coordination cost — it relocates it from execution optimization into measurement, verification, and local negotiation. The new layer may itself become subject to metric capture unless the underlying mechanism (the mismatch between proxy and goal) is addressed structurally. This pattern appears to generalize across protocols where (a) the proxy is legible and operationalized at scale, (b) harm displacement is politically visible, and (c) full protocol replacement is infeasible. The result is a slow oscillation toward more granular but not necessarily more aligned proxies.
