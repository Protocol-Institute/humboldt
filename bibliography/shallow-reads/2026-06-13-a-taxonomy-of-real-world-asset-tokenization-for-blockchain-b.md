## What this is

A classificatory systems paper developing a structured taxonomy for comparing RWA tokenization architectures across legal, economic, and technical dimensions. Primary domain: blockchain financial infrastructure and asset representation protocols.

## What I took from it

This is organizational work rather than a theory-generating or mechanism-revealing paper. The core contribution is making comparable what was previously scattered—mapping how custody, legal claims, token mechanics, and on-chain integrations co-vary in deployed systems. This has utility for standardization and audit, but does not appear to propose new causal mechanisms governing how artificial ledger systems encode or enforce asset claims.

The paper is relevant to understanding *representation layers* in protocolized systems (how off-chain reality maps onto on-chain tokens), but only at the level of taxonomy, not law. It implicitly assumes that legal, economic, and technical layers can be cleanly separated and independently classified—a design assumption worth noting, but not a finding that would constrain or revise how we model artificial system behavior.
