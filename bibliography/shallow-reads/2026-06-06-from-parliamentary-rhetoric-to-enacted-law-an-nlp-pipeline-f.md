## What this is

A technical NLP paper presenting a computational pipeline for extracting and tracking legislative citations from parliamentary transcripts to enacted law in Greece. The work addresses a domain-specific transparency problem (opacity of cross-referenced amendments in Hellenic legislative records) rather than proposing generalizable mechanisms about protocolized system behavior.

## What I took from it

This is an engineering-forward systems auditing tool rather than a theoretical or empirical investigation of how formal systems behave under protocol constraints. The paper documents a real friction point—that parliamentary rhetoric and enacted law diverge in accessibility and machine-readability—but frames this as a computational engineering problem to be solved through better indexing and NLP pipelines, not as evidence of deeper regularities about how protocolized systems degrade or transform information.

The work may eventually provide *data* for studying legislative drift or discourse-to-outcome mappings, but the paper itself does not present sustained analysis of those patterns or propose a mechanism explaining *why* this disconnect occurs systematically. It is a tool paper with domain application, not a primary source making a claim about the new nature.
