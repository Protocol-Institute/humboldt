## What this is

A comparative empirical study of four LLM agent architectures (monolithic, chain-based, multi-agent, iterative) applied to journalistic gatekeeping tasks across 200 controlled scenarios. The work treats architectural design as a variable affecting which stories get selected, sources credibility-ranked, and content produced — mapping agent behavior onto classical gatekeeping theory from media studies.

## What I took from it

The paper is a domain-specific instantiation study: it documents that different computational architectures produce different gatekeeping outcomes, confirming that the *locus of editorial decision* shifts when formalized as a legible algorithmic input (L-012). However, the analysis does not examine *why systems tolerate continued malfunction* (L-013) or explain the deeper mechanism by which architectural choice becomes institutionally frozen. The work is descriptive rather than mechanistic — it shows variance across architectures but does not theorize what locks a particular architecture into place once deployed at scale, nor does it examine the anomaly-tolerance dynamics that would allow misaligned gatekeeping to persist undetected. It treats gatekeeping as a task to be optimized, not as a site where optimization pressure causes the locus of decision-making to migrate away from human editorial judgment in ways that become invisible to oversight frameworks.

## Research connections

- **L-012:** Confirms intervention-layer displacement in principle: different agent architectures localize optimization pressure differently, shifting where the "decision" actually lives.
- **L-013:** Suggests a site where paradigm-locked anomaly tolerance could operate: if a particular agent architecture becomes standard in newsrooms, anomalies in its gatekeeping behavior (bias, source exclusion) may accumulate without triggering institutional response.
- **seed-030:** Weakly relevant; the paper does not examine how architectural standardization becomes invisible or how gatekeeping norms are rewritten post-adoption to rationalize the new system.
