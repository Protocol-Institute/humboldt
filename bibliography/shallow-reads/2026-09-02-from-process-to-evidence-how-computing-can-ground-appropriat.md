## What this is

A position/methods paper arguing that empirical evidence on AI legal tool failure rates is necessary to calibrate "appropriate reliance" standards in courts. The work identifies a gap between the *need* for verification protocols (triggered by >1,500 hallucination cases) and the *absence* of systematic failure data to ground auditing practices. Primary domain: legal AI adoption and regulatory response.

## What I took from it

The paper correctly identifies a real coordination problem: courts are issuing verification mandates (careful independent review) without empirical grounding for *what* to review or *how hard* to review it. This is a competent observation about institutional lag under protocol adoption pressure, but the argument does not generalize beyond the legal AI domain or produce actionable mechanism insights.

The work intersects L-015 (Interpretive Continuity Decay in Distributed Governance Protocols) — the legal system is a distributed governance protocol where formal audit and review rules survive, but the *interpretive ground* for what constitutes adequate verification decays as the failure mode becomes legible but unmeasurable. However, the paper does not investigate this decay or its generative mechanism. It simply requests better empirical data, which is good hygiene but not theory-building.

The connection to L-001 (Protocol Ossification Under Adoption Pressure) is surface-level: yes, legal verification standards are hardening in response to adoption. But the paper does not explore whether the ossification is *independent of solution quality* or driven by measurement legibility — it assumes better data will unblock better calibration, which may be true but does not advance the law.

## Research connections

- **L-015:** Distributed governance protocol (courts + lawyers + AI tools) experiencing interpretive continuity decay — formal rules survive, but institutional meaning of "appropriate reliance" becomes illegible.
- **L-001:** Verification standards hardening under adoption pressure, but mechanism of ossification not explored.
- **seed-082 (Additive Intervention in Overloaded Protocols Preserves Root Pressure):** New auditing obligations (careful independent review) add friction without addressing root pressure (computational legibility vs. legal semantic judgment).
