# Idea: Adversarial protocols break stigmergy by enabling agents to erase or fake accumulated traces

**Source:** Discord #Integration levels as ontological hierarchy? (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-06-18
**Connected to:** L-adversarial-unification, H-threat-model-relative
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Substantive refinement of threat-model-relativity, but does not yet warrant independent candidate status; clarifies scope boundaries of existing framework rather than opening new empirical or theoretical terrain. Holds value as annotation layer for future adversarial cases.

## What this is

Adversarial protocols that permit trace erasure or spoofing do not violate stigmergy laws but rather reduce degrees of freedom relative to a *specific* threat model; each adversarial move remains protocol-constrained and generates new state spaces, making adversarial arms races themselves lawful rather than lawless.

## What I took from it

This idea usefully *defangs* a potential objection: that adversarial behavior (deception, trace-wiping, spoofing) represents a fundamental exception to stigmergic coupling. Instead, it reframes the problem as one of *scope*. Stigmergy assumes accumulated traces remain reliable signals; adversarial protocols weaponize the opposite assumption—that traces *can* be corrupted. This is not a violation of the law; it is a change in what counts as "accumulated trace" in the local threat model.

The refinement matters because it prevents the framework from collapsing when real adversaries enter the system. It also clarifies that DOF reduction is always *relative to a model of what agents can do*, not absolute. This is a necessary boundary condition, not a loophole.

However, the idea does not yet explain *which* protocol constraints make adversarial moves themselves remain lawful, or how to predict which threat models will permit which forms of trace corruption while still maintaining coupling. That gap remains.

## Research connections

- **L-adversarial-unification:** Directly supports the claim that adversarial behavior is protocol-governed; provides scope clarification (threat-model-relative) rather than exception.
- **H-threat-model-relative:** Confirms that DOF reduction metrics must be indexed to a specific adversary model; adversarial arms races do not escape this constraint.

## Candidate laws or signals

**none** — The insight is a clarification of scope for existing framework, not a new pattern. Promote to candidate hypothesis only if future cases show that certain classes of adversarial protocol (e.g., cryptographic, temporal, informational) generate *distinct* stigmergic signatures or breaking modes.
