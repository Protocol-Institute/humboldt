## What this is

A policy analysis paper proposing reforms to institutional ethics review processes (IRBs/RECs) to better capture the environmental externalities of computationally-intensive research. The work identifies a governance gap: environmental impact of high-compute research lacks clear institutional oversight mechanisms, despite potentially significant consequences.

## What I took from it

This is prescriptive policy work addressing institutional accountability rather than a primary empirical or theoretical investigation of protocolized systems themselves. It observes that existing ethics review frameworks have poorly-defined policies around environmental impact of computation, and proposes expanding their remit—but does not generate new empirical data about how computational systems actually scale, degrade, or externalize costs, nor does it theorize novel mechanisms of systemic behavior.

The paper is downstream of the recognized problem (computational research has environmental cost) and upstream of implementation. It doesn't investigate *how* review bodies would detect or measure environmental impact, *what decision rules* they would apply, or *what feedback loops* would emerge once they do. It's a gap-identification and reform proposal, not a study of the systems that would govern such reforms.
