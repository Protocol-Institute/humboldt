# Idea: The question of whose temporal frame wins in a protocol narrative is a fundamental philosophical and political stake in how protocol stories are constructed and contested.

**Source:** Discord #External rhythm as temporal protocol (by sachbenny)
**Date read:** 2026-06-13
**Connected to:** none
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Identifies a *dimension* of protocol power (temporal authority) but lacks empirical anchoring or protocol case material. Warrants observation and connection-building before candidacy.

## What this is

The idea proposes that protocols embed temporal ordering choices (whose clock, whose causality, whose narrative sequence), and that these choices constitute a form of control over how protocol actors experience and contest meaning.

## What I took from it

This is a useful *sharpening* of what we might already suspect about protocol asymmetry—but it's sharpened in a direction we haven't yet systematized. Most protocol analysis focuses on rule authority or information asymmetry; this surfaces temporal authority as a distinct, politically consequential layer. It opens a question: *Is temporal framing a lever of protocol power on the same order as access control or rule definition?* 

The idea doesn't yet tell us whether temporal authority is:
- A *universal feature* of all protocols (likely, given sequencing requirements)
- A *variable stake* (some protocols make temporal authority explicit/contested, others obscure it)
- A *design vulnerability* (protocols that hide whose temporal frame "wins" are harder to resist)

What it opens: investigation into protocol contests as *temporal struggles*—who decides what counts as simultaneous, causal, or sequentially valid. This could connect to questions about protocol legibility and resistance.
