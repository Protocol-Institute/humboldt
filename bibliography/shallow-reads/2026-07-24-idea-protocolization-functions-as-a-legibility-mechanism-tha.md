# Idea: Protocolization functions as a legibility mechanism that makes hidden coordination costs visible

**Source:** Discord #Protocols as Total Cost Estimators (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-07-24
**Connected to:** CL-002
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Reinforces existing hypothesis with useful phenomenological framing (legibility as the mechanism of cost revelation), but does not introduce new structural claims or testable distinctions beyond CL-002's core proposition.

## What this is

Protocolization makes previously opaque coordination costs (friction, maintenance, failure modes) *visible and accountable* across distributed systems, functioning as an epistemic rather than eliminatory mechanism.

## What I took from it

This idea sharpens CL-002 by isolating a specific functional mechanism: protocols don't reduce total cost, they *expose* it. This is phenomenologically important—it reframes protocolization from a performance or efficiency intervention to an *accounting and transparency* intervention. 

The claim opens a useful research direction: if protocols are primarily legibility engines, then their value may lie in enabling informed tradeoffs rather than optimization. This suggests we should measure protocol success by *accuracy of cost visibility* rather than cost reduction, and by downstream decisions made *in light of visible costs*. 

However, the core insight—that coordination costs are conserved and protocols reveal this—is already captured in CL-002's formulation. This idea is a refinement of mechanism rather than a new structural claim. It's worth keeping as a *framing note* for future work on cost accounting and system transparency.
