# Idea: The instinct to treat variance in model outputs as a reliability problem (better model selection, variance reduction) may miss the underlying coordination problem the organization must solve.

## What this is

Organizations under pressure to stabilize AI outputs often misidentify the problem as technical variance, leading them to formalize selection and decision procedures rather than address the underlying coordination failure that variance reveals.

## What I took from it

This idea sharpens CL-001 by proposing a *misattribution mechanism*: variance isn't the problem—it's the symptom. The true problem is that the organization lacks a shared decision rule or commitment to what outputs mean in context. The instinct to reduce variance through model selection or post-hoc filtering treats the symptom, leaving the coordination gap intact while *appearing* to solve reliability.

This opens a productive reframing: what looks like a technical problem (model quality) is often a *governance problem* (who decides what counts as an acceptable answer?). Organizations formalize procedures not because variance is solved, but because formalization transfers decision authority away from the model to the institution—a coordination move disguised as a technical one.

The idea challenges the assumption that reliability *scales* with variance reduction. It suggests reliability may instead scale with *clarity of delegation*—the codification of who bears responsibility for interpretation.

## Research connections

- **CL-001 (Formalization Ratchet):** This idea identifies the *mechanism* by which technical pressure triggers formalization: misidentification of variance as the problem rather than recognition of coordination failure.

## Candidate laws or signals

**CL-HUM-001 (Variance-as-Symptom Displacement):** When an organization encounters irreducible output variance under coordination uncertainty, the instinct to reduce variance through technical selection often precedes—and may prevent—the formalization of decision authority. The formalization that follows appears to solve reliability but primarily relocates responsibility.

*Status: candidate for promotion once we observe decision-point triggers (e.g., governance crisis, audit pressure) that distinguish when displacement occurs vs. when coordination is addressed directly.*
