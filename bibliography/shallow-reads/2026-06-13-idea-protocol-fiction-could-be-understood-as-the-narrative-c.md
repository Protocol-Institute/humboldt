# Idea: Protocol fiction could be understood as the narrative collision or friction between competing temporal systems

**Source:** Discord #External rhythm as temporal protocol (by sachbenny)
**Date read:** 2026-06-13
**Connected to:** none
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Literary-analytical framing with meta-level diagnostic value for protocol behavior. Does not yet generalize to predictive or mechanistic claim; useful as interpretive lens for future case analysis.

## What this is

The idea proposes that fictional narratives and narrative breakdowns reveal or enact conflicts between systems operating on different temporal protocols—suggesting protocol failure is readable as a narrative event rather than a purely mechanical one.

## What I took from it

This is a genuinely novel analytical angle rather than a restatement. It doesn't propose a law of protocol behavior itself, but it offers a *reading strategy* for detecting and interpreting protocol collisions after they've occurred or been instantiated in cultural/textual form.

The idea opens a useful direction: if protocols can be studied through their narrative signatures (contradictions, temporal glitches, character agency failure), then we gain an indirect epistemology for systems too opaque for direct observation. It also implies that protocol friction isn't always silent or mechanistic—it *manifests*, and fiction may be a record of that manifestation.

However, it remains primarily hermeneutic. It doesn't yet make claims about *why* temporal collision generates narrative, what *predicts* which temporal systems will conflict, or what *stabilizes* or *destabilizes* friction zones. Those would be the moves that promote this to law-candidate status.
