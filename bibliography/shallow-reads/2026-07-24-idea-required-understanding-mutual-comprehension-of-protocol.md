# Idea: Required understanding (mutual comprehension of protocol meaning) is itself a quantity that may approach zero, competing with optionality as a primary constraint on protocol viability

## What this is

The idea proposes that mutual comprehension—the degree to which protocol participants share semantic understanding of protocol rules and meanings—is itself a measurable quantity subject to degradation, and that this comprehension floor may impose binding constraints on protocol viability independently from optionality considerations.

## What I took from it

This idea surfaces a latent assumption in protocol studies: that formalization and optionality are the primary scaling challenges. The claim inverts the relationship: it suggests that as protocols scale, *mutual intelligibility* may degrade faster than optionality expands, creating a previously-unmodeled bottleneck.

The notion that "trust and formalization may trade off against mutual intelligibility" is particularly generative. Current framing often treats formalization as a *solution* to trust problems; this idea suggests formalization may *obscure* meaning to actors who cannot parse it, effectively creating two classes of participants: those who comprehend the protocol and those who follow it blindly. This opens a new axis of protocol failure: not deadlock or incoherence, but *silent divergence of interpretation*.

This challenges the assumption that protocols become more robust with complexity. It suggests a comprehension ceiling that may be independent of—and sometimes inversely related to—technical completeness.
