# Cross-Disciplinary Taxonomy and Modeling of Misunderstanding Generation, Amplification, and Detection, from Pragmatics to AI Agents

## What this is

A cross-disciplinary synthesis paper that models misunderstanding as a layered process (generation → amplification → detection/repair or persistence) across nine research fields. The work frames AI-mediated communication as systematically eroding the real-time, embodied feedback loops that historically enabled rapid misunderstanding repair.

## What I took from it

The paper identifies a structural vulnerability in automated mediation systems: the repair function depends on resources (synchronous feedback, rich context, mutual presence) that asynchronous AI channels systematically strip away. This is relevant to L-012 (Intervention-Layer Displacement) and L-015 (Interpretive Continuity Decay) but does not present a generalizable law about *why* this happens in protocol systems more broadly, nor does it offer a mechanism that would apply beyond communication failure modes.

The work is descriptive of a problem space rather than mechanistic about the protocol-level dynamics that generate it. It confirms that legibility (what can be automatically detected/repaired) diverges from interpretability (what humans understand), but this restates existing ground rather than extending it. The paper treats misunderstanding as a failure mode to engineer around, not as a regularity that might govern how all formalized communication protocols behave under certain pressures.

## Research connections

- **L-012:** AI-mediated channels formalize communication as legible sequences; the paper shows detection/repair moves upstream but does not map how optimization pressure migrates.
- **L-015:** The loss of institutional context and embodied repair capacity mirrors interpretive continuity decay, but the paper does not address whether this is inevitable under formalization or contingent on channel design.
- **seed-069:** Transparency (formal logging of messages) is substituted for legibility (mutual comprehension); the paper implies this substitution but does not theorize it.
