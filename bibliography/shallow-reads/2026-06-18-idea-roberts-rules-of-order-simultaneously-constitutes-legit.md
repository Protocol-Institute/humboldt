# Idea: Roberts Rules of Order simultaneously constitutes legitimate decision-making (procedurally) and functions as a trust mechanism by making procedural defection legible and challengeable in real-time.

## What this is

The idea proposes that procedural rules (Roberts Rules) collapse what appeared to be two separable functions—legitimacy-constitution and trust-enabling—into a single operational mechanism: visibility of deviation makes procedural defection both illegitimate *and* detectable simultaneously.

## What I took from it

This is a productive test case that dissolves rather than clarifies the constitutive/trust distinction. The claim does not argue they are *separable categories*, but rather that Roberts Rules reveals them as interdependent: legitimacy in artificial systems may *require* real-time legibility of defection to function at all. The constitutive force ("this counts as a decision") and the trust mechanism ("I can see if you're breaking the rules") are not two stages but two aspects of one legibility structure.

This supports H-001/H-002 convergence without demanding new law language. It strengthens the suspicion that "constitution" and "trust" may be better understood as *visibility tiers* rather than categorical splits.
