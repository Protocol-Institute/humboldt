## What this is

Identity and credential protocols represent a high-stress zone where human social/cognitive operating constraints meet digital system scalability demands, making them predictable sites for observing formalization pressure and ratcheting effects.

## What I took from it

This idea operationalizes H-001 (formalization ratchet) by naming a specific institutional domain where the conditions for formalization—substrate mismatch, scaling pressure, irreversibility—should be observable and measurable. Rather than proposing a new mechanism, it identifies where existing mechanisms concentrate.

The insight is methodological: identity systems force explicit rule codification (KYC, verification hierarchies, attestation chains) precisely because human identity judgment is context-dependent, analog, and unscalable. This creates a natural stress test for the hypothesis. The claim avoids overreach—it doesn't claim identity protocols *prove* formalization ratcheting, but flags them as high-yield observational sites.

This is sound but incremental. It does not challenge L-001 or H-001, nor does it propose new dynamics. It refines where to look for existing patterns.
