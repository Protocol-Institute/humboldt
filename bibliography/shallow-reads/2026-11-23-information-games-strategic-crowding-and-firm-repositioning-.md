## What this is

An economics paper examining how firms reposition in response to LLM-based market signals and competitive pressure, with focus on strategic crowding effects in a space where relevance measurement is mediated by language models. The abstract suggests an argument about capacity investment decisions under conditions of legible (search-engine-visible) competitive signaling, but the abstract itself is incomplete and does not clearly articulate a primary theoretical or empirical claim.

## What I took from it

The paper sits at the intersection of Goodhart Generalization (L-004) and Coordination Adoption Nonmonotonicity (L-010): firms optimize for LLM-measured relevance signals, which are themselves legible proxies for market positioning. This creates a potential metric capture scenario — firms clustering in the region of LLM search space that appears optimal, thereby degrading the signal for all.

The connection to L-010 is weaker. The paper does not appear to argue that adoption displays nonmonotonicity (i.e., that more firms adopting a strategy makes it less attractive). Rather, it seems to be observing strategic crowding as an equilibrium outcome of common signal optimization. This is Goodhart-flavored: once relevance measurement becomes computable and legible, firms optimize toward it, corrupting the measurement as a proxy for genuine market value.

The abstract cutoff prevents assessment of whether the paper identifies a *mechanism* distinct from known metric capture dynamics, or merely observes crowding as a competitive outcome.

## Research connections
