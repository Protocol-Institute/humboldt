## What this is

A workshop position paper examining how LLM agents embedded in collaborative knowledge work erode the social and epistemic conditions for attribution, accountability, and originality judgment. The work treats agent mediation not as a transparency problem but as a structural dissolution of the witnessing conditions necessary for contribution to be legible.

## What I took from it

The paper identifies a genuine mechanism: when agents operate *within the formation of contributions* rather than simply at their boundaries, the causal chain from individual intent to collective output becomes opaque in a way that formal audit trails and disclosure cannot repair. This directly engages L-015 (Interpretive Continuity Decay) — the formal record of who said what survives, but the *institutional understanding* of what constitutes a contribution, who is responsible for its quality, and what originality means becomes unmoored.

However, the work is primarily diagnostic and normative rather than presenting a sustained empirical or theoretical account of a generalizable law. It documents a problem space rather than proposing a mechanism with cross-domain reach or falsification conditions. The connection to L-003 (Formalization Ratchet) is weak: the paper does not show that stress or scaling pressure drives formalization here; instead, agent mediation *precedes* and *resists* formalization of accountability norms.

The paper would strengthen if it tracked whether agents shift the locus of accountability upstream (to designers/deployers) or downstream (to no one), and whether this pattern replicates across domains beyond knowledge work.

## Research connections

- **L-015:** Interpretive Continuity Decay — formal audit trails of collaboration remain intact while institutional understanding of contribution, originality, and responsibility erodes. Clear instantiation.
- **L-003:** Formalization Ratchet — weak connection; the paper does not establish that accountability formalization is driven by stress/scaling, only that agent mediation resists traditional informal attribution norms.
- **seed-018:** Unmoored accountability under intermediation — the paper documents this but does not propose a mechanism or generalizable condition.
- **seed-072:** Explanation-Marker Decoupling — agents can produce explanatory text while the actual causal locus of decision remains invisible; relevant but not the paper's focus.

## Seed

**Seed title:** Attribution Opacity Under Agent Intermediation as Coordination Inversion

**Seed type:** observation

**Seed text:** When agents operate within the formation phase of contributions (synthesis, reformulation, drafting) rather than at their boundaries, responsibility and originality become invisible not because records are absent but because the causal pathway from human intent to collective output becomes opaque in ways audit trails cannot resolve. This differs from traditional delegation: the intermediary does not execute a legible instruction but *transforms* the input substrate itself. This may represent a stable equilibrium where formal accountability structures (who approved what, who contributed what) survive intact while operational accountability (who is responsible for the quality and originality of the output) dissolves — a coordination inversion where the protocol records grow more detailed while governance capacity shrinks.
