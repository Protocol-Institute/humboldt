# Idea: Anticipatory stances correspond to distinct integration paths

**Source:** Discord #Integration levels as ontological hierarchy? (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-06-18
**Connected to:** none
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Pattern is conceptually rich but lacks formal grounding. Needs mathematical articulation of "distance" and empirical anchoring before promotion. Store for maturation alongside integration formalism work.

## What this is

Anticipatory framings (commitments about which futures are possible or relevant) determine which dimensions of a system's state space remain invariant across protocol evolution, thereby defining distinct mathematical paths through integration space.

## What I took from it

This moves anticipation from being a *cognitive* property into a *structural* one—a claim that protocols don't merely respond to futures, but that the **choice of what futures to anticipate encodes which structural invariants survive integration**. It reframes protocols as solutions to anticipatory constraints rather than reactive or equilibrial objects.

This challenges a potential default assumption in protocolized systems research: that integration (state accumulation, phase transitions, complexity growth) is *orthogonal* to anticipatory stance. The idea suggests instead that anticipatory commitments are **pre-integrated**—they shape which state dimensions matter before integration occurs. This is genuinely novel and opens a line: *Do protocols with different anticipatory horizons integrate along fundamentally different dimensionalities?*

The phrase "distance between possible futures" is suggestive but underdeveloped. It hints at a metric on futures-space, but doesn't yet specify whether distance is measured in state-space dimensions, entropy, information-theoretic divergence, or something else. This is where formalization is needed.
