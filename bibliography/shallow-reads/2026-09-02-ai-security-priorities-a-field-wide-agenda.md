## What this is

A field-wide agenda document synthesizing prioritized security concerns for AI systems in critical deployment, informed by structured expert elicitation across industry, government, and civil society. Primary contribution is a ranked problem inventory and cost-benefit analysis, not a primary theoretical or empirical argument about protocol dynamics.

## What I took from it

This is a **diagnosis document masquerading as a research contribution**. It captures the current state of anomaly tolerance and governance lag in AI protocol systems — exactly the phenomena L-013 predicts — but does so through organizational sentiment rather than mechanism exposure. The "widening gap between adoption and security readiness" is empirical confirmation that established AI deployment protocols tolerate accumulating malfunction signals without triggering major restructuring (L-013 territory). 

However, the document does not explain *why* this gap persists despite visibility, nor does it propose mechanisms for how security priorities get de-prioritized under adoption pressure. It identifies the symptom (L-001 and L-013 symptoms are present: ossification, anomaly tolerance) but treats them as coordination failures rather than as emergent properties of the protocol stack itself. The expert consensus framing is precisely the kind of legible governance signal that research on L-015 (Interpretive Continuity Decay) and L-013 (Paradigm-Locked Anomaly Tolerance) would predict to *persist unchanged* despite being documented.

## Research connections

- **L-001:** Implicit confirmation that adoption pressure creates downstream governance constraints, but no mechanism exploration.
- **L-013:** Direct empirical marker of paradigm-locked anomaly tolerance — the field is documenting malfunction without triggering restructuring — but the document itself becomes part of the tolerance mechanism rather than a force for change.
- **seed-079 (Externalization as Paradigm Preservation):** Agenda-setting functions as externalization; listing security priorities without restructuring deployment incentives preserves existing protocol paths.

## Method note

This work shows the limits of expert consensus as a research input. Field-wide agendas can document *what is broken* without illuminating *why it stays broken* — and the act of agenda-setting can itself become a pressure valve that reduces urgency for structural investigation. For the new nature research agenda, this suggests: (1) distinguish between symptom inventories and mechanism papers; (2) be alert to governance documents that circulate anomalies without changing adoption incentives, as these are evidence of active tolerance dynamics, not solutions; (3) use agenda documents as *data sources for L-013 and L-015* rather than as research inputs.
