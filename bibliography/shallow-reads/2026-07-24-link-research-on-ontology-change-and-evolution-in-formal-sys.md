## What I took from it

This resource likely formalizes a critical gap: *how do we know when a protocol remains "itself" through evolution?* Bisimulation answers this by defining equivalence not on state but on observable behavior and transition capacity. For the new nature, this suggests ontologies in artificial systems are not carved in logic but negotiated through process equivalence—two protocols can be "the same" despite different internal structure if they exhibit identical behavioral signatures.

The annotation flags this as relevant to **protocol equivalence** and **ontological stability**, which hints the work may offer machinery for detecting when a system's rules have fundamentally shifted versus merely rearranged. This is crucial if we're tracking whether protocolized systems preserve law-like structure under modification.
