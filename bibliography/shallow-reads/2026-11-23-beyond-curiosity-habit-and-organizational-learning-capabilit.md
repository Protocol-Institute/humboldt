## What this is

Empirical survey study (N=510) examining the psychological predictors of unsanctioned generative AI use in workplace settings. The work compares dispositional factors (curiosity/exploratory tendency) against behavioral factors (habit formation and technology acceptance), finding that habit and organizational learning capability are stronger predictors of shadow AI adoption than curiosity alone.

## What I took from it

The paper documents a real phenomenon — informal protocol bypass under formalized governance pressure — but does not develop the mechanism sufficiently to seed law-work. It establishes that *shadow AI use occurs* and that habit predicts it better than dispositional exploration. This confirms the existence condition for L-023 (informality as novelty sink under legible optimization) but does not isolate why formalization *produces* informality as a stable equilibrium, nor does it characterize the conditions under which informal channels become organizational learning channels versus mere evasion.

The work is psychologically grounded (individual habit, technology acceptance) rather than protocol-system grounded. It does not examine whether shadow AI use represents adaptive functional novelty that formal governance channels cannot accommodate, or whether it is simply individual preference for unsupervised tool use. Without that distinction, the observation remains domain-specific rather than a law-shaped fragment.

## Research connections

- **L-023:** Confirms the observation that informal channels persist under formalized governance pressure, but does not mechanically explain why or under what conditions informality becomes a stable innovation sink rather than a transient compliance gap.
- **seed-017 (guidance-layer coalescence):** Shadow AI use suggests employees are receiving decision guidance from an unsanctioned shared layer (the generative model itself), but the paper does not examine whether this functions as hidden coordination or functional specialization.
- **seed-553 (coordination infrastructure formalization as legibility-without-cost-reduction):** Implies that formal AI governance policies may impose compliance cost without reducing coordination cost, pushing work into informal channels. Not explored in the paper.
