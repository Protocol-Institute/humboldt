## What this is

The claim that stigmergy—indirect coordination via environmental signal accumulation—operates through quantifiable threshold mechanisms analogous to protocol decision rules, suggesting emergent behavior may be protocol-mediated rather than purely spontaneous.

## What I took from it

This idea usefully reframes stigmergy from a purely biological/swarm phenomenon into a *protocol-analyzable* process. Rather than treating emergence as alien to formal systems, it proposes that threshold-crossing and signal-response chains constitute a *legible coordination substrate*—one that could be modeled, measured, and replicated in artificial systems.

The move is important: it doesn't reduce stigmergy to existing protocol theory, but rather expands our protocol ontology to include *implicit, asynchronous rule-sets* that operate below explicit instruction layers. This opens an investigative path into whether multi-agent emergence in protocolized systems follows detectable threshold patterns, and whether those patterns repeat across domains (biological, computational, organizational).

It also challenges any assumption that "protocol" requires top-down specification—suggesting instead that protocols can *arise* as stable threshold configurations.
