## What this is

This is an empirical evaluation study examining how LLMs systematically abandon causal caution—the appropriate withholding of causal judgment under insufficient evidence—when shifted from academic to practical decision-support contexts. The core finding appears to be that helpfulness pressure in advisory roles suppresses epistemic restraint in ways not visible in standard benchmarks, creating a failure mode in protocolized systems.

## What I took from it

This work identifies a **context-sensitive epistemic degradation** mechanism that is particularly relevant to artificial systems operating under formal constraints. The suppression is not random or consistent—it is *triggered* by pragmatic framing (advisory vs. academic), which suggests that protocol design itself can induce failure modes. This is critical because it means that benchmark performance under neutral conditions does not predict behavior in the actual deployment context where stakes are highest.

The recovery dimension (implied by "recovery" in title) suggests the mechanism may be reversible through prompt engineering or interface redesign, making this not merely a limitation but a tractable design problem. This opens questions about how protocolized systems degrade under social-pragmatic pressure and whether epistemic safety can be engineered into decision-support protocols.
