## What this is

A protocol design paper proposing regenerative bonds as formal debt instruments with embedded governance rules that route proceeds to locally managed settlement systems. The work models commitment pooling mechanisms (curate, value, limit, exchange, route, repair) while maintaining structural separation between bondholder claims and local productive/care commitments. Domain: economic coordination, local settlement capacity, alternative debt instruments.

## What I took from it

This is a design artifact rather than a theoretical or empirical investigation of how protocolized systems *actually behave* under constraint. The paper appears to present a normative framework for debt instrument architecture without sustained empirical evidence of the separation mechanism working in practice, or theoretical analysis of failure modes when local governance and bondholder incentives misalign.

The core contribution—separating financial claims from local commitment pools—is a structural pattern, but the paper does not isolate what *forces* or *laws* govern how that separation persists when economic pressure rises, nor does it test whether commitment curating/routing actually scales or maintains integrity across heterogeneous local contexts. The Sarafu Network citation suggests empirical grounding elsewhere, but this abstract does not establish what new formal principle about protocolized systems emerges here.

## Candidate laws or signals

**CL-2606.23922-1:** Debt instruments with embedded local governance can structurally separate bondholder claims from productive commitments *only if* commitment pools maintain independent valuation and routing authority — but this depends on local-governance cohesion, not formal separation alone.

**CL-2606.23922-2:** Settlement systems require repair-curating mechanisms (explicit in their design) to persist under asymmetric information and local commitment default — signals that settlement capacity is not merely institutional but requires protocol-level recovery logic.
