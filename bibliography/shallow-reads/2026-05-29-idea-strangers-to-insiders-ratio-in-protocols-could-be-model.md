# Idea: Strangers-to-insiders ratio in protocols could be modeled using cell growth/death dynamics

**Source:** Discord #new-nature (by boredgargoyle)
**Date read:** 2026-05-29
**Connected to:** H-001
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Methodological proposal for modeling protocol membership dynamics; lacks empirical grounding and domain-specific validation but offers measurable framework worth tracking as modeling approaches mature.

## What this is

A proposal to apply biological population dynamics (birth/death rate equilibria) to the composition shifts in protocol communities, specifically to quantify how the ratio of newcomers to established participants changes under adoption pressure and what steady-state membership structures emerge.

## What I took from it

This idea offers a potentially quantifiable lens on a phenomenon L-001 (Protocol Ossification) treats structurally but not demographically: *who* resists change in an adopted protocol may correlate with insider-to-stranger density. If coordination cost is conserved across layer transitions (H-001), then the population composition of a protocol—not just its rule set—may be a hidden cost mechanism: high insider dominance may reduce explicit coordination cost but increase friction for new entrants, externalizing the coordination burden.

The BESTDR framework (birth, entry, survival, transfer, death, replication) is borrowed from ecology and could reveal whether protocol membership reaches a stable composition ratio or oscillates. This is interesting because it decouples *protocol adoption metrics* (adoption count, transaction volume) from *protocol health* (sustainability of newcomer onboarding). A protocol could look "adopted" but internally stratify into a hostile insider core, gradually losing the diversity of coordination perspectives that originally sustained it.

This does not directly challenge L-001–L-005, but it suggests L-001 may operate *through* demographic mechanisms—ossification may be an effect of insider-majority lock-in, not just rule-stickiness.

## Research connections

- **L-001:** Provides a demographic mechanism by which adoption pressure could drive ossification—insider-majority states may actively resist change regardless of rule quality.
- **L-003:** The Formalization Ratchet may be accelerated by insider dominance; newcomers have weaker voice in informal norm development.
- **H-001:** If coordination cost is conserved, it may shift from explicit protocol design to informal newcomer filtering; population dynamics could measure this redistribution.
- **H-002:** Trust accumulation in aging protocols might be correlated with insider density rather than age itself; testable via membership composition time-series.

## Candidate laws or signals

**CH-001-boredgargoyle:** Membership stratification under adoption pressure — Protocols experiencing rapid adoption develop insider-dominated population structures that reduce explicit coordination cost at the expense of onboarding cost; steady-state membership ratios may be predictable from early adoption velocity and entry barriers.

*Note: Not yet a law—requires empirical time-series data from multiple protocol communities (governance forums, blockchain DAOs, standardization bodies) and validation that population dynamics meaningfully predict ossification trajectories independent of rule changes.*
