## What this is

An empirical mapping study documenting ownership concentration and structural opacity in online news media across three jurisdictions. The work quantifies who controls outlets and measures whether co-ownership correlates with editorial alignment.

## What I took from it

The paper documents a familiar pattern: formalization of ownership *reveals* concentration and responsibility diffusion, but does not itself generate a new mechanism. The finding that >50% of outlets sit behind single-entity control while the remainder are "hard to trace" confirms L-003 (Formalization Ratchet) in a media domain — as ownership structures become legible, the pressure to consolidate increases, and informal plurality becomes formally concentrated. The secondary finding (co-owned outlets report more similarly) is consistent with L-004 (Goodhart Generalization) applied to editorial policy: once ownership becomes the measurable proxy for editorial independence, editors optimize toward convergence.

However, this is a snapshot measurement in a single domain, not a theoretical or mechanistic contribution. It does not show *why* formalization drives consolidation, under what conditions the effect reverses, or what the failure modes of ownership-as-accountability-proxy are. The causal chain (ownership → responsibility → editorial uniformity) is assumed, not derived or tested.

## Research connections

- **L-003:** Confirms that formalization of media ownership structures produces legible concentration; consistent with broader Formalization Ratchet hypothesis.
- **L-004:** Co-ownership correlation with editorial uniformity suggests ownership has become a measurable proxy for editorial independence, and optimization toward that proxy drives convergence.
- **seed-015:** Supports the observation that transparency about governance structure can paradoxically entrench concentration by making it legible as an optimization target.
