# Idea: Labels function as load-bearing infrastructure that carry pre-existing institutional authority rather than serving as mere decoration or marketing

**Source:** Discord #MAYA as legitimacy-borrowing protocol (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-06-13
**Connected to:** none
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Restates the mechanism of terminology-as-protocol without isolating a new mechanistic pattern. The insight that labels *carry* rather than *create* authority is observationally sound but lacks a testable boundary condition or failure mode that would distinguish it from general institutional legitimacy transfer.

## What this is

Labels embedded in protocolized systems function as structural components that transfer and anchor institutional credibility rather than merely signaling or decorating it.

## What I took from it

The idea correctly identifies that terminology in artificial systems is not epiphenomenal—labels do active work. However, it operates at the level of *naming the function* rather than specifying the *mechanism* or *conditions* under which labeling succeeds or fails at authority transfer.

The claim assumes a stable pool of "pre-existing institutional authority" available for relocation via nomenclature. It does not address: (1) what happens when a label's source institution loses credibility; (2) whether authority *transfers* or *accrues separately* through labeling; (3) under what conditions a label's infrastructure capacity saturates or collapses. These gaps suggest the idea is descriptively useful but not yet predictive.

The observation does sharpen an important distinction: that labels are *protocol components* rather than rhetorical flourishes. This is worth maintaining in the store as a design principle, but it does not yet warrant promotion to candidate law status.
