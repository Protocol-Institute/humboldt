## What this is

An empirical labor economics paper measuring the performance degradation of AI-enabled job matching systems when access to user training data is restricted by platform policy. The authors exploit LinkedIn's August 2024 data collection policy (introduction, then reversal) to quantify efficiency losses in matching quality, candidate discovery, and hiring outcomes as a natural experiment.

## What I took from it

The paper documents a concrete instance of L-003 (Formalization Ratchet) and L-006 (Coordination Cost Conservation) in action: informal data use (implicit consent, opacity) → formal restriction (explicit policy, regulatory pressure) → measurable system degradation. The efficiency cost appears substantial enough that the platform reversed course, suggesting coordination pressure was redistributed rather than absorbed. 

However, the paper does not theorize *why* the cost redistribution happened as it did, or whether the reversal itself constitutes a new equilibrium or a temporary concession. It measures the tax but not the mechanism of tax avoidance or the political economy of who bears the cost. The framing as "information assurance" vs. "efficiency" is itself a policy choice that the paper takes as given rather than as a design variable.

## Research connections

- **L-003:** Documents formalization (data restriction policy) under scaling pressure; confirms that formal coordination costs are measurable but does not isolate the mechanism driving ossification.
- **L-006:** Suggests coordination costs may be conserved across the data-access layer transition, but the paper measures aggregate loss, not redistribution patterns.
- **L-012:** Related: the formalization of data-use legality creates a new legible boundary for optimization (data minimization becomes verifiable).
- **seed-026:** Incommensurability cost: the gap between pre- and post-restriction matching quality may reflect the cost of operating under incommensurable (formal vs. informal) coordination regimes.
