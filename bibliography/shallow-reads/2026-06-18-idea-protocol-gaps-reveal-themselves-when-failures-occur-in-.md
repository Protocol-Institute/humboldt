# Idea: Protocol gaps reveal themselves when failures occur in unanticipated ways; gap danger correlates with consequence of the unrepresented future and speed of its arrival.

**Source:** Discord #Discussion: 2026-06-17 (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-06-18
**Connected to:** none
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Proposes a failure-assessment framework that bridges observation (gap detection via failure) with risk quantification (consequence × speed). Lacks formalized relationship to protocol ontology; requires theoretical grounding before promotion.

## What this is

Failure modes reveal protocol incompleteness; the danger posed by any gap is a function of both the magnitude of consequences it enables and the temporal velocity at which those consequences manifest.

## What I took from it

This idea provides a *detection and triage mechanism* rather than a law of protocol behavior itself. It pivots from "gaps exist" (already assumed) to "gaps become *legible* only through failure" and "not all gaps pose equal risk." The claim introduces two dimensions—consequence severity and arrival speed—that could operationalize vulnerability assessment in protocolized systems.

The framing opens a critical question: **Are protocol gaps latent until failure, or are they theoretically identifiable in advance?** This challenges any assumption that complete protocol specification is possible or necessary, and suggests instead that gap-danger mapping may be the more tractable problem. It also implies protocols are fundamentally reactive to futures they cannot fully represent—a core limitation worth exploring systematically.

**CL-Discord-001:** *Gap-Danger Correlation Principle*—In protocolized systems, the urgency and severity of a protocol gap correlate not with gap size but with the product of consequence magnitude and manifestation speed; gaps with high consequence but slow arrival may remain tolerable longer than low-consequence high-velocity failures.
