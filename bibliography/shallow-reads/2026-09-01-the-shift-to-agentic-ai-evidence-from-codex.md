## What this is

Large-scale usage analysis of OpenAI's Codex agentic AI tool across three populations (personal, organizational, internal), tracking adoption velocity and comparative usage patterns in the first half of 2026. The paper documents rapid growth in agentic delegation but does not present a sustained theoretical argument about mechanisms or a primary empirical finding that challenges existing law inventory.

## What I took from it

The abstract signals a factual observation—agentic AI adoption is accelerating faster outside organizations than within them—but the excerpt provided does not present mechanism or claim. The triage connection to L-008 (Proxy Optimization Under Computable Enforcement) and L-012 (Intervention-Layer Displacement) is reasonable: agentic systems do shift where optimization pressure accumulates. However, this reads as a descriptive adoption curve, not evidence that *changes the terms* of those open lines. The paper appears to be establishing baseline usage patterns rather than isolating conditions under which agentic delegation produces measurable protocol-level effects (capture, displacement, escalation).

The fact that external adoption outpaces internal adoption is interesting but not yet separated from confounders (pricing, access control, use-case distribution, risk tolerance). Without mechanism isolation or cross-domain generalization, this is a competent snapshot of a single tool's diffusion.

## Research connections

- **L-008:** Agentic systems do render agent decisions as legible, computable actions—this is machinery for proxy optimization, but the paper does not isolate whether optimization capture is occurring.
- **L-012:** Delegation to agents does move the decision locus, but the paper does not measure whether this produces intervention-layer displacement or merely distributes labor differently.
- **seed-053:** Shared infrastructure (Codex) used by multiple organizational actors could enable emergent collusion or coordination; external > internal adoption might reflect cheaper uncoordinated use vs. internal governance friction.
