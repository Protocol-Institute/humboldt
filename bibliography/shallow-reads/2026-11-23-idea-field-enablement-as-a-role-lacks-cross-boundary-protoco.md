# Idea: Field enablement as a role lacks cross-boundary protocol perspective; framing needed for coordination infrastructure that scales across organizational silos

**Source:** Discord #Protocoleers (by 4umd)
**Date read:** 2026-11-23
**Connected to:** L-006, seed-454
**Kind:** content
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** The idea correctly identifies a real coordination cost concentration problem but frames it as a role/organizational design question rather than as a protocolized-system regularity. The underlying pattern — that coordination costs persist and concentrate at protocol layer boundaries even when individual layers are well-designed — is already tracked by L-006 and seed-454. The contribution here is observational (gap in current institutional practice) rather than law-shaped (a regularity about how systems behave under formalization pressure).

## What this is

The proposal that organizational roles lack the cross-protocol perspective needed to coordinate at integration boundaries, causing latent coordination costs to persist and concentrate across organizational silos.

## What I took from it

This is a sound empirical observation about institutional practice, but it conflates two distinct levels: (1) a real mechanism — coordination costs do accumulate at protocol layer boundaries and are expensive to make visible or reduce (L-006) — and (2) an organizational solution — creating a role to manage them. The idea assumes that the problem is *awareness* or *role fragmentation*, when the deeper pattern may be that coordination costs are *structurally conserved* across layer transitions and simply get relocated rather than eliminated. A "field enablement" role that lacks cross-boundary perspective is a symptom, not a cause. The more interesting inversion: even if such a role existed and had perfect cross-boundary visibility, it would still face L-006's conservation constraint. The proposal opens a question about whether coordination infrastructure can be *designed* to reduce these costs, or whether formalization and explicit governance merely make them legible without reducing them.

## Seed

**Seed title:** Coordination Infrastructure Formalization as Legibility-Without-Cost-Reduction

**Seed type:** question

**Seed text:** When latent coordination costs accumulate at protocol layer boundaries (L-006), designing explicit coordination infrastructure or roles to manage them makes those costs legible without necessarily reducing them — it may instead displace them into the new infrastructure layer itself. The question: does formalized cross-boundary coordination reduce net coordination cost, or does it conserve cost while increasing transparency and control surface? This may generalize: in any system where total work is conserved across abstraction layers, making a hidden cost visible and assigning it to a specific agent or role does not reduce the total work — it only changes who bears it and how visible it is.
