## What this is

An experimental economics paper using controlled communication protocols to decompose level-k strategic reasoning in iterated guessing games. The work applies the Burchardi-Penczynski method to the beauty contest and 11-20 games, aiming to identify how agents at different reasoning depths form beliefs about "level-0" behavior and coordinate accordingly.

## What I took from it

The paper sits at the boundary between game-theoretic reasoning and empirical coordination failure, but does not develop a sustained theoretical argument about *why* coordination adoption is nonmonotonic or what mechanism explains the gap between level-k models and observed behavior. It is a diagnostic tool applied to a narrow domain (iterated guessing games under laboratory conditions with explicit communication channels).

The triage note flags L-010 (Coordination Adoption Nonmonotonicity), but the paper does not generalize beyond the game-theoretic setting. The intra-team communication method is a measurement apparatus, not a law-generating observation. The work confirms that level-k models have residual unexplained variance (the "inconsistency" with standard predictions in 11-20 games), but it does not propose a mechanism that would transfer to protocolized systems, distributed governance, or automated decision architectures.

## Research connections

- **L-010:** The paper documents coordination failure modes in guessing games but does not explain nonmonotonicity or identify the underlying protocol structure that would generate it in wider systems.
- **seed-073 (Correlated Failure Under Proxy Consensus):** Level-k models rely on agents sharing a model of "level-0" behavior; if that proxy collapses or becomes heterogeneous, consensus in iterated play fails — but this mechanism is not explored.
