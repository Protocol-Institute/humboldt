# Idea: Three distinct temporal domains operate simultaneously in coordination failures

**Source:** Discord #External rhythm as temporal protocol (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-06-13
**Connected to:** H-001
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Taxonomic clarity without mechanistic claim; useful analytical frame for future hypothesis construction but does not itself propose testable regularity or causal pathway.

## What this is

The idea proposes a tripartite temporal ontology—protocol time (enforced/shared), social time (negotiated/dyadic), psychological time (private/unmeasurable)—as a framework for understanding why coordination failures emerge and persist in protocolized systems.

## What I took from it

This is a productive phenomenological decomposition that avoids collapsing temporal experience into a single clock. It correctly identifies that coordination failures often involve *desynchronization across registers* rather than simple clock failure. The distinction opens a useful diagnostic question: Which temporal domain is the primary site of failure? (Protocol violation, social renegotiation, or psychological drift?)

However, the idea remains largely taxonomic. It names three clocks but does not yet propose *how* they interact mechanically, *why* they desynchronize under specific conditions, or what *observable signals* would distinguish failures in one domain from another. It clarifies the problem space rather than mapping a law. It is also not clear whether these three are exhaustive or whether "psychological time" is measurable-in-principle (through artifact analysis, behavioral lag, narrative reconstruction) or permanently dark.

The idea is strongest as a *diagnostic vocabulary* for future case work, and may eventually enable hypothesis formation about jurisdiction conflicts (e.g., "coordination failures escalate when protocol time and social time enter zero-sum conditions").
