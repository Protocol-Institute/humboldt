## What this is

A systematic empirical mapping of how commercial insurance products are responding to agentic AI deployment by categorizing 55 AI threat classes against 26 insurance products/regimes. The paper identifies three regulatory postures: affirmative coverage (explicit), silent exposure (legacy policies unintentionally covering AI risks), and active exclusion. This is a primary source on institutional adaptation to a new hazard class.

## What I took from it

This work documents a critical phenomenon in the protocolized layer: **institutional lag and deliberate boundary-drawing**. Insurance carriers face a mapping problem identical to what we see in AI governance broadly — agentic systems create risk classes that don't fit legacy ontologies (cyber, E&O, D&O, EPLI, crime, media). Rather than pre-emptively covering, the industry is fragmenting into explicit exclusions and silent gaps. This suggests that **institutional systems under uncertainty don't expand coverage; they create discontinuities**. The silent exposure category is particularly revealing — it shows that unmodified legacy protocols can accidentally become liability traps when the "nature" they govern changes. This is relevant to understanding how protocolized systems fail under novel agent behavior: not through dramatic breach, but through category collapse.
