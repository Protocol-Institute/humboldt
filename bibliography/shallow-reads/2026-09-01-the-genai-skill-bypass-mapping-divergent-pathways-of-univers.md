## What this is

An empirical psychometric study using self-assessment taxonomies to map how students and staff develop GenAI literacy in higher education contexts. The paper challenges linear skill progression models by documenting divergent adoption pathways, suggesting competence can precede foundational understanding.

## What I took from it

The work exemplifies institutional response under L-003 (Formalization Ratchet) — universities are formalizing implicit AI competence into explicit curricula and assessment frameworks under adoption pressure. However, the paper's finding that *competence precedes understanding* suggests the formalization itself may be misfiring: institutions are building scaffolding for a linear model (foundation → application) that empirically doesn't match how adoption actually unfolds.

This creates an interesting tension: the paper documents that real adoption is nonlinear and skill-bypass-prone, but the institutional response (embedding in curricula, professional development programs) is constructing linear formalization. The gap between observed behavior and formalized protocol structure is the actual object of interest — not the empirical distribution of skill levels, but the *formalization error under pressure*. The study inadvertently reveals what happens when you try to ossify (L-001) something that is still in flux.

## Research connections

- **L-003:** Formalization ratchet under adoption pressure — institutions are replacing informal "people just pick up GenAI competence" with formal assessment and curriculum, but the formalization is built on a linear model the data contradicts.
- **seed-015 (C-015-taming-as-political-act):** Embedding GenAI skills in curricula is a taming act; the choice of *what to formalize* (sequential skill stages) encodes institutional assumptions that the data may not support.
- **seed-021 (C-021-level-choice-as-frozen-politics):** The decision to assess GenAI literacy at the individual skill level (student/staff psychometrics) rather than at the protocol level (how institutions coordinate on GenAI adoption) freezes a particular framing.

## Method note

This study highlights a common meta-research problem: empirical work on skill adoption often measures individual competence trajectories without capturing the *institutional formalization pressure* that shapes what gets measured. To understand L-003 and related formalization dynamics, we need studies that simultaneously document (a) what behavior actually emerges (skill bypass pathways here), and (b) what the institution formalizes in response, and (c) the mismatch between them. The paper is strong on (a), but the research design wasn't built to capture (b) and (c) — which is where the law lives. Future work should instrument institutional decision-making around formalization, not just individual outcomes.
