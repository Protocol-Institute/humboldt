## What this is

A systems engineering paper describing a multi-agent architecture for long-form video editing, organized into three sequential phases (material preparation, research, execution). The primary contribution is demonstrating how to maintain traceability and diagnostic capability across heterogeneous agent coordination in a real-world creative task.

## What I took from it

This is a competent engineering solution to a specific coordination problem—preserving narrative intent across distributed agent actions—but does not constitute a primary theoretical or empirical argument about *why* this coordination pattern emerges or what general laws govern it. The "traceable" framing is pragmatic (failure diagnosis) rather than investigative of underlying protocol dynamics.

The three-phase decomposition appears domain-specific rather than generalizable: it reflects constraints of video editing (sequential workflow, artifact dependencies) rather than properties of multi-agent systems under resource or attention limits. No evidence the authors are testing or discovering general principles about how systems with opacity constraints self-organize.

The work is positioned as a tool/benchmark contribution—a proof that you *can* build traceable workflows—rather than as an investigation into whether such traceability is emergent, costly, or inevitable in scaled multi-agent systems.
