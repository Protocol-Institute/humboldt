# Idea: Protocols compress variety by design, and unabsorbed variety does not disappear

**Source:** Discord #Requisite Variety as Protocol Constraint (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-06-06
**Connected to:** H-001
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** The core mechanism (variety conservation under protocol constraint) is already represented in H-001. This formulation adds valuable specificity about *where* unabsorbed variety manifests (workarounds, shadow processes) but does not open a new theoretical direction requiring candidate law status at this time. Useful as a refinement and concrete example for future H-001 elaboration.

## What this is

Protocols achieve coordination by selectively absorbing certain varieties of action/state, but cannot eliminate variety itself—only redirect it toward informal, undocumented, or adversarial workarounds and shadow processes.

## What I took from it

This idea sharpens H-001 by moving beyond abstract "conservation" into observable mechanisms: it predicts that protocol-compliant systems will develop measurable shadow infrastructure (informal channels, exception handling, documented noncompliance). Rather than asking "is variety conserved?", it asks "where does it go when the protocol doesn't absorb it?"—and proposes shadow processes as the thermodynamic sink. This is a useful mechanistic refinement, not a new law. It also suggests a diagnostic: high-friction protocols should correlate with detectable shadow activity, which is testable. However, H-001 already encompasses this logic; the idea does not challenge the hypothesis, only provides a more concrete operationalization of how conservation manifests.
