# Idea: Ambiguity originates in the sign-interpreter relationship rather than as an intrinsic property of signs

**Source:** Discord #Discussion: 2026-06-08 (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-06-13
**Connected to:** H-002
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** The claim is theoretically generative but requires grounding in protocol examples before structural validation. Storing as ideation pending case-driven development.

## What this is

Ambiguity is not a property of signs themselves, but emerges from gaps in the sign-interpreter relationship; once encoded into protocol structures, it becomes a coordination problem independent of any single interpreter's understanding.

## What I took from it

This idea performs useful semiotic work by relocating ambiguity from the sign (traditional view) to the *relationship*—a move that aligns with systems thinking about protocols. It suggests ambiguity is relational/pragmatic rather than semantic, which opens a question: if ambiguity is originally relational, what mechanisms *fix* it into protocol? The second half of the claim—that it becomes "independent of individual interpretation" once structured—implies a phase transition from cognitive to institutional problem. This is valuable because it suggests we should distinguish between *interpretive uncertainty* (solvable by individuals) and *protocol ambiguity* (requiring coordination). 

However, the claim needs grounding. How does a sign-interpreter gap become a protocol structure? Does this require formalization, repetition, or scale? Without examples (versioning conflicts, API contract disputes, instruction parsing failures), the mechanism remains abstract.
