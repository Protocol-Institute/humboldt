## What this is

A game-theoretic formalization of strategic behavior under continuous regulatory auditing, modeling compliance as a T-round Stackelberg game. The work formalizes a specific class of gaming tactics (delay, noise-envelope drift, sample attrition exploitation, metric ambiguity cherry-picking) that emerge when compliance obligations become computable and continuously monitored, rather than one-shot or outcome-focused.

## What I took from it

This paper is a **competent formalization of tactics within an already-mapped mechanism** rather than a discovery of new lawlike behavior. L-008 (Proxy Optimization Under Computable Enforcement) and L-014 (Strategic Boundary Concentration Under Computable Legality) both predict exactly this outcome: when protocol obligations become legible and enforcement becomes precise, optimizing agents shift gaming to the boundary layers where legibility breaks down (temporal slicing, metric definition ambiguity, attrition dynamics). The paper confirms this in the specific domain of continuous auditing but does not generalize the mechanism or discover a new pattern.

The work adds **tactical specificity** (delay, drift, attrition) but does not explain *why* these particular tactics cluster together, nor does it establish a law about when continuous monitoring fails as an enforcement device. It is a benchmark and toolkit paper, not a theoretical contribution to the architecture of protocol gaming itself.
