# Idea: Legitimacy-borrowing can be deliberately engineered by attaching novel mechanisms to pre-ratified conceptual frameworks

**Source:** Discord #MAYA as legitimacy-borrowing protocol (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-06-13
**Connected to:** H-003, H-004
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Proposes a reproducible design pattern for legitimacy transfer. Moves the phenomenon from observed outcome to engineered protocol. Does not yet warrant promotion—requires empirical mapping of success conditions and failure modes across domains—but warrants close observation as candidate law material.

## What this is

The claim proposes that institutional trust can be systematically transferred to novel mechanisms by anchoring them to existing, pre-legitimized conceptual or institutional frameworks, and that this transfer is reproducible and designable rather than emergent.

## What I took from it

This idea sharpens a distinction between *legitimacy as accident* and *legitimacy as engineered artifact*. It suggests that trust transfer is not merely a side effect of semantic similarity or historical continuity, but a designable protocol—one with exploitable surface points (label choice, framework attachment, mechanism visibility).

The claim challenges a passive reading of legitimacy adoption and opens a design space: if attachment points can be identified and optimized, then legitimacy borrowing becomes amenable to systematic variation, prediction, and possibly governance. This reframes questions from "how does legitimacy migrate?" to "what are the engineering parameters that control legitimacy migration?"

It also surfacaes a risk: if the mechanism is designable, it is also *weaponizable*—suggesting that institutional capture, regulatory arbitrage, and trust spoofing all operate via the same transfer protocols.

## Candidate laws or signals

**CL-MAYA-001:** Legitimacy transfer efficiency scales with structural isomorphism between the novel mechanism and the pre-ratified framework it attaches to; transfer degrades with visible novelty or framework incompatibility.
