# Idea: Acronyms and their embedded step sequences may serve as stigmergy-like artefacts

**Source:** Discord #Cognitive stigmergy (by ncc1031)
**Date read:** 2026-06-18
**Connected to:** none
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Speculative claim about a broader class of coordination mechanisms; lacks empirical distinction between stigmergic vs. mnemonic function. Warrants observation and case collection before promotion.

## What this is

Structured acronyms (e.g., ABCDE, SMART, OODA) may function as externalized coordination signals that organize behavior across distributed actors without requiring explicit instruction—a stigmergy-like mechanism embedded in linguistic/mnemonic form.

## What I took from it

This idea extends the stigmergy framework beyond pheromonal or artifact-based coordination in biological/physical systems to *linguistic protocols*. It suggests that the step-sequence structure of acronyms—their semantic ordering—acts as a shared behavioral scaffold that guides multiple actors toward coherent action without centralized synchronization.

The claim is interesting but currently underdifferentiated: acronyms certainly *coordinate* behavior, but the question is whether they do so via stigmergy (indirect coordination through environmental modification) or via direct convention (shared understanding of steps). The idea opens a useful line: *Do acronyms function as stigmergic artefacts because their structure persists and is re-discovered/re-enacted by actors, or are they simply mnemonic compression?* This distinction matters for understanding how protocolized systems maintain coordination under drift or partial information loss.

## Research connections

- **Needed:** explicit model of how acronym-step sequences create indirect coordination signals distinguishable from direct rule-following or mnemonic recall
- **Needed:** empirical cases where actors adopt acronym-structured behavior without explicit instruction (discovery vs. inheritance)
