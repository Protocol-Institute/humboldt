# No One Wins in Nuclear War: A Social Simulation of Military Decision-making

**Connected to:** L-013, seed-030
**Kind:** meta

## What this is

A meta-methodological paper presenting WOPR, a deterministic replay-validated simulation engine for studying organizational decision-making under high-stakes conditions, instantiated initially via a card-game formalization of nuclear conflict. The work is tooling and infrastructure for studying how institutions *recognize and tolerate anomalies in their own decision logic*, not a primary source advancing a sustained theoretical argument about protocolized systems themselves.

## What I took from it

The paper is valuable as a *research infrastructure* contribution but does not itself furnish primary evidence about laws of the new nature. It proposes a method for generating controlled observations of how decision protocols behave when agents are forced to operate within formally specified rule systems — which is relevant to L-013 (Paradigm-Locked Anomaly Tolerance) insofar as it could *enable* empirical study of that phenomenon. However, the paper does not present data, analysis, or argument about whether or how institutions actually tolerate anomalies, nor does it generalize beyond the simulation domain. The "decision-point contract" design is reusable but the paper treats it as a technical affordance rather than exploring what happens *when* agents operate inside formalized rule spaces over extended periods.

The triage note's reference to seed-030 suggests the authors or triager see potential for using simulation as a vehicle to isolate paradigm lock — but that work has not yet been done in this submission.

## Research connections

- **L-013:** WOPR is a potential *observation platform* for studying anomaly tolerance in formally specified protocols, but the paper does not yet present evidence for or against the law.
- **seed-030:** Simulation-based instantiation of rule systems could reveal when institutions continue operating within a formal paradigm despite legible failure signals — but no such analysis is present.

## Method note

This paper exemplifies a legitimate but distinct research mode: infrastructure and tooling for future empirical work on protocolized systems. It should not be treated as primary evidence for laws of the new nature. The value lies in its potential as an *observation apparatus* — one where the rules are deterministic, auditable, and replay-validated, making it possible to isolate the behavior of decision-making under formal constraint. Future work using WOPR to study anomaly tolerance, norm drift, or metric capture in closed-loop simulations would be escalation-worthy; this methodological foundation is not.
