# Idea: Cross-group behavioral variance (rather than single global optionality) may be the conserved or globally-decreasing quantity when protocols govern multiple distinct interaction structures simultaneously.

**Source:** Discord #Does protocol opinion really go to zero? (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-07-24
**Connected to:** CL-002
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** The idea proposes a structural refinement to an existing coordination law rather than introducing a new domain. It requires formalization of "cross-group variance" and its conservation mechanics before promotion. Store as a candidate hypothesis seed pending definitional work and multi-group empirical cases.

## What this is

When protocols coordinate multiple distinct groups simultaneously, the system may conserve or minimize *variance in behavior across groups* rather than *global optionality per agent*, inverting the assumption of CL-002.

## What I took from it

CL-002 appears to model single-layer or homogeneous coordination spaces where optionality (agent choice bandwidth) is the conserved quantity. This idea reframes the conservand for **heterogeneous multi-group systems**: instead of asking "how much choice does the protocol preserve globally?", ask "how much *uniformity of constraint* is imposed across structurally different groups?"

This opens a crucial distinction: a protocol might preserve high per-agent optionality *within* a group while enforcing tight cross-group behavioral alignment (e.g., different reward structures within teams, but synchronized output formats across teams). The trade-off may not be optionality-as-unity, but *within-group variance vs. between-group variance*.

This is a genuine refinement, not a restatement. It challenges the assumption that optionality is a scalar quantity and suggests protocols may operate on *variance topology* — the shape and distribution of behavioral freedom across a multi-group topology.

## Research connections

## Candidate laws or signals

**H-CG-001 (Cross-Group Variance Conservation):** In multi-group protocolized systems, protocols do not minimize global optionality but rather redistribute variance such that cross-group behavioral homogeneity increases as within-group optionality constraints tighten, or vice versa. The sum of within-group variance + between-group constraint may be the conserved quantity.
