## What this is

A game-theoretic paper studying divisible resource allocation when complementary goods are held by decentralized agencies with partial observability of need profiles. The work isolates information cost from incentive cost by comparing decentralized allocation against a generous benchmark, measuring welfare loss under incomplete coordination.

## What I took from it

This is competent mechanism design work within a well-established frame (information asymmetry in allocation). The core contribution appears to be quantifying welfare approximation under decentralized observation constraints — a bounded loss result. 

The relevance to L-006 (Coordination Cost Conservation) is surface-level: the paper documents that decentralized coordination imposes welfare cost, but does not argue that this cost is *conserved* across protocol transitions, nor does it explore where coordination pressure gets displaced when agencies formalize their information exchange. The connection to seed-130 (Heterogeneous Sufficiency as Boundary Displacement) is similarly weak — the paper does not examine whether relaxing the decentralization constraint shifts the coordination boundary or creates new heterogeneity costs elsewhere in the system.

No sustained mechanism emerges that generalizes beyond the allocation domain. The work is domain-specific game theory with clean theoretical results, not a law-shaped inquiry into how protocols behave under stress or adoption pressure.

## Research connections

- **L-006:** The paper documents coordination cost under decentralization but does not explore cost conservation or displacement across protocol layer transitions.
- **seed-130:** No examination of whether boundary elimination (e.g., centralizing information) creates heterogeneous sufficiency tradeoffs elsewhere.
