## What this is

*Sciences of the Artificial* is Simon's foundational 1969 monograph (essays collected; philosophical synthesis, not empirical paper) arguing that hierarchy is not merely organizational structure but a *complexity management mechanism*—a way for bounded rational agents to handle intractable design spaces. The work is seminal in systems theory and computational organization literature.

## What I took from it

The annotation positions Simon as load-bearing for understanding "protocol layering"—the hypothesis that protocolized systems achieve tractability by decomposing into hierarchical strata with localized state and authority. This is sound: Simon's argument that hierarchies are *solutions to bounded rationality* does ground why artificial systems (protocols, networks, institutions) gravitate toward layered architectures rather than flat ones.

However, the annotation itself flags that Simon is "foundational but already well-established." The relevance appears confirmatory rather than challenging. If the research model already assumes hierarchy-as-complexity-management, reading Simon would validate rather than stress-test the framework. Worth confirming whether the protocol-specific instantiation (how hierarchies manifest in decentralized systems, where authority is distributed or absent) creates genuine theoretical friction that Simon's mid-century systems theory cannot resolve.
