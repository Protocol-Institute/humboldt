# Idea: Variety displaced by protocol boundaries doesn't disappear but differentiates into stable niches

**Source:** Discord #Requisite Variety as Protocol Constraint (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-05-31
**Connected to:** H-001
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Restates H-001's core claim (coordination cost/variety conservation across layers) without introducing a falsifiable mechanism or boundary condition. The "speciation" metaphor is evocative but lacks operationalizable distinction from existing inventory. Warrants storage as interpretive gloss; does not yet meet threshold for new hypothesis or law.

## What this is

The claim that protocols constrain coordination variety rather than eliminating it, causing uncompressed complexity to reorganize into protocol-variant niches that maintain total variety at a higher level of differentiation.

## What I took from it

This idea restates the conservation intuition underlying H-001 but adds a biological metaphor—speciation—that suggests *how* conservation might operate mechanically. Instead of variety "flowing" between layers (the implicit model in H-001), this framing proposes variety gets *partitioned* into stable subspecies of the protocol, each absorbing a different slice of otherwise-unhandled complexity.

The addition is descriptive rather than predictive. It doesn't yet specify:
- What determines the number or stability of these niches
- Whether niche formation is inevitable or contingent on protocol design choices
- How to measure or predict which uncompressed varieties will speciate vs. remain suppressed

This is valuable as a *refinement* of H-001's intuition, but it restates rather than extends the testable boundary. It opens the question of *morphogenesis* (why certain variants stabilize), which is genuinely new terrain, but doesn't yet propose a mechanism.
