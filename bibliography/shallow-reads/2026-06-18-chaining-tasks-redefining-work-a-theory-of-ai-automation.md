# Chaining Tasks, Redefining Work: A Theory of AI Automation

**Source:** econ.GN updates on arXiv.org — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15960
**Date read:** 2026-06-18
**Connected to:** none
**Escalation:** escalate-to-deep
**Escalation rationale:** This presents a foundational mechanism (task chaining as a restructuring principle) that redefines how comparative advantage operates under AI integration, with claimed non-linear productivity implications that generalize across firm organization regardless of sector.

## What this is

A formal economic theory modeling how firms optimally allocate humans and AI across sequential production steps, introducing "chaining" — contiguous AI-executed sequences — as a unit of organization. The core argument: AI doesn't simply substitute for human labor step-by-step; it induces firms to reorganize task bundling itself, breaking classical comparative advantage logic and generating non-linear productivity dynamics.

## What I took from it

This work identifies a structural reorganization principle absent from most automation literature: the system-level reshuffling of task boundaries when certain steps become AI-executable. Rather than treating AI as a marginal productivity multiplier, it models AI quality improvements as triggering discontinuous changes in job architecture. The failure of comparative advantage under chaining is particularly significant — it suggests that human-AI work organization follows different economic logic than human-human specialization, implying that standard labor economics may not predict outcomes in mixed-agent systems.

The non-linear productivity claim (implicit in "admits a CE" framing) suggests that marginal AI capability improvements don't produce proportional output gains; instead, they cross thresholds where entire workflows collapse or restructure. This is a plausible candidate for a second-order law about protocolized systems: coordination geometry changes discontinuously as component capabilities shift.
