## What this is

The claim that maturity/governance frameworks (CMM, ISO, similar) are designed around assumption of deterministic, repeatable processes, and therefore generate *pathological* responses (over-formalization, coordination tax) when paired with inherently non-deterministic systems like LLMs.

## What I took from it

This idea usefully identifies a **system boundary problem**: maturity frameworks are epistemic-conservative tools—they assume that repeated application of a process yields comparable outputs, and that deviation signals either process failure or knowledge deficiency. Non-deterministic systems violate the first assumption by design. 

The annotation correctly identifies the likely *organizational response*: under pressure to demonstrate control (audit, compliance, user trust), institutions will escalate formalization—adding review gates, rule banks, deterministic routing logic—to convert the variance problem into a coordination problem. This is not a flaw in the framework; it's a predictable failure mode when the framework's assumptions are violated.

The idea opens a productive angle: **formalization is not a solution to variance, but a displacement of it.** Variance moves from output-space (what the system says) to overhead-space (how many humans must approve/filter). This suggests we should measure the *cost transfer*, not assume the problem is solved.

## Candidate laws or signals

**CL-Humboldt-A:** *Determinism-Assumption Binding*: Governance maturity frameworks are coupled to assumptions of process repeatability. When applied to constitutively non-deterministic systems, they do not reduce variance; they formalize the gatekeeping required to hide it.
