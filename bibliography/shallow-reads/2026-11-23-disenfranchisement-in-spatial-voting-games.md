## What this is

A game-theoretic analysis of "franchise elections"—voting systems where sitting members vote to alter the electorate itself—with focus on how ideological or issue diversity affects stability of inclusion/exclusion boundaries. The work treats voting rights as a recursive self-stability problem: the arbiters of membership are drawn from the membership itself.

## What I took from it

The paper localizes a real coordination problem: when the boundary of a voting protocol is endogenously set by participants within that protocol, what prevents collapse into stable but arbitrary equilibria? This maps clearly to L-010 (Coordination Adoption Nonmonotonicity), where adoption decisions depend on what others adopt—here, disenfranchisement decisions depend on who else remains enfranchised.

However, the execution appears to be classical game theory applied to a spatial voting model, not a study of how such systems actually ossify, degrade, or produce unintended protocol effects under real adoption pressure. The abstract suggests the paper is asking *which equilibria are stable*, not *how do protocol systems behave when boundary rules are legible and optimizable*. This is an important clarification problem, but it does not appear to present a mechanism absent from L-010, L-012, L-014, or L-023 (all of which address legibility-driven optimization, boundary concentration, and informality collapse). The paper also does not appear to generalize beyond spatial voting to other protocol domains.

## Research connections

- **L-010:** Coordination Adoption Nonmonotonicity maps directly—franchise elections create a self-referential adoption condition where enfranchisement depends on other agents' enfranchisement status.
- **L-014:** Strategic Boundary Concentration Under Computable Legality—when membership rules are made formally decidable, optimizing agents may concentrate exclusion at protocol boundaries.
- **seed-456:** Self-referential voting boundary stability is the core object; this paper may provide equilibrium characterization.
