## What this is

A proposal that protocol systems can be axiomatically constructed without causality as a primitive, instead treating causality as a derived property of state-slice measurement and temporal ordering.

## What I took from it

This idea challenges a deep assumption in how we model artificial systems: that causal ordering must be fundamental. The claim suggests instead that protocols operate on *state observations at discrete moments*, and that what we call "causality" is a narrative or inferential pattern we impose when comparing those states across time slices.

This is genuinely novel relative to the current inventory (which is minimal). It opens a question about what *does* serve as the primitive organizing principle if not causality—is it constraint satisfaction? State consistency? Measurement compatibility? The idea identifies a gap but doesn't fill it. That gap is valuable: it suggests that moving causality to the emergent layer might clarify protocol semantics, but requires a concrete mechanic. Without that, it reads as a reorientation proposal rather than a law.
