## What this is

Critical policy document analysis applying a pedagogically-grounded framework to Ghana's National AI Strategy (2025–2035). The work examines how national AI strategies frame education, teacher agency, curriculum design, and implementation, with attention to cultural and ethical dimensions often absent from technocratic strategy documents.

## What I took from it

This is a *policy reception* study, not a systems-level investigation of protocol behavior. It documents how formal strategy documents *present* AI adoption narratives to institutions (schools, teacher-training systems, labor markets) rather than how those institutions *internalize or resist* those narratives under adoption pressure.

The triage flagged L-001 (Protocol Ossification Under Adoption Pressure) as potentially relevant—the reasoning being that national strategies create adoption pressure that ossifies institutional knowledge. However, this paper does not investigate the *mechanism* of that ossification or measure resistance/adaptation over time. It performs a snapshot analysis of framing, not a longitudinal study of institutional response or norm formalization under stress. The paper is valuable for documenting *how national protocols are rhetorically constructed*, but it does not generate evidence for whether formal adoption pressure produces the predicted ossification dynamics.

## Method note

This work exemplifies a critical gap in the research behavior graph: policy-frame analysis and institutional-response analysis are often separated. Strong meta-research practice would embed longitudinal institutional observation *within* policy document analysis, capturing not just how strategies are framed but how adoption pressure reshapes the cognitive and normative baseline of the institutions that receive them. Critical policy analysis is a necessary upstream lens, but alone it cannot generate claims about protocol dynamics—it requires downstream observation of institutional behavior under adoption pressure.
