## What this is

The claim that technological change does not produce gradual, continuous drift in human institutional and social protocols, but instead triggers rapid reorganizations punctuated by stable intervals—importing Eldredge & Gould's model from evolutionary biology into protocol systems.

## What I took from it

This is a useful temporal framing that challenges a default assumption: that protocols adapt smoothly to technological opportunity. The punctuated equilibrium model suggests instead that protocols remain sticky, resistant to change until technological pressure exceeds some threshold, then reorganize rapidly, then restabilize. This opens a substantive question about *why* protocols are sticky—friction, path dependence, coordination costs, institutional inertia—and predicts that we should observe clustering of protocol changes around technological moments rather than distribution across time.

The idea is distinct from simple "adoption curves" (which describe individual uptake) and adds a structural claim: that *systems of protocols* reorganize together, not piecemeal. This is a genuine addition to the inventory if true, and if measurable. However, it risks becoming a restatement of known dynamics (e.g., institutional lock-in, critical junctures, punctuated institutional change in political science) unless formalized with novel predictive content specific to *artificial* and *protocolized* systems.
