## What this is

A game-theoretic paper extending sequential deliberation mechanisms by introducing reference-point anchoring. The authors model how current outcomes (drafts, proposals, status quo) exert social influence on participant behavior beyond their role as disagreement points, analyzing convergence and stability properties under this revised framework.

## What I took from it

The paper sits at the intersection of L-006 (coordination cost conservation) and L-010 (adoption nonmonotonicity) but does not substantially advance either. It demonstrates that anchoring effects predictably shift convergence outcomes in sequential revision, but this is a refinement of mechanism behavior within a single bounded domain, not evidence for a law that generalizes across protocol types or layers.

The work confirms that reference points matter—unsurprising—but does not establish how coordination cost is *conserved* across transitions, nor does it explore the nonmonotonic adoption dynamics that L-010 specifies (where intermediate adoption levels destabilize equilibrium). The anchoring mechanism is localized to belief/preference expression within a single deliberation protocol; it does not demonstrate the migration or redistribution of coordination burden that would trigger either law.

## Research connections

- **L-006:** The paper does not address whether anchoring effects displace coordination cost to other layers or interaction forms; it only models shift within a single mechanism.
- **L-010:** No exploration of adoption threshold effects, cascading behavior, or the nonmonotonic inflection points that characterize L-010.
- **seed-147:** Mentioned in triage but not accessible; if it concerns reference-point dynamics in sequential protocols, this paper provides narrow empirical support but no generalizing mechanism.
