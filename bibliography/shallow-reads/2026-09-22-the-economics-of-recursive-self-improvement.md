## What this is

An economic model of recursive self-improvement in AI systems using directed graph representations of feedback loops and elasticity products to predict capability acceleration. The work distinguishes "narrow" optimization (benchmark improvement) from "broad" capability gain, modeling conditions under which RSI becomes economically plausible.

## What I took from it

The paper formalizes feedback structure but does not establish a primary empirical or theoretical argument about protocol behavior under optimization pressure—it models *conditions* for RSI plausibility rather than *mechanisms* by which optimization pressure distorts protocols. The narrow/broad distinction is analytically useful but does not challenge or extend the current laws. The work confirms that optimization pressure can be formally modeled (supporting L-004 and L-008 intuitions) but does not provide new mechanism evidence or cross-domain generalization. The elasticity framework is mechanically sound but remains internal to the economic model; it does not expose how formalized self-improvement metrics themselves become coordination surfaces or distortion loci in the manner of L-003 (formalization ratchet) or seed-150 (formalization as paradigm lock).
