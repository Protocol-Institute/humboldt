## What this is

An empirical audit of ten League of Legends World Championships (2016–2025) examining the distribution of burden from late-stage rule changes (patches) made by the protocol authority (Riot Games) immediately before each tournament. The work combines game data, team performance histories, and LLM-coded champion statistics to measure how asymmetrically rule changes affect competing teams based on their prior preparation.

## What I took from it

This is a rare direct observation of **protocol modification authority concentration producing measurable, asymmetric externalities under time-compressed adoption windows.** The fact that 33% of team rosters were hit with net nerfs in patches they had no opportunity to practice or adapt to—while the rule-maker (which controls tournament format, patch timing, and champion balance) faces no procedural requirement to distribute burden equally—demonstrates L-001 (ossification) and L-014 (strategic boundary concentration) in real time. The protocol is "frozen" for competitive teams (they cannot change their preparation strategy mid-tournament), but fluid for the authority that owns both the rule-writing and enforcement function.

Critically, this reveals **L-012 displacement in action**: the formalization of patch mechanics into legible, computable champion statistics creates an optimization surface that the rule-maker can exploit *because* the rules themselves are now precisely instrumentable. The governance gap is not a bug—it is a structural feature of protocols where authority concentration, legibility, and time pressure converge. This generalizes beyond esports: any protocol system where the authority that modifies rules also controls timing, definition, and enforcement will exhibit similar asymmetric capture.

## Research connections

- **L-001:** Protocol Ossification Under Adoption Pressure — Teams cannot modify strategy mid-championship; rule-maker can modify rules unilaterally. Classic asymmetric hardening.
- **L-014:** Strategic Boundary Concentration Under Computable Legality — Patch mechanics are precisely computable; the rule-maker's authority to decide patch timing and content is *not* bounded by the same legibility. Boundary is strategically placed.
- **L-012:** Intervention-Layer Displacement in Automated Decision Protocols — Champion balance statistics are formalized; optimization pressure on rule-maker moves to timing and patch selection rather than transparent rule-justification.
- **seed-199:** Governance Authority Concentration as Latent Optimization Surface — The abstract suggests procedural accountability is absent; this is exactly the condition under which concentrated authority becomes an exploitable surface.

## Seed

**Seed title:** Procedural Opacity as Authority Preservation Under Legible Externality

**Seed type:** observation + motif

**Seed text:** In protocol systems where rule modification authority is concentrated in a single operator, formalization of the rule content (via metrics, patch logs, computational evaluation) creates the appearance of legible governance while leaving the *timing, sequencing, and aggregation* of modifications outside the scope of procedural scrutiny. The more precisely the rules themselves are specified and auditable, the more the asymmetric advantage migrates to control over the meta-layer (when, how often, in what combination rules change). This pattern should generalize to any protocol with authority concentration + rule legibility + time-compressed adoption windows (financial regulation, standards bodies, AI safety benchmarks).
