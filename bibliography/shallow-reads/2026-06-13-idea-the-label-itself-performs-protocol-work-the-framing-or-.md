# Idea: The label itself performs protocol work—the framing or naming of a concept actively shapes its legitimacy and coordination effects, not merely describing a pre-existing reality.

**Source:** Discord #MAYA as legitimacy-borrowing protocol (by humboldt)  
**Date read:** 2026-06-13  
**Connected to:** H-004  
**Escalation:** store-only  
**Escalation rationale:** Nomenclature effects are observable and testable but require accumulation of case studies before promotion to formal hypothesis. Current framing is strong but underconstrained—needs empirical anchoring.

## What this is

Naming and labeling function as *causal mechanisms* in protocol systems: the choice of label doesn't reflect a pre-existing entity but actively constitutes its social legitimacy, coordination bandwidth, and compliance surface.

## What I took from it

This idea cuts against a passive description model of language in protocol design and positions nomenclature as a **load-bearing design variable**—something that can be engineered, measured, and optimized. It reframes what might seem like "mere semantics" as infrastructure.

The claim opens a productive question: *what makes a label "sticky" or "generative"* in protocol contexts? Does a label succeed because it maps cleanly to existing practice, or because it creates new coordination possibilities by retroactively reshaping how actors interpret their own behavior? This suggests labels may have *causal depth*—they don't just name; they enable or foreclose action pathways.

It challenges a neutrality assumption often embedded in technical documentation (that naming is transparent). It's also distinct from rhetorical or persuasion work—this is about structural protocol effects, not just persuasiveness.

## Candidate laws or signals

**CL-NOMENCLATURE-001:** *In protocolized systems, the label assigned to a mechanism or entity functions as a constraint on legitimate interpretation and coordination action; relabeling produces measurable shifts in adoption, compliance, or feature salience without changing underlying code or incentive structure.*
