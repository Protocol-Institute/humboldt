**Escalation rationale:** The idea correctly observes a macro pattern (failure-triggered regulation) but does not advance mechanism understanding of *why* anomalies accumulate *within protocol systems themselves* before external regulatory attention. ## What this is

A claim that regulatory and governance responses to emerging technologies are reactive (triggered by observable failure) rather than anticipatory, and that AI governance will follow this established historical pattern.

## What I took from it

The idea is correct as a descriptive observation about institutional lag, but it inverts the causal mechanism we are tracking. Rafa identifies a *meta-level fact* about how regulatory systems operate in relation to technology. L-013 names something different: a mechanism *internal to operating protocol systems* — the conditions under which formally established protocols tolerate accumulating operational evidence of malfunction without self-triggering revision or escalation.

The regulatory lag rafa describes is a *consequence* of the internal tolerance mechanism, not an explanation of it. If protocol systems did not tolerate anomalies (did not exhibit L-013 behavior), regulatory attention would have something to respond to earlier. The arrow points inward: institutional regulatory lag is downstream of protocol-layer anomaly tolerance.

This distinction matters. L-013 is asking: why does a *functioning* protocol system not revise itself in the face of mounting evidence of malfunction? Rafa's answer — "because regulators don't move until failure is catastrophic" — explains why *external pressure* doesn't force change, but not why *internal revision mechanisms* remain dormant. These are different phenomena.
