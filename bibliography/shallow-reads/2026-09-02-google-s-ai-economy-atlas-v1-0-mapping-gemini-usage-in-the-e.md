# Google's AI & Economy ATLAS v1.0: Mapping Gemini Usage in the Economy

## What this is

A large-scale empirical mapping of AI tool usage across economic activities, occupations, and geographies using de-identified Gemini interaction data. The work catalogs usage patterns but does not advance a sustained theoretical argument about protocol dynamics, ossification, or coordination failure.

## What I took from it

The ATLAS dataset is valuable as a legibility-production instrument — it renders diffuse AI adoption visible and classifiable at scale. However, the paper itself appears to be a resource contribution and observational survey rather than a mechanistic investigation. The connection to L-004 (Goodhart Generalization) and L-006 (Coordination Cost Conservation) is suggestive but undeveloped: the paper documents *that* AI adoption is heterogeneous across tasks and occupations, but does not examine whether the act of measurement and classification itself creates incentive distortions or shifts coordination costs between protocol layers.

The metadata richness (800 occupations, 4000 tasks, 300 household activities) is the core contribution, but without a hypothesis about how classification drives behavior change, metric capture, or protocol evolution, this remains a cartography rather than a mechanism study.

## Research connections

- **L-004:** Usage mapping makes AI labor substitution measurable across occupations; the legibility itself could become an optimization target for workers, managers, or policy, but this mechanism is not explored.
- **L-006:** The paper could address whether AI adoption shifts coordination costs *within* occupational workflows vs. *between* them, but does not frame the question this way.
- **seed-080 (Proxy Collapse Under Upstream Asymmetry):** Task classification could function as a proxy for actual labor displacement; misalignment between category and outcome is unexamined.
