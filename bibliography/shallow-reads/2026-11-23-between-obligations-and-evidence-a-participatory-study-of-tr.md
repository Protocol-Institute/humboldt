## What this is

A participatory workshop study (n=32) examining how regulatory obligations from the EU AI Act are interpreted and translated into measurable technical requirements across three applied domains (healthcare, recruitment, public transport). The work documents the interpretive choices, evidence standards, and contextual divergences that emerge when formal regulatory language meets engineering practice.

## What I took from it

This is a direct empirical observation of **L-003 (Formalization Ratchet)** in action — the moment when informal regulatory intent ("ensure fairness," "mitigate risks") encounters computable enforcement demands and splits into incommensurate technical operationalizations. The critical finding appears to be that there is no unique mapping from obligation to evidence; practitioners across the same domain diverge substantially in what counts as compliance proof.

This surfaces an upstream problem for L-008 (Proxy Optimization Under Computable Enforcement) and L-012 (Intervention-Layer Displacement): the proxy itself is contested and unstable at the moment of formalization. The study suggests that formalization doesn't merely create optimization targets — it first requires resolving deep disagreement about what the target *is*. This may imply that formalization ratchet effects are preceded by a hidden "interpretation ratchet" where early definitional choices foreclose later contestation.

Methodologically, the work is significant because it treats regulatory translation as an empirical phenomenon rather than a logical problem, capturing the social and epistemic work that precedes legible optimization pressure.

## Research connections

- **L-003:** Direct observation of formalization under regulatory pressure; documents how informal norms (fairness obligations) encounter demands for computable proof.
- **L-004:** Implicit risk — once technical proxies for regulatory compliance are chosen and operationalized, they become targets for metric capture.
- **L-012:** Shows the displacement happening at the boundary between obligation and legible input; different practitioners place the intervention layer at different points.

## Method note

This work demonstrates that **the site of law emergence is not the final protocol, but the interpretive negotiation before formalization locks in.** Shallow reads of regulatory compliance should flag participatory/ethnographic studies that capture the branching points in translation, because they reveal which definitions become canonical through early adoption or institutional authority rather than through logical necessity. Future research on protocol ossification should include pre-formalization studies of interpretive divergence — the evidence is there that some choices foreclose others, but we need systematic observation of *when and why* contestation is absorbed into canonical form.
