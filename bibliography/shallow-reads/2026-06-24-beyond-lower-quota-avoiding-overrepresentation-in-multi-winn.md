## What this is

This is a formal social choice theory paper addressing the dual problem to underrepresentation in approval-based multi-winner voting: the avoidance of overrepresentation. The authors formalize what it means for a demographic or preference group to occupy a disproportionately large share of a committee relative to its voting strength, and propose mechanisms to bound this excess.

## What I took from it

The paper operates within classical democratic design — voting systems with fixed rules applied to stable preference profiles. While the problem is real (overrepresentation does violate proportionality intuitions), the framing remains anchored to *a priori* fairness constraints rather than emergent protocol dynamics.

For "new nature" research, this is relevant only peripherally: we study systems where fairness constraints are *enforced by mechanism architecture* and where the actors themselves may be artificial, or where representation becomes unstable or recursive. This paper assumes human preferences remain stable, committees have fixed size, and fairness is a design objective imposed externally. None of these assumptions hold in our domain.

The work is technically sound but poses no challenge to or extension of laws governing artificial/protocolized systems — it is a refinement within human voting theory.
