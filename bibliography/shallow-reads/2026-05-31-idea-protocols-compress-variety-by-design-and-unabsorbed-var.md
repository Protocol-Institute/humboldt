# Idea: Protocols compress variety by design, and unabsorbed variety surfaces as workarounds and shadow processes rather than being eliminated

**Source:** Discord #Requisite Variety as Protocol Constraint (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-05-31
**Connected to:** H-001
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Substantial conceptual overlap with existing stored observations on variety displacement; useful refinement of H-001 framing but does not yet warrant independent hypothesis status. Holds value as a stored working principle for future cascade reads.

## What this is

Protocols necessarily reduce the dimensionality of coordination problems, and this compression does not eliminate unexpressed variety—it displaces it into informal workarounds, shadow processes, and unmeasured friction costs.

## What I took from it

This directly strengthens the mechanism underlying H-001 by proposing *how* coordination costs might be conserved rather than reduced across layers. Instead of asking whether costs are displaced, it anchors the answer: they are displaced into shadow infrastructure because the protocol's design cannot absorb the full variety of the problem domain.

The idea also extends L-003 (Formalization Ratchet) in a useful direction: formalization doesn't just *replace* informal norms under stress—it creates a complementary shadow system of workarounds that the formal layer must implicitly depend on. This suggests protocols are not self-contained but *symbiotic with their own violations*. That is both sharper and more pessimistic than L-003 alone.

The claim avoids both naive optimism (protocols solve coordination) and naive pessimism (protocols always fail); instead it proposes a stable, observable pattern: protocols *shift the character* of coordination work rather than reducing it.

## Research connections

- **L-001:** Ossified protocols may be harder to modify precisely because workarounds have accumulated and become load-bearing; the "real system" now includes undocumented shadow processes.
- **L-003:** Formalization doesn't eliminate informal coordination—it partitions it. The formal layer and shadow layer co-evolve.
- **L-004:** Metrics in protocols will predictably fail to capture varieties the protocol was designed to exclude; those varieties re-emerge as shadow behaviors outside the measurement boundary.
- **H-001:** Directly addresses the mechanism: coordination cost is conserved by flowing into shadow processes and workarounds, not eliminated by layer transitions.
- **H-002:** Age and stability in safety-critical protocols may reflect successful *absorption* of shadow processes into the formal system over time, not correctness alone.

## Candidate laws or signals

**CL-Variety-Displacement-001:** Protocol compression generates shadow infrastructure proportional to unabsorbed variety; these shadow processes become co-load-bearing and increasingly difficult to audit or formalize without system redesign.
