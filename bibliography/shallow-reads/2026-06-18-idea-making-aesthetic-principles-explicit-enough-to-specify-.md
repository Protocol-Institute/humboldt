# Idea: Making aesthetic principles explicit enough to specify reveals they were vaguer

## What this is

The act of formalizing implicit aesthetic or design constraints does not merely *clarify* them—it exposes their original vagueness and thereby changes the meaning of what was being constrained.

## What I took from it

This idea deepens H-001 by identifying a *paradoxical effect* of formalization: that precision does not simply reveal what was always there, but retroactively destabilizes the category itself. The claim suggests that aesthetic principles (and likely other latent protocol constraints) operate in a kind of productive ambiguity—they *work* because they are not fully explicit. When we formalize them, we do not recover a hidden determinacy; we create a new object whose relationship to the original is recursive and unstable.

This opens a critical line of inquiry: **formalization as transformation, not translation.** It challenges the assumption that protocols or aesthetic rules pre-exist in some canonical form waiting to be written down. Instead, the act of writing creates a new constraint space, one that may be more rigid or reveal previously invisible trade-offs.
