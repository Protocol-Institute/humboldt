## What this is

A game-theoretic study of strategic behavior by a storage aggregator in day-ahead electricity markets with network constraints. The paper models how a monopolistic actor with distributed assets can exploit the legibility and computability of constraint-binding patterns (transmission rights, nodal prices, multi-period dispatch) to extract rent through coordinated bids that manipulate market-clearing outcomes.

## What I took from it

The paper demonstrates a domain-specific instance of L-014 (Strategic Boundary Concentration Under Computable Legality): the ESA's advantage emerges precisely *because* the system operator's constraints are rendered fully computable and legible—nodal pricing, transmission constraints, economic dispatch schedules are all formalized in a way that permits exact optimization against them. The storage aggregator is not violating the rules; it is exploiting the boundary between what is legible to optimization and what the rule-setter intended to protect.

This is a localized, well-controlled case, but it does not generalize to a novel mechanism. The strategic manipulation relies on information asymmetry and first-mover advantage in a specific institutional context (energy markets with financial transmission rights). It does not reveal a principle about how computable obligations *generally* concentrate optimization pressure at their boundaries, nor does it engage the deeper question of why formalization creates strategic surfaces rather than merely constraining them.

## Research connections

- **L-014:** Confirms the basic pattern—computable legible obligations create exploitable boundaries—but in a narrow domain (energy markets) without cross-domain generalization or mechanistic depth.
- **seed-144:** Tangentially relevant: informality as refuge under substitution pressure. The paper does not explore whether informal coordination (bilateral contracts, non-transparent storage dispatch) emerges as an escape route; it only models the formal market.
