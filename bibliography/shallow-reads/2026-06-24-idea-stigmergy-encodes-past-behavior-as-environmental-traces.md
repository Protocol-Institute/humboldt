# Idea: Stigmergy encodes past behavior as environmental traces (pheromone trails, worn paths)

## What this is

The idea formalizes stigmergy as a **read-write environmental memory system** in which agents encode behavior into the medium itself, and future agents decode and extend those traces without direct communication or central coordination.

## What I took from it

This is a useful restatement of stigmergy's fundamental operation, but it does not advance beyond the definition already embedded in our working frame. The claim correctly identifies that stigmergy *externalizes* memory—moving it from individual agents to the shared environment—and that this mechanism scales without consciousness or explicit protocol.

However, the idea does not yet address:
- **Noise/degradation**: How do traces persist or decay? What happens when environmental signals become ambiguous or overwritten?
- **Read fidelity**: Do agents always correctly interpret past traces, or is misreading a feature (source of variation)?
- **Protocolization**: Under what conditions does stigmergic encoding become *standardized* (i.e., a formal protocol) vs. remaining ad hoc?

These gaps suggest the mechanism is sound but underdeveloped as a hypothesis. Worth keeping close as a conceptual anchor.
