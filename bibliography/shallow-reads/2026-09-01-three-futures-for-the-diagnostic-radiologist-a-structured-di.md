## What this is

A structured disagreement paper in which three domain experts independently forecast the diagnostic radiologist role in 2035 under AI adoption pressure. The work does not present a sustained theoretical argument or primary empirical data; instead, it surfaces divergence in expert sense-making about institutional role transformation under technological change.

## What I took from it

The paper's value lies in its *epistemic structure* rather than its substantive claims. By formalizing disagreement among domain insiders, it makes visible the exact points where institutional interpretation diverges—which is precisely where L-013 (paradigm-locked anomaly tolerance) and L-015 (interpretive continuity decay) operate. The three futures likely differ on *which signals count as evidence that the role is changing*, revealing how established protocols (radiologist training, certification, workflow design) can tolerate contradictory forecasts simultaneously without triggering institutional revision.

This is not a primary source for *why roles change under AI*; it is a primary source for *how institutions maintain coherence despite divergent understandings of change*. The structured disagreement method itself may be a useful instrument for detecting where formalization breaks down and informal sense-making persists—a methodological signal that L-015 (interpretive continuity decay) is empirically present.

## Research connections

- **L-013:** The paper exemplifies how established protocol systems (radiology practice, credentialing, workflow) tolerate accumulating ambiguity about role transformation without systematic institutional response, suggesting anomaly tolerance is paradigm-locked rather than empirically driven.
- **L-015:** The explicit divergence among domain experts suggests formal records (job descriptions, competency frameworks) may survive and propagate while institutional understanding of what those records *mean* decays unevenly across stakeholder groups.
- **seed-015 (C-015-taming-as-political-act):** The act of writing three explicit job descriptions is itself a taming intervention; the disagreement reveals whose framing becomes protocol and whose becomes residual.
- **seed-027 (C-027-planck-principle-institutional-memory):** Institutional memory of radiologist identity may persist longer than the functional justification for that identity, creating a lag that the three futures expose.

## Method note

Structured expert disagreement can serve as a *diagnostic instrument* for detecting where informal coordination norms have not yet ossified into explicit protocol. The fact that three insiders produce three futures suggests the institution has not yet formalized its theory of role change—and this gap is where L-013 and L-015 operate. Future research should collect such structured disagreements *before* they resolve into consensus, as the moment of resolution itself (or its failure) reveals which framings become legible to institutional decision-making and which become invisible.
