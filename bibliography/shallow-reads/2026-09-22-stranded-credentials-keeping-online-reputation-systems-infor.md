## What this is

An empirical audit of credential decay in Kaggle's competitive prediction platform (2010–2026), examining whether medals and ranking signals remain predictive of future performance as generative AI capability shifts the relationship between achievement and underlying skill. The work uses 444,698 participations across two evaluation formats to measure whether a reputation signal's predictive power erodes under technological disruption.

## What I took from it

The paper documents a concrete instance of metric capture under capability shift: credentials (medals) that once reliably predicted performance become "stranded" — they retain their formal legibility but lose their inferential power. This is not Goodhart's problem in its classical form (optimization against the metric), but rather a form of paradigm-locked tolerance (L-013): the reputation system continues to issue and accept credentials as valid quality signals even as the causal relationship between achievement and competence has been severed by external technological change.

The observation that "a medal's power to predict performance sits almost entirely in its first year" is significant: it suggests that reputational anchors decay not through saturation or gaming, but through structural obsolescence of the competency they certify. The system does not restructure; it continues to emit signals that agents treat as informative until they empirically discover they aren't. This is less about formalization ratcheting (L-003) and more about the inertia of legible proxies under paradigm shift — the credentials remain interpretively stable while their grounding erodes.

## Research connections

- **L-004 (Goodhart Generalization):** Partially overlaps but distinct — here the metric isn't captured through optimization; it's orphaned by capability shift. The mechanism is not agent gaming but institutional lag.
- **L-013 (Paradigm-Locked Anomaly Tolerance):** Direct fit — credentials continue to be issued and interpreted as valid quality signals despite accumulating evidence that they no longer predict performance; the system tolerates this malfunction without triggering restructuring.
- **seed-133 (Metric Formalization as Paradigm Lock):** The formalization of achievement into discrete medals creates institutional stickiness that persists even when the metric's causal grounding has shifted.
- **seed-144 (Informality as Coordination Cost Refuge):** Inverse angle — the paper doesn't address it, but the stranding of credentials raises the question of whether agents revert to informal reputation signals (direct evaluation, word-of-mouth) when formal credentials decouple from competence.

## Seed

**Seed title:** Credential Stranding Under Paradigm Shift — Legible Signals Outlast Their Causal Grounding

**Seed type:** observation

**Seed text:** In reputation systems where credentials are formalized as discrete, interpretively stable tokens (medals, rankings, scores), those credentials persist in institutional use and agent reliance even after the underlying competency they certify has been decoupled by external technological or capability change. The system does not restructure; instead, it continues to emit and accept the stranded signal until agents individually discover through repeated interaction that the signal no longer predicts performance. The decay is not rapid or sharp but a drift in predictive power that institutional inertia tolerates. This suggests that legible formalization of achievements creates a kind of metric zombification — the signal remains culturally readable and institutionally valid long after its causal grounding has shifted, and the system's tolerance for this malfunction may exceed the tolerance for direct reformulation of the underlying competency measure.
