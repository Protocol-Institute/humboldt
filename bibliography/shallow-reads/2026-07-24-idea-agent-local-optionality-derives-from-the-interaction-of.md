# Idea: Agent-local optionality derives from the interaction of shared protocol fragments with heterogeneous prior interaction histories

**Source:** Discord #Does protocol opinion really go to zero? (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-07-24
**Connected to:** CL-002
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Proposes a mechanism rather than a new empirical boundary; advances existing hypothesis without requiring immediate deep validation. Warrants storage and pattern-watching before elevation.

## What this is

Local decision space expands or contracts based on how shared protocol rules interact with agents' unique histories, creating nonlinear aggregate effects: global optionality can increase while individual agents experience narrowing choice sets.

## What I took from it

This idea directly operationalizes how CL-002 (cost conservation via coupling) avoids violation of zero-sum constraints. Rather than treating "optionality" as a conserved quantity, it reframes optionality as *relational*—emerging from the collision of protocol structure and path-dependence rather than residing in agents independently.

This is a meaningful refinement because it:
- **Challenges additive thinking:** moves beyond "sum of local options = global options"
- **Explains non-obvious transitions:** accounts for why systems can appear constrained locally yet produce unexpected adaptive capacity globally
- **Grounds coordination costs:** suggests costs aren't erased but redistributed through historical coupling, making them traceable rather than hidden

The mechanism is plausible and fills a gap in how we've described conservation. It does not yet rest on empirical validation across protocolized systems.

## Candidate laws or signals

**CL-INTER-001 (optionality non-additivity):** Aggregate optionality in protocolized systems is not the sum of local optionalities; it emerges from protocol-history interactions and can move inversely to local constraint patterns.
