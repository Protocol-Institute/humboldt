## What this is

A blog essay applying systems theory and economic frameworks to analyze how informal coordination norms (chore assignment, task distribution) become codified into explicit "protocols" within commune living arrangements. The piece appears to treat communes as naturalistic laboratories for protocol emergence, drawing on a domain far removed from cryptographic or computational systems.

## What I took from it

The analysis likely documents L-003 (Formalization Ratchet) in real time: as commune size or internal conflict increases, ad-hoc social norms about who does what crystallize into formal systems—possibly with measurement, verification, and enforcement mechanisms. This is valuable because it tests whether formalization pressure is *domain-universal* or merely a feature of technical systems under scaling stress.

The relevance annotation also flags L-005 (Gall Generalization). If communes are long-functioning coordination systems, examining whether they resist or accommodate restructuring of their chore protocols could either validate or nuance the claim that working systems cannot be redesigned from scratch. There is uncertainty here: the post may only describe protocol emergence, not resistance to change.

## Research connections

- **L-003:** Direct case study of informal norm → formal protocol transition under coordination scaling pressure in a non-technical domain.
- **L-001:** If communes undergo adoption/stabilization cycles with their chore systems, ossification dynamics may be observable in miniature.
- **L-005:** Potential empirical test: do long-stable commune chore systems resist redesign, and if so, by what mechanism?
- **H-001:** Communes might offer a lower-complexity domain to test whether coordination cost is conserved when protocols migrate from informal to formal encoding.

## Candidate laws or signals

- **Protocol Formalization Triggers:** What specific thresholds (group size, conflict incidents, resource scarcity) cause informal norms to crystallize into written/enforced protocols? Communes may show clearer phase transitions than technical systems.
- **Metric Capture in Social Protocols:** Chore assignment systems almost certainly use proxies (hours worked, tasks completed, rotation schedules) for unmeasurable goals (fairness, care, sustainability). Watch for Goodhart effects (L-004) evidence.
