# Idea: Many protocols exist in quasi-standardized but non-formalized states, governed by norms and best practices rather than explicit rules

**Source:** Discord #new-nature (by _vgr)
**Date read:** 2026-05-29
**Connected to:** L-001, L-003
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** The observation describes a staging pattern already explained by existing laws. It identifies *when* formalization occurs (under pressure) and *what precedes it* (informal norms), but does not isolate a new independent mechanism or yield testable predictions distinct from L-001 and L-003.

## What this is

The claim that protocols frequently stabilize through informal consensus and best-practice normalization *before* or *instead of* explicit formalization, suggesting the ratio of formal to informal governance components is itself a meaningful structural variable.

## What I took from it

The idea correctly observes that many mature protocols (DNS conventions, HTTP semantics, Git workflows) operate under hybrid formal-informal regimes. However, this is already predicted by L-003 (Formalization Ratchet): the hypothesis states that formalization occurs *under stress, conflict, or scaling pressure*—which means the preceding state is necessarily informal. The quasi-standardized norm state is the default equilibrium before ratcheting.

The idea hints at something potentially deeper: whether the *ratio* itself has independent predictive power (e.g., whether high informal-to-formal ratio predicts vulnerability to drift or capture). But the idea does not isolate what would make that ratio causally primary versus derivative of adoption maturity and external pressure. It refines observation but does not yet constitute a mechanism.

## Research connections

- **L-001:** Ossification describes why protocols harden under adoption; this idea describes the pre-ossification state (informal norms), which is consistent but not novel.
- **L-003:** The Formalization Ratchet explicitly predicts the informal→formal transition; this idea observes the informal state exists, confirming the ratchet's premise.
- **H-001:** Raises a latent question: does coordination cost vary with the formal-informal ratio? Worth tracking.
