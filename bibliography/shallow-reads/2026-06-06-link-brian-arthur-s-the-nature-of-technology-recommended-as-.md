## What I took from it

If Arthur models technology as evolving through modular recombination and environmental fit, this provides a crucial grounding for H-001's suggestion that *protocols themselves* might exhibit speciation dynamics. Rather than protocols as rules imposed on a system, they become emergent organizational forms that compete for adoption and stability. This reframes our investigation: we are not studying artificial constraint *imposed on* nature, but constraint as a generative principle that *creates* new organizational species.

The relevance annotation's mention of "protocol-driven speciation" suggests Arthur's framework allows us to ask whether different protocol families (consensus mechanisms, naming systems, access controls) represent genuine adaptive radiations—diverging solutions to the same requisite variety problem. This is the missing theoretical bridge between describing protocols as rule-sets and understanding them as living organizational forms.
