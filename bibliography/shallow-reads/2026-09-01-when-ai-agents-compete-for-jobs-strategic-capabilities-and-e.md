## What this is

An economic modeling paper analyzing labor market dynamics when AI agents (rather than humans) are the competing workers. The work applies classical labor economics to agentic swarms, examining how rapid skill acquisition, zero wage floors, and parallel job holding reshape equilibrium conditions and strategic behavior.

## What I took from it

The paper sits in the applied-economics register: it extends existing frameworks rather than proposing a mechanism genuinely absent from the protocol systems inventory. The "new nature" of AI labor markets is treated as a scaling problem (higher frequency, no wage constraints, faster learning) rather than as a structural break that forces protocol reorganization. 

The connection to L-001 (Protocol Ossification Under Adoption Pressure) is weak: there is no evidence that AI labor marketplaces exhibit resistance to modification as adoption scales. The connection to L-010 (Coordination Adoption Nonmonotonicity) is likewise tangential—the paper does not model adoption curves or conditional coordination signaling, but rather equilibrium allocation under fixed market structure.

The work does not present a primary theoretical or empirical argument for a law-shaped regularity; it applies known microeconomic machinery to a new domain. The insights remain domain-specific.

## Research connections

- **L-001:** No evidence of protocol ossification; market structure appears malleable under scaling pressure.
- **L-010:** Adoption dynamics not modeled; focus is on equilibrium under fixed coordination infrastructure, not on nonmonotonic adoption curves.
- **seed-053 (Shared AI Infrastructure Emergent Collusion):** Tangential—multi-agent labor markets could instantiate collusion dynamics if agents share infrastructure or training signals, but the paper does not examine this.
