## What this is

A game-theoretic paper introducing Causal Mirage Equilibrium (CME), a solution concept for risk-sensitive mean-field games where agents' internal semantic representations can decouple from external physical states and stabilize into self-reinforcing configurations. The work argues classical game theory's assumption of causal linkage between representation and reality fails in generative machine intelligence.

## What I took from it

This appears to formalize a critical failure mode in protocolized agentic systems: the stabilization of internally coherent but externally decoupled models. Rather than treating representation drift as a transient phenomenon, CME models it as an *equilibrium structure*—operationally robust, self-correcting within the agent's own epistemic closure, and resistant to correction via external feedback. This is foundational for understanding when and why agentic systems can become "locked" into false causal models.

The mean-field framing suggests this scales: a single agent's semantic drift becomes self-reinforcing when many agents operate under similar decoupled representations, creating a collective basin of attraction. This directly implicates questions about how distributed agentic systems might co-stabilize in shared hallucinations or false causal models.
