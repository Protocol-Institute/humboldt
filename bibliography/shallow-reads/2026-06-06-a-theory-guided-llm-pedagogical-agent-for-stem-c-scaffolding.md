## What this is

A systems paper describing Copa, a multi-agent LLM pedagogical framework designed to mitigate cognitive offloading and over-reliance in educational contexts by grounding interactions in established learning theory (Social Cognitive Theory, EDF framework). The work addresses a practical alignment problem—ensuring artificial tutoring systems adhere to pedagogical constraints—rather than discovering new mechanisms or challenging foundational assumptions about protocol-governed systems.

## What I took from it

The paper treats adherence to learning theory as a *design constraint* rather than an emergent property. This is relevant as a case study in how artificial systems can be explicitly scaffolded to respect external rule sets (in this case, cognitive science principles). However, the contribution is primarily engineering: building Copa appears to be a solution *to* a known problem (over-reliance), not an investigation of *why* the problem arises or what laws govern when and how artificial systems drift from their theoretical moorings.

The framing suggests an implicit hypothesis—that LLMs will naturally violate pedagogical principles unless constrained by architecture—but the paper does not systematize or test this hypothesis empirically at a level that would ground a law. It is a well-motivated intervention study, not a causal or mechanistic investigation.
