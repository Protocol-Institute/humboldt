## What this is

An economic analysis of how AI-assisted authorship changes the cost structure of scientific certification. The paper argues that when polish becomes cheap to produce but quality judgment remains costly, the information content of surface-level signals deteriorates, entry expands, the uncertified pool degrades, and certification premiums rise — creating rents for credentialing monopolists.

## What I took from it

This is a precise instantiation of **L-004 (Goodhart Generalization)** applied to peer review and journal gatekeeping: polish becomes a captured proxy for contribution value when verification cost remains sticky. The mechanism is asymmetric cost reduction — a classic **L-013 (Paradigm-Locked Anomaly Tolerance)** signature. The system tolerates the mismatch (polished-but-hollow submissions) because the institutional logic still rewards certification gatekeepers; the anomaly isn't yet treated as triggering revision of the certification protocol itself.

The core insight is structural rather than novel: it formalizes what already occurs in prestige markets. But it does clarify a second-order effect worth noting — that certification systems under asymmetric cost pressure do not become *more* permissive; they become *more extractive*. The monopolist captures the value destroyed by the new-entrant flood. This is potentially relevant to **L-006 (Coordination Cost Conservation)** if one models certification as a coordination layer: costs don't disappear; they migrate from production to filtering.

## Research connections

- **L-004:** Direct application — polish as legible proxy for quality under optimization pressure; verification cost sticky, execution cost drops.
- **L-013:** Certification systems accumulate tolerance for polish-quality mismatch without triggering protocol redesign; anomaly is absorbed as "lower signal quality" rather than institutional failure.
- **seed-018:** Revision of certification standards would implicates responsibility for prior credentialing decisions; path dependency locks in tolerance.
- **seed-026:** Tension between incommensurability (what makes a contribution *good* is not what is easily verifiable) and deformalization cost (moving away from polish as signal requires costly institutional renegotiation).

## Method note

This work illustrates a critical methodological move for research on protocolized systems: tracking what happens *between* the layers when one layer's cost structure changes. The paper does not study AI adoption directly; it studies how certification institutions *respond* to asymmetric cost shifts in their inputs. This suggests the research agenda should prioritize structural analyses of incentive redistribution under technological change, rather than comparative benchmarks of "before/after" system behavior. The meta insight is that institutions defending their gatekeeping function often rationalize dysfunction as a market correction rather than a protocol failure — this rationalization itself becomes a causal mechanism worth instrumenting.
