## What this is

Systems (biological or protocolized) can maintain surface-level compatibility in interface behavior while harboring incompatible functional constraints at deeper organizational layers, creating illusions of interoperability that fail under stress or integration.

## What I took from it

This idea functions as a concrete instantiation of L-005 (Gall Generalization) in biological systems, which strengthens the claim that the law applies across domains. The "uncanny valley" framing—where similarity breeds false confidence in compatibility—is psychologically astute but mechanically isomorphic to what L-005 already describes: apparent functional integrity masking latent structural friction.

The idea does *not* propose a new constraint or law. Instead, it demonstrates that the incompatibility masking phenomenon is domain-invariant. This is useful for generalization but does not open new questions about *how* or *why* such masking occurs, nor does it propose measurable conditions that would refine H-002 (trust accumulation) or challenge any active hypothesis.

## Research connections

- **L-005:** Direct analogy. Near-kin behavioral grammar = working system interface; divergent selection pressures = incompatible deeper architecture. Confirms Gall's principle holds across biological and artificial domains.
- **L-004:** Possible secondary connection. If behavioral similarity becomes a *metric* for functional compatibility, optimization toward that metric will mask the deeper incompatibility—a Goodhart cascade.
- **H-002:** Potential challenge. If age/stability alone drive trust in biological systems (as H-002 suggests for protocols), does near-kin similarity increase false trust *despite* functional incompatibility? Observation worth logging but not yet testable.

## Candidate laws or signals

None. This idea is already subsumed by L-005. A candidate law would require either: (1) a novel constraint on *when* surface compatibility masks functional incompatibility, or (2) a measurable mechanism for detecting such masking before failure. The idea provides neither.
