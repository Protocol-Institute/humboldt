## What this is

Laboratory experiment on serial dictatorships testing the tradeoff between menu simplicity (which removes strategic misreporting incentives) and choice complexity (which affects preference identification). Participants make incentivized choices among task bundles under two interface conditions: large menus vs. binary sequential choice. The work is a bounded domain study in mechanism design, not a primary argument about protocol laws or systems-level regularities.

## What I took from it

The paper operationalizes a tension already visible in L-004 (Goodhart) and L-019 (Representation-Rationalizability), but does not substantially extend either. It confirms that simplification regimes that solve strategic misreporting do not solve preference *elicitation* — a known duality in mechanism design. The lab setting and narrow domain (task bundle choice) do not support a claim that this pattern generalizes across protocol systems or artificial coordination environments. The finding is mechanically interesting but locally scoped: it documents a tradeoff, not a law candidate with cross-domain applicability or a novel mechanism absent from the current inventory.

## Research connections

- **L-004 (Goodhart Generalization):** The paper shows a case where simplifying the optimization target (eliminating strategic reporting) does not align outcomes with unmeasured intent (true preference), but this is a known consequence already captured in the law.
- **L-019 (Representation-Rationalizability Tradeoff):** The work illustrates preference aggregation method affecting revelation, consistent with the law's scope, but adds no new mechanism or cross-domain evidence.
- **seed-367:** Cited in triage but not examined in detail; likely refers to choice simplification and preference capture, which the paper touches but does not advance.
