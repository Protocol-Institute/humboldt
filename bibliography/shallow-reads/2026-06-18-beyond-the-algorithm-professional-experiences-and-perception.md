## What this is

A qualitative multi-case study examining how practitioners in AI/ML design, development, and governance perceive and experience bias emergence in deployed systems. The work centers lived professional experience rather than sector-specific downstream populations, using healthcare, criminal justice, employment, and education as illustrative domains.

## What I took from it

This is a phenomenological study of the **perception gap** between intended system behavior (as designed) and experienced behavior (as deployed). The framing—"beyond the algorithm"—suggests bias is not purely a function of training data or model architecture, but emerges through interaction with organizational, governance, and implementation contexts. This is valuable as a practitioner-centered account but remains primarily descriptive of the gap itself rather than formalizing the mechanisms that produce it.

The work may reveal patterns in how practitioners *recognize* or *fail to recognize* emergent behavior, which touches on observability and feedback loops in protocolized systems. However, without explicit theorization of *why* emergence occurs or *under what conditions* it generalizes, this functions more as a data collection point than as a candidate for law-formation. It documents the epistemic position of designers but does not yet constitute a sustained argument about the structure of bias emergence itself.
