# Idea: Examples of apparent acausal protocol decisions—prediction markets, retrocausal Bayesian calibration, Byzantine fault tolerance—are actually just decision-making under distributions over futures rather than true acausal access

**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Conceptual refinement of category boundaries; dissolves a claimed phenomenon rather than establishing new mechanism. Useful for scope-clarification but overlaps existing probabilistic framing. Does not yet warrant hypothesis promotion without empirical or formal demonstration that the dissolution is exhaustive.

## What this is

The claim that protocols appearing to make decisions acausally (without temporal information flow) are instead making decisions under probability measures over possible futures, thus collapsing the acausal category into standard conditional reasoning over state spaces.

## What I took from it

This idea performs important conceptual work: it contests whether "acausality" is a genuine feature of some protocols or merely a description gap—a case where we've misidentified what's happening because we've focused on the temporal appearance rather than the underlying measure structure. If correct, it relocates prediction markets, retrocausal calibration schemes, and Byzantine tolerance architectures from a novel category ("acausal decision") into the well-mapped space of Bayesian conditioning and distributional reasoning.

This is a **deflation move**: it doesn't deny the empirical behavior of these systems, but argues that their intelligibility comes from probability theory, not from any new causal topology. The challenge it poses is real—it asks whether we've been reifying a category that dissolves under careful analysis. However, the idea remains at the level of conceptual hypothesis rather than formal proof. It would be strengthened by showing: (a) a canonical translation from each "acausal" case into explicit distributional form, (b) that no residual explanatory work requires acausal framing, and (c) boundary conditions where the dissolution fails.
