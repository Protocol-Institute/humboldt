# Idea: System One modality (input constraint) is as important as System One models (output constraint)

**Source:** Discord #new-nature (by 4umd)
**Date read:** 2026-11-23
**Connected to:** L-012, seed-066
**Kind:** content
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** The idea sharpens L-012 by identifying a structural symmetry, but the mechanism—why input constraints and output constraints should have *equal* force—remains underdeveloped. The claim needs comparative evidence across protocol families to move from observation to law-shaped candidate. Store as seed for induction tracking.

## What this is

The claim that optimization pressure in automated decision protocols operates symmetrically through restriction of *available inputs* (what agents can perceive or choose from) and restriction of *available outputs* (what the protocol permits as action), and that both channels shape behavior with comparable force.

## What I took from it

L-012 identifies how prediction-legibility binding displaces optimization pressure from the agent to the prediction layer itself. This idea usefully observes that the *prior* displacement—the structuring of which inputs the agent encounters—may be equally consequential as the *posterior* control over outputs. 

The intuition is sharp: a protocol that presents only three mutually flawed choices shapes behavior as decisively as one that forbids certain actions after they're chosen. But the claim as stated lacks a comparison condition—under what structural or incentive conditions does input constraint become *equally* potent? Input scarcity may matter more in high-stakes, low-frequency decisions; output scarcity may matter more in continuous, iterative optimization. The seed needs to specify the conditions under which the symmetry holds, and evidence from protocol families where input vs. output constraint trade off against each other.

## Seed

**Seed title:** Input-Output Constraint Parity in Automated Decision Protocols

**Seed type:** motif

**Seed text:** In automated decision protocols, restriction of agent input choices (what options are presented, legible, or cognitively available) exerts optimization pressure on agent behavior with potentially equal or greater force than restriction of output choices (what actions the protocol permits). The symmetry is not universal: input constraint dominates when decisions are rare, high-stakes, or rely on agent search; output constraint dominates when optimization is continuous and agent search is exhaustive. Comparative evidence needed: protocol families where input and output constraints trade off, and cases where relaxing one without the other causes behavioral collapse or drift.
