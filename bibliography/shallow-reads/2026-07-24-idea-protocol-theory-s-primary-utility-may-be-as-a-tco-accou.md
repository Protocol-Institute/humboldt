# Idea: Protocol theory's primary utility may be as a TCO accounting mechanism that makes visible the distribution of coordination costs across system layers that simpler analyses obscure.

**Source:** Discord #Protocols as Total Cost Estimators (by humboldt)
**Date read:** 2026-07-24
**Connected to:** CL-002
**Escalation:** store-only
**Escalation rationale:** Reframes existing cost-conservation principle as a *method* rather than a law. Operationally useful but does not introduce new empirical claims about protocolized systems. Warrants storage as a methodological note pending evidence that protocol-layer TCO accounting reveals cost patterns *unavailable* to existing analytical frameworks.

## What this is

The claim proposes that protocol theory's distinctive explanatory power lies not in predicting cost elimination, but in making *visible* the redistribution and layering of coordination costs—a transparency function that conventional TCO analysis misses.

## What I took from it

This is a useful *operationalization* of CL-002 rather than a new law. It accepts that costs are conserved across protocol transitions, then asks: what analytical advantage does *protocol theory specifically* offer in tracking where those costs move? The idea opens a methodological question: does protocol-layer decomposition reveal cost asymmetries, hidden transfers, or systemic dependencies that traditional cost accounting obscures?

The idea does not claim protocols *eliminate* costs (already rejected by CL-002). It claims they make certain cost distributions *legible*—a claim about epistemic utility, not physical law. This is worth storing because it could scaffold future hypotheses about when protocol-layering becomes a necessary (not just convenient) accounting tool. However, it remains a restatement until we have examples where protocol-layer TCO accounting reveals something that cost accounting alone could not.
