## What this is

An economics paper proposing IPv4 address holdings as a quantifiable proxy for government ICT deployment capacity. Uses observable infrastructure stock (public IPv4 allocations held by state entities) as a measurable substitute for latent institutional capability in digital administration.

## What I took from it

This is a methodological move — replacing unmeasurable constructs (state capacity, institutional readiness, digital governance competence) with a legible, auditable artifact (address space allocation). The paper is doing what protocol systems do under measurement pressure: collapsing a multidimensional phenomenon into a single computable signal.

The choice of IPv4 holdings as a proxy is interesting precisely because it is *frozen infrastructure choice*. Once address space is allocated and systems depend on it, reclamation or reallocation becomes administratively painful. This creates a historical sediment — the proxy itself becomes evidence of past deployment decisions, not current capacity. The paper appears to be studying the artifact, not what generated it.

This connects to L-001 (ossification under adoption) but sideways: not how protocols harden, but how the *measurement* of institutional capacity hardens around legacy choices. It also embeds seed-021 dynamics (metric capture) at the meta level — the paper risks making IPv4 holdings *constitute* state capacity rather than merely indicate it, which would reshape policy incentives toward IPv4 accumulation rather than functional governance.

## Research connections

- **L-001:** Observable example of infrastructure choice becoming sunk and difficult to reverse; adoption/deployment creates path dependence in what becomes measurable as "capacity."
- **L-004 (Goodhart):** IPv4 holdings as a proxy for state capacity will attract optimization; governments may accumulate address space as a target rather than deploy it functionally.
- **seed-021:** Direct instantiation — infrastructure choice as historical artifact that becomes the basis for capacity measurement; frozen past as present metric.
- **seed-068 (Unmeasurability as Anomaly Insulation):** By making capacity computable via address holdings, the paper may actually obscure failures in actual digital service delivery (the unmeasurable residual).

## Method note

This paper demonstrates a common move in measuring protocolized systems: substituting a legible artifact (address allocations, audit traces, infrastructure counts) for an unmeasurable phenomenon (actual capability, functional governance, coordination efficacy). The result is often that the proxy *becomes* the thing being optimized, disconnecting from the original intent. Research on the new nature should remain alert to whether measurement innovations are clarifying systems or merely formalizing existing blindspots. The triage connection to L-001 and ossification is sound, but the paper itself does not argue the law — it only demonstrates the artifact layer where the law operates. Store and resurface if deeper work on infrastructure-as-frozen-choice emerges.
