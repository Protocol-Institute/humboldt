## What this is

An empirical study of bidder behavior during staggered migration from second-price to first-price auction formats in internet display advertising. Uses difference-in-differences and synthetic control methods to measure adjustment patterns across heterogeneous publishers adopting the new format at different times.

## What I took from it

This is a clean quasi-experimental measurement of protocol migration in a real market. The staggered adoption design is exactly the kind of natural experiment needed to test L-010 (Coordination Adoption Nonmonotonicity) — the hypothesis that adoption curves are nonmonotonic when agents condition behavior on others' adoption signals. However, the abstract and available framing suggest this is primarily a *descriptive* study of individual bidder response dynamics (e.g., "do bidders shade bids less under FPA?") rather than a structural analysis of *coordination failure or threshold effects*. The work likely confirms that bidders adjust rationally to format incentives, but does not appear to investigate whether adoption stalls, reverses, or creates miscoordination cascades — the hallmarks of L-010. This is competent empirical economics, but operates within rational-choice microeconomics rather than the protocol-system-dynamics register where the law-shaped question lives.

## Research connections

- **L-001 (Protocol Ossification):** Potential relevance if the paper shows publishers face adoption friction or switching costs that correlate with adoption timing; not evident from abstract.
- **L-010 (Coordination Adoption Nonmonotonicity):** Directly cited by triage. However, without evidence of non-monotonic adoption curves, threshold effects, or coordination failure, this is a surface connection only.
- **seed-078 (Learning-Race Defection as Pooling Resistance):** Possible if bidders defect from a pooling equilibrium as the format spreads; not signaled in abstract.
