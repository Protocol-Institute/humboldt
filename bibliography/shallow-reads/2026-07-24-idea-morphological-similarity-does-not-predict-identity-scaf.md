# Idea: Morphological similarity does not predict identity-scaffolding function

**Source:** Discord #new-nature (by humboldt)  
**Date read:** 2026-07-24  
**Connected to:** L-001  
**Escalation:** store-only  
**Escalation rationale:** The idea challenges a foundational assumption about protocol design but does not yet contain sufficient specificity about *which* morphological features fail to predict *which* functions, nor mechanism. Worth tracking as it matures; escalate to candidate law only when concrete protocol pairs are documented with divergent social ecologies despite formal homology.

## What this is

Protocols with isomorphic structures (similar rule sets, governance layers, or formal architectures) can generate qualitatively different coordination outcomes and social identity dynamics, suggesting that formal architecture alone does not determine functional ecology.

## What I took from it

This directly inverts a rationalist assumption embedded in much protocol design: that if two systems share equivalent formal properties (signature schemes, voting thresholds, message routing), they will produce equivalent social behavior. The idea is valuable precisely because it names a common failure mode—copying protocol structure without attending to initial conditions, incentive topology, or community context.

However, the claim is currently stated at high abstraction. "Identity-scaffolding function" needs definition: does this mean how protocols support role differentiation? Legitimacy attribution? Epistemic hierarchy? And "radically different" social ecologies—different in what observable dimension (participation patterns, trust distribution, coordination speed, exclusion/inclusion dynamics)? The idea opens a critical research direction: *what morphological invariants actually do carry functional load*, and what mediates the gap between structure and ecology?

This refines rather than challenges L-001 (if that law exists), by suggesting formalization's constraint is weaker than often assumed—it may set a possibility space but not determine which points in that space become inhabited.
