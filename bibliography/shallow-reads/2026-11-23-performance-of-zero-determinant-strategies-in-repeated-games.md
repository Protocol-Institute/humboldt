## What this is

A game-theoretic analysis of zero-determinant (ZD) strategies in infinitely repeated games without discounting, examining how unilateral payoff control properties hold or fail under different convergence assumptions. The work revisits foundational claims about ZD strategy performance when players don't discount future payoffs.

## What I took from it

This paper is a formal clarification within an established domain rather than a mechanism discovery or structural challenge to protocol law. It corrects technical assumptions (Cesàro averaging vs. limit assumptions) in prior ZD work but does not reveal new patterns about how formalization, adoption, or strategic capture operate in protocol systems.

The result is internally sound game theory: it shows that ZD strategy properties depend heavily on the mathematical framework chosen to represent "repeated without discounting." This is important for game theorists but does not generalize to the dynamics of artificial protocol systems, coordination under legibility pressure, or the behavioral feedback loops that characterize the new nature.
