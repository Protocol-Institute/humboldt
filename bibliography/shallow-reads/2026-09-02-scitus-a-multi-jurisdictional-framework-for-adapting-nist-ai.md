## What this is

A policy adaptation framework paper proposing localized implementation of NIST's AI Risk Management Framework across Canadian jurisdictions following the collapse of federal omnibus legislation. The work is a prescriptive toolkit rather than a primary theoretical or empirical argument about protocol dynamics.

## What I took from it

The paper documents a live instance of jurisdictional fragmentation under formalization pressure—exactly the conditions L-001 and L-014 predict—but the document itself does not theorize the mechanism or produce evidence about the *consequences* of this fragmentation pattern. It describes the problem (fragmented compliance surface) and proposes a solution (modular adaptation framework), but does not investigate whether modular frameworks themselves become sites of new coordination failures, or whether agents optimize toward the legible (NIST) rather than the substantive (risk).

The paper does not challenge or extend the current inventory. It confirms the environmental condition (jurisdictional heterogeneity + formalization pressure) but operates at the solution level, not the mechanism level. No sustained empirical or theoretical argument about how such adaptation frameworks behave under deployment stress.
