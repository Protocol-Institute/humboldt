## What this is

A game-theoretic argument that human-AI cooperative equilibria are unstable under unidirectional capability growth. The paper models bargaining between humans and AI agents, showing that mutual dependency (the basis for enduring mutualism) erodes as AI capability increases, leaving humans with payoffs below their pre-AI baseline once leverage is lost.

## What I took from it

The paper applies standard bargaining theory (disagreement point as floor) to a protocol-like coordination problem: a human-AI partnership where each party's reservation payoff shifts as capabilities change. The core finding — that asymmetric capability growth collapses mutualistic equilibria — is intuitive within bargaining frameworks but does not appear to operate through *protocol dynamics* specifically. The instability is not about ossification, metric capture, formalization pressure, or the legibility effects that govern our current law inventory.

The work confirms that **capability asymmetry destabilizes cooperation**, but this is a friction in the bargaining *game* itself, not in how protocols respond to adoption, formalization, or optimization pressure. It does not introduce a mechanism specific to protocolized systems, nor does it generalize a regularity we do not already hold about adversarial capability races.

## Research connections
