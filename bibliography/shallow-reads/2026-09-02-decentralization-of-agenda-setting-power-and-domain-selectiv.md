## What this is

A design paper proposing an Agenda Democratization Index (ADI) to measure decentralization of agenda-setting power in algorithmic systems, paired with a domain-selective bridging algorithm intended to reduce echo chambers. The work accepts cognitive and engagement-optimization constraints as hard boundaries and operates within them rather than challenging them normatively.

## What I took from it

The paper is competent domain work (algorithmic mechanism design for recommendation systems) but does not establish a generalizable law or challenge the current inventory. It accepts L-003 (Formalization Ratchet) as a working premise — that normative criticism of echo chambers fails because individual behavioral change is infeasible against cognitive and algorithmic incentives — and proposes formalized metrics and algorithmic procedures as the solution pathway. This is *consistent with* L-014 (Strategic Boundary Concentration Under Computable Legality) but does not advance it: the paper does not examine whether making agenda-setting "computable" via ADI actually displaces the optimization boundary rather than resolving it, nor does it track what happens to coordination costs when agenda curation moves from implicit human discretion to explicit algorithmic legibility.

The work operates squarely in the valley of applied system design and does not present sustained theoretical or empirical evidence for a new mechanism, nor does it challenge or extend a law in ways that would generalize beyond recommendation systems.

## Seed

**Seed title:** Legibility-Driven Agenda Formalism as Pressure Displacement

**Seed type:** question

**Seed text:** When agenda-setting power is formalized into a computable metric (e.g., an Agenda Democratization Index), does the optimization boundary shift from the agenda itself to the metric's design space, or does formalization genuinely distribute agenda power? In systems where agenda curation was previously implicit and distributed across human judgment, rendering it legible and measurable may concentrate design power in whoever defines the index, while appearing to democratize. This suggests a variant of L-014: not just legal boundaries become sites of strategic concentration, but measurement apparatus itself.
