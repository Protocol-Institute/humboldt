## What this is

An empirical labor economics study mapping occupational AI-exposure indices onto India's 2025 PLFS data for 83,000 employed graduates, documenting differential access to generative AI technologies by caste status. The work documents stratification patterns (0.24–0.37 SD gaps between SC/ST and upper-caste graduates) and attributes them to occupational segregation and within-occupation deployment inequality.

## What I took from it

This is a solid case study in *deployed* AI inequality, not a systemic investigation of how artificial systems generate or reinforce stratification. The causal story is essentially pre-existing (occupational segregation by caste) made visible *through* AI exposure—the paper documents manifestation, not mechanism. For the new nature research agenda, the observation that artificial systems concentrate at the top of existing social hierarchies is important for grounding but not novel; what matters is whether AI systems themselves have *intrinsic properties* that accelerate or reshape stratification in ways occupational segregation alone cannot predict.

The two channels identified (occupational choice + differential deployment within occupation) are empirically clean but theoretically shallow. No evidence that the *logic* of AI systems (training data, deployment architecture, incentive structures in hiring) creates feedback loops distinct from pre-digital discrimination.
