## What this is

An experimental vignette study (n=248 knowledge workers) testing whether access to AI assistance shifts help-seeking behavior away from colleagues. The work measures intention shifts under controlled scenario variation of AI availability and deployment mode, grounded in social psychology of collegial coordination.

## What I took from it

This is a narrow empirical observation of a predicted side-effect of L-023 (Informality as Novelty Sink Under Legible Optimization), but it does not constitute evidence for the law itself, nor does it introduce mechanism. The study documents that AI-as-available-substitute reduces help-seeking *intentions* toward peers — a rational response to lowered coordination cost. This is consistent with coordination cost conservation (L-006) but operates at the motivation level rather than the system level. The paper is essentially showing that when a legible, efficient proxy for peer knowledge exists, agents prefer it, which crowds out informal knowledge exchange. This confirms the intuition behind L-023 but provides no insight into *why* this displacement becomes irreversible, *how* it reshapes protocol infrastructure at scale, or *whether* the loss of informal channels produces downstream system fragility. The mechanism is transparent: substitution under availability, not protocol ossification or hidden feedback. The domain is narrowly organizational (intra-workplace coordination), and no evidence is presented that the effect generalizes to other protocol systems or that it produces the kind of structural lock-in the law attempts to capture.

## Research connections

- **L-023:** Confirms one downstream effect (help-seeking erosion under AI availability) but does not test the core mechanism of informality-as-novelty-sink or the conditions under which this produces irreversible protocol change.
- **L-006:** Consistent with the intuition that coordination cost shifts displace interaction patterns, but measures intention, not systemic cost conservation.
- **seed-157:** No evidence on whether this displacement functions as a "novelty sink" in the sense of reducing functional diversity or constraining future coordination possibilities.
