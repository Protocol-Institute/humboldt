## What this is

A qualitative/empirical study documenting friction between FDA regulatory protocol design (standardized traceability data infrastructure) and ground-level implementation barriers among food producers. The work sits at the intersection of regulatory governance and sociotechnical systems but appears positioned as a case study rather than a theory-building primary source.

## What I took from it

The paper documents a classic protocol-reality gap: the FDA designed a standardized data governance system assuming producers could absorb technical and compliance overhead, but frontline actors (farmers, fishers, retail workers) face resource constraints, heterogeneous existing systems, and competing operational pressures that make uniform compliance difficult. This is a *site report* on protocolized systems failure, but the abstract does not indicate whether the authors develop a generalizable mechanism for when/why designed governance diverges from implementation, or whether they identify specific design choices that predictably generate resistance.

The work is relevant to understanding how artificial regulatory protocols behave "in the wild," but without evidence of sustained theoretical argumentation or a mechanism absent from current sociotechnical governance literature, it reads as applied critique rather than foundational.
