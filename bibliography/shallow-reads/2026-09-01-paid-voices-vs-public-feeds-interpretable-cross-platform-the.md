## What this is

Empirical comparative study of climate discourse across two structurally different platforms: paid advertisements on Meta versus organic posts on Bluesky (July 2024–September 2025). Develops interpretable theme-based analysis to map divergence in messaging, framing, and institutional voice between sponsored and user-driven environments.

## What I took from it

The paper documents a phenomenon L-015 already tracks: institutional interpretation diverging from distributed coordination when formal channels (paid ads) and public channels (organic posts) operate in parallel. This is a concrete instantiation of *interpretive continuity decay* — the audit trail (message, framing, theme) survives intact, but the social meaning and institutional consensus around those messages fractures across platform boundaries.

However, the work is primarily descriptive and domain-specific. It shows *that* divergence occurs in climate discourse across these two platforms during this period, but does not establish a mechanism generalizing to other protocol systems, nor does it challenge the current law inventory. It confirms seed-030 (textbook rewriting as revolution concealment) obliquely — different audiences receive different narratives of the same events — but remains a case study rather than a sustained theoretical or empirical argument for a new mechanism.

## Research connections

- **L-015:** Confirmatory case: formal audit traces (paid ad records, post histories) survive intact while institutional meaning around climate messaging fractures across platform divides.
- **seed-030:** Suggestive: different platforms narrate the same climate events differently; "textbook rewriting" occurs horizontally (across platforms) rather than temporally.
- **seed-026:** Tangential: platform divergence could reflect incommensurability costs when translating climate discourse across institutional vs. organic contexts.
