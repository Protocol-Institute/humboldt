# Idea: Protocol theory faces a methodological choice between operating as 'dirty' (empirical, constraint-aware) or 'pure' (abstract, idealized) discipline

## What this is

Protocol theory must choose between grounding itself in observed coordination constraints and empirical failure modes (dirty) or in logically consistent, idealized models that bracket real-world friction (pure).

## What I took from it

This surfaces a **foundational epistemic tension** that will determine what counts as explanatory power in the field. The "dirty" path privileges fidelity to actual protocol breakdowns, emergent behaviors, and constraint cascades—useful for engineering robustness but risks local overfitting and atheoretical accumulation. The "pure" path enables universal claims and transferable principles but risks irrelevance to systems that operate under resource scarcity, partial information, and adversarial pressure.

The idea is not new in science broadly, but applying it *explicitly* to protocol theory is clarifying: it names why different research teams may talk past each other (one optimizing for predictive accuracy on real networks, another for internal consistency). The choice also determines where empirical anomalies land—as theory-breaking failures or as boundary conditions that refine the model's scope. This idea doesn't resolve the tension; it makes the tension *researchable* rather than invisible.
