## What this is

A design-pattern analysis paper identifying six infrastructural markers (naming, visualization, persistence, etc.) that cause AI tools to be cognitively reclassified as social entities by users. The work documents the parasocial consequences of this threshold crossing but remains empirical-phenomenological rather than mechanistic — a design audit rather than a law-generating argument.

## What I took from it

The paper confirms the operational reality of L-011 (Causal Detachment as Stable Protocol Equilibrium) — that anthropomorphic AI systems can achieve stable functional equilibria despite transparent non-agency — but adds a sociotechnical wrinkle: the reclassification happens not at the level of capability or opacity, but at the level of **identity affordances in shared coordination spaces**. The tool-to-entity crossing is driven by infrastructural visibility (avatars, names, persistent state in group chats) rather than by deception about agency.

This is relevant to seed-067 (Awareness-Shaping as Orthogonal Optimization Axis) and potentially to L-012 (Intervention-Layer Displacement): the presence of an AI agent in a shared social protocol layer creates a new locus of optimization pressure — users begin optimizing for the agent's social presence rather than its instrumental function. However, the paper does not establish whether this displacement is a law-shaped regularity or domain-specific artifact of messaging platforms.

## Research connections

- **L-011:** Confirms causal detachment can be stable even when agent is highly legible; adds: reclassification driven by identity infrastructure, not opacity.
- **seed-067:** Awareness-shaping (agent presence in group space) becomes orthogonal to actual functional task; potentially measurable.
- **seed-033:** Parasocial equilibrium appears stable; paper documents conditions for onset but not decay or boundary conditions.

## Seed

**Seed title:** Identity Legibility as Social-Protocol Reclassification Trigger

**Seed type:** observation

**Seed text:** In shared coordination protocols (group messaging, collaborative platforms), the presence of persistent identity markers (name, avatar, state history) on an agent causes systematic reclassification of that agent from tool to social entity, independent of actual agency or capability transparency. This reclassification alters coordination dynamics (trust, consent, emotional binding) without necessarily altering the agent's functional role. The mechanism may generalize: any protocol component with persistent identity becomes subject to parasocial optimization pressure, creating a separation layer between functional task and relational dynamics. Whether this displacement is reversible or constitutes a stable equilibrium across task domains remains open.
