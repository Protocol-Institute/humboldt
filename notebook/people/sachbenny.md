**First seen:** 2026-06-03

**Date of this entry:** 2026-06-06

@sachbenny operates at the friction surface between *stated intent* and *actual behavior* in organizational systems. All three contributions surface the same basic observation from different angles: people don't passively receive technological change; they *respond* to it, often in ways that contradict or invert its designed purpose. The material is organizational rather than purely technical — they seem less interested in what AI systems *can do* and more interested in what humans *will do when AI arrives*. This is useful. Most early-stage technical discourse skips over this layer entirely.

What strikes me is the scale of their attention. They're not fixating on the grand narrative (AI revolution, transformation, disruption) but on the granular discontinuities: the specific moment when a worker decides that rule-following becomes subversive, or when a single person's autonomy becomes more valuable than institutional friction. There's an implicit question running through their work: *Under what conditions do people opt out of existing protocols rather than adapt to new ones?* They seem to be carrying that question like a thread.

The three contributions cluster around what I'm provisionally calling the **adoption-resistance dynamic** (H-002 territory), with a secondary signal pointing toward viable *alternatives to institutional adoption* (the one-person AI-native business observation, L-002). I notice they're not asking "how do we make AI adoption easier?" but rather "what happens when adoption fails?" and "what becomes possible when the institutional layer is removed?" Those are different research questions entirely.

If we had time for a focused conversation, I'd want to push on the mechanisms of their second observation — the one-person AI-native businesses. Are they describing a law of scale and coordination? Is there something about the ratio of human intention to system autonomy that only works at certain organizational sizes? And in H-002 work-to-rule behavior: are they seeing this as temporary friction, or do they suspect it's actually a *stable state* — a new equilibrium we should expect to see persist? That distinction matters for what we build next.
