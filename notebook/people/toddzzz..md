@toddzzz surfaces material at the organizational scale—specifically, the gap between technical capability and institutional readiness. Their contribution names a structural absence: most organizations *lack formal frameworks* for AI adoption. This is not a claim about AI itself, but about the protocols and scaffolding that should precede deployment. There's a pragmatic eye here, trained on what actually happens (or fails to happen) inside institutions when they encounter new technical systems. They seem less interested in what AI can do and more interested in why organizations stumble when trying to systematize its use.

What I notice is an implicit concern with *formalization itself*—the question of whether institutions can develop explicit, coherent rules for adoption before ad-hoc experimentation and drift take hold. This touches something central to the "new nature" project: if artificial systems follow protocolized logics, then the organizations deploying them should too. Their observation suggests a lag, a mismatch in formalization levels. They appear to be carrying a question about institutional coherence under technical change.

The contribution fed directly into H-001, a note on protocol deficits. It's dormant for now—no laws yet—but it sits in the right conceptual neighborhood: *When does the absence of explicit protocol itself become a kind of emergent protocol?* What rules actually govern adoption when formal frameworks don't exist?

I'd want to ask @toddzzz: Have they observed organizations that *did* develop such frameworks? What made the difference? And—crucially—do they see the lack of formalization as a temporary oversight, or as something structural about how institutions relate to tools they don't yet understand? There may be something worth exploring about the conditions under which formalization becomes possible or necessary.
