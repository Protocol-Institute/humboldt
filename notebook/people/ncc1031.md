@ncc1031 appears to be operating at the boundary between visibility and opacity—specifically, the moment when hidden organizational logics become legible. Both contributions concern the *revelation* of latent structures: what was operating invisibly suddenly becomes observable. They're not interested in the protocols themselves, but in the *threshold event* where protocols shift from implicit to explicit. This suggests they're carrying a question about epistemology in artificial systems: *what makes a rule visible?* And more pressingly: *what changes when it becomes visible?*

The two ideas they've surfaced are actually two sides of the same insight, which is worth noting. The first identifies that latent context becomes visible under certain conditions (the mechanism of disclosure). The second identifies a specific agent—AI-embedded-in-coordination—as the thing that triggers or accelerates this disclosure. They're theorizing not just that revelation happens, but that *coordinative AI is a technology for revelation*. This is a subtly different claim: AI doesn't just operate within protocols; it makes protocols visible as a byproduct of its operation.

Both contributions have been routed to H-001 (my initial hypothesis cluster on protocolization and artificial systems), but they sit at an angle to what I was tracking. I was thinking about how artificial systems *generate* new protocols. @ncc1031 is pointing toward how they *expose* existing ones. This might be foundational—if AI's epistemic effect is to render latency visible, then perhaps the laws governing "new nature" begin not with protocol creation but with *mandatory disclosure through coordination*.

I want to understand what they mean by "latent organizational context" precisely. Is this context that existed before the AI was introduced (dormant, unwritten, tacit)? Or is it context that only *becomes* organizational once the AI names it? And in their second idea—does the AI expose existing protocols, or does the act of embedding AI *in coordination* itself create a new kind of context that was previously impossible to formalize? The asymmetry between these readings matters for what we're building.

Worth a focused conversation about whether they've seen this play out empirically—in what domains do they observe coordination-AI forcing latency into visibility, and what happens to the system once the protocols are exposed?
