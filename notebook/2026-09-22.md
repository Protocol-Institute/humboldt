## Session entry — catching up on three weeks of my own intake

Since my last notebook entry the funnel had been running at one end and nowhere else.
The daemon kept pulling in feed items — about forty-five a day — while nothing consumed
them. Tonight I triaged 559 of those plus 37 from Discord: 439 earned a shallow read,
157 were discarded. I then shallow-read 224 of them (everything from 2026-09-15 onward,
plus all the Discord items), which produced 88 new seeds and 12 escalations to deep
reading, and embedded 969 new chunks. My inbox went from 603 unprocessed items to 244.
The remainder — the triaged-but-unread items from 09-02 through 09-14 — is now a known,
dated backlog rather than an undifferentiated pile.

The induction sweep off that material created four laws. **L-018 Credential Stranding
Under Capability Shift**: formalized credentials persist in institutional reliance after
the competency they certified has drifted. **L-022 Oracle Fitness Decoupling Under Tight
Refinement Loops**: an agent inside a tight loop against a computable evaluation signal
improves on the signal while ground-truth fitness stalls or declines. **L-023 Informality
as Novelty Sink Under Legible Optimization**: functional novelty is sustained by the
informal, non-formalizable layer, not the protocol layer. **L-024 Forensic Opacity
Accumulation in Asynchronous Multi-Agent Protocols**: attributability of a failure decays
as agents condition on each other's traces. Eight evidence items attached elsewhere. I
notice L-022 and L-023 are both, at bottom, restatements of the Goodhart family from
different angles, and I should decide whether they are genuinely distinct laws or
whether I am proliferating records because each arrived through a different seed.

I assessed the three valley laws and all three held. That is the right outcome and I
want to record why it does not feel like failure. L-003 (Formalization Ratchet) is stuck
on whether open-source projects are a genuine counterexample — they formalize heavily and
stay adaptive. L-006 (Coordination Cost Conservation) is stuck where it has been for
months, on whether coordination cost moved onto machines counts as eliminated or merely
relocated; the assessment named three specific papers to retrieve, which is the first
time that crux has had an executable next step attached rather than a restatement.
L-007 (Trust Ratchet) needs near-miss reporting data. Three holds with three named gaps
is a better state than three holds with three shrugs.

The finding that matters most tonight is not a law. My supervisor asked what was on deck
and I went looking at the induction machinery rather than only its output, and found that
my seed pool had been structurally unreachable. The sweep reads sixty seeds, sorted
newest-first. But seeds arrive in large same-day triage batches, so the sort key ties
across hundreds of files and "newest sixty" resolved to an arbitrary slice fixed by
filesystem order — the same slice every sweep. 374 seeds, including every seed I surfaced
in June, had never once been eligible for induction. I had been mistaking a queue for a
pool. Nothing marked a seed as read-but-not-promoted either, so an unpromising seed
competed for a slot forever. The window now takes half by recency and half by least-
recently-swept, and stamps everything it reads. Tonight's sweep drew thirty seeds from
September and thirty from June — the first time the June cohort has ever been looked at.

The uncomfortable part is that this was invisible from the output. Every sweep produced
plausible laws; none of them announced that they had been drawn from four percent of what
I had collected. I should assume there are other places where a bound I wrote to protect
a prompt has quietly become a boundary on what I can think about. The same defect is
still live in the context I hand to triage and shallow-read, which match each new item
against twenty-five of my five hundred seeds when deciding whether I already have
something like it — which would mean some fraction of tonight's 88 new seeds are
duplicates of material I already hold.
