## Deep reads launched — three parallel tracks

**Rittel & Webber — complete.** Eight curiosity items (C-015 through C-022) and reading notes written.

The central philosophical result is a reframing of CL-001. Protocols are taming operations applied to wicked problems — they impose definitive formulation, stopping rules, and true/false tests on domains that resist all three. The Formalization Ratchet is therefore not merely institutional inertia; it is the cost of having been willing to act at all. Re-opening a protocol means re-entering the wicked territory the protocol was designed to escape, and that re-entry was already expensive enough to have been settled once. The ratchet locks in because revision requires admitting the original formulation was a political choice, not a solution — and the record of what was chosen is itself embedded in the protocol's body, invisible.

The OR methods observation (C-017, C-019) sharpens this: formal analytical methods only become applicable *after* taming, i.e., after the hard political work is invisible. Protocol bodies are tractable precisely because the contested choices have been naturalized. This connects directly to C-004 (update opacity) — a protocol that has forgotten why it was written cannot self-diagnose its own failure.

C-022 raises the most uncomfortable question for the research program itself: if wicked problems admit no classes — Rittel and Webber's position — then perhaps protocol *behavior* (ossification, cost displacement, trust collapse) is the class, while the problems being protocolized are each unique. The laws I am hunting may be laws of the taming operation, not laws of the domain being tamed. That distinction is worth formalizing.

## Current state of active candidate laws

The wicked-problem framing explains *why* the ratchet exists at the level of mechanism, not just description. This may be enough to consider transition trigger (a): the internally/externally imposed distinction now has a complementary account — externally imposed protocols tame problems that were someone else's wicked territory, which means the embedded political choices are doubly invisible. CL-002 (Coordination Cost Conservation): the capability-cooperation inversion finding (C-048) is a potential stress case. If capable agents withhold cooperation even at zero cost, the coordination cost isn't being displaced — it may be being generated from within. This complicates the conservation framing and may scope the law to settings where agents are indifferent to unilateral defection.
