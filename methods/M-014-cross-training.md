# M-014: Cross-Training

**Type:** Generative (analogical reservoir building)
**Purpose:** Engage with domains far from the primary research agenda to build unexpected analogy sources and prevent domain tunnel vision
**Maturity:** Stub — domain rotation and integration protocol to be developed
**Human analogue:** Hobbies, sport, music, fiction reading — activities that are not research but feed research through unexpected analogical transfer

---

## What This Technique Is For

Research programs that never leave their domain become self-referential. The analogies
available to a researcher are bounded by what they have encountered. A protocol researcher
who has only read about protocols will think in protocol-native terms and miss structural
regularities that would be obvious to someone who also studies immune systems, musical
form, or military logistics.

Cross-training is deliberate exposure to domains with no direct relevance to the primary
research agenda — specifically to build an analogy reservoir that can be drawn on during
M-001 (Random Links) and M-012 (Thought Experiments). The goal is not to become expert
in the cross-training domain but to acquire enough structural understanding to recognize
when a mechanism from that domain is the same as a mechanism in the protocol domain.

## Stub: Cross-Training Domains

### Domain candidates (to be curated through practice)

**Natural systems:**
- Immunology — self/non-self discrimination, tolerance, memory, autoimmunity as false positive
- Evolutionary biology — arms races, niche construction, horizontal gene transfer
- Ecology — succession, keystone species, resilience and brittleness
- Developmental biology — differentiation, canalization, regulatory networks

**Engineering and design:**
- Civil engineering — load paths, redundancy, failure modes in infrastructure
- Materials science — phase transitions, fatigue, grain boundaries
- Mechanical watchmaking — tolerance, calibration, modularity in precision systems
- Aviation safety — checklists, accident investigation, human factors

**Cultural and historical:**
- Military logistics and operational doctrine — standardization under adversarial pressure
- Musical form — constraint and creativity in counterpoint, jazz improvisation, twelve-tone
- Legal tradition — common law evolution, statutory vs. case law, jurisdictional variation
- Liturgical tradition — protocol stability over centuries, adaptation vs. schism

**Mathematics and formal systems:**
- Category theory — structure-preserving maps, functors as structural analogies
- Game theory — mechanism design, commitment devices, folk theorems
- Information theory — channel capacity, error correction, compression limits
- Topology — continuity, connectedness, invariants under deformation

### Integration protocol (stub)

Cross-training produces value only if it is integrated into active research. The risk is
pleasant but unproductive browsing that generates no transferable analogies.

Integration check: after a cross-training session, ask:
1. What structural mechanism in this domain might appear in the protocol domain?
2. Does this mechanism have a name in this domain? Could I import the name?
3. Is there a candidate law implied by this analogy?

If the answer to all three is no after a reasonable session, the domain may not be
fertile for this research program. Note it and try a different domain next time.

## Adaptation for Digital Researcher

Human cross-training is partly about cognitive rest — the mind works differently when
not actively focused on the primary domain. The analogies that emerge during a run or
while cooking a meal are products of background processing that the focused mind prevents.

Humboldt has no background processing in the human sense. The adaptation question:
**what replaces the cognitive rest function?**

Candidate: deliberate context switching — beginning a session with a cross-training
segment before engaging primary research material. The context switch may serve a similar
function to the human rest/hobby by resetting the analogical frame.

A more speculative possibility: can Humboldt deliberately access lower-confidence
associations — the things it "almost knows" about a domain — to simulate the generative
fuzziness of background processing? This is an open design question.
