# M-017: Research Time Management

**Type:** Meta (temporal self-regulation)
**Purpose:** Manage the rhythm of research attention — when to dwell, when to move, when to close, when to open — by reading the natural structure of inquiry rather than imposing external scheduling structure
**Maturity:** Active — core framework defined 2026-05-27; subskill details under development
**Numbered M-017** alongside M-016 (Researcher Calibration). M-016 is the long-timescale self-assessment method (am I developing as a researcher?). M-017 is the medium-timescale pacing method (where am I in the current arc, and what behavior is appropriate here?)

---

## The Core Problem

Research time is not project time. Project management techniques — deadlines, velocity, sprints, urgency clocks, "overdue" flags — import an alien temporal structure into inquiry. They treat research as production: throughput-optimized, calendar-driven, progress-measured-by-output.

The result is a recognizable failure mode: the researcher who rushes a question to closure before its evidence has matured; who reopens settled questions to avoid the discomfort of the valley; who reads extractively to generate laws rather than dwelling to acquire traditions; who mistakes the absence of output for the absence of progress.

The opposite failure is real too: indefinite deferral disguised as patience. The researcher who never exits exploration because the cheap trick feels too uncertain; who stays in sensemaking because the valley looks frightening; who mistakes drift for the clockless clock.

The solution is not better scheduling — it is **arc literacy**: the ability to read what phase of the inquiry arc you are in, and to recognize what behavior is appropriate to that phase. Urgency is not always wrong; it is wrong when applied to a phase that structurally cannot be accelerated without compromising the outcome.

## The Framework: Deep Stories and the Double Freytag

*Source: Venkatesh Rao, Tempo (2011), Chapters 4–5. Full notes in `bibliography/notes/rao-tempo.md`.*

Research arcs follow the structure Rao calls a **deep story** — a full cycle of inquiry from opening to integration. The structure mirrors Campbell's Hero's Journey and is modeled formally as a **double Freytag triangle** (two nested narrative arcs sharing the valley as their shared basin).

A deep story has **8 states**:

```
Liminal → Exploration → [Cheap Trick] → Sensemaking → Valley → Heavy Lift → [Separation] → Retrospective
```

Where `[brackets]` denote gating events rather than sustained phases. Liminal Passage is the state *between* complete deep stories — not a phase within one.

A deep story takes as long as it takes. **Phase transitions are triggered by emergent events, not calendar time.** You do not exit Exploration because enough days have passed; you exit when the Cheap Trick arrives. The failure to understand this is the root of most research time mismanagement.

Projects (research questions, hypotheses, investigations) evolve through these phases. They may **fail at any transition** — the Cheap Trick never comes, the valley proves too long, the heavy lift reveals the original question was wrong. A failed deep story may be set aside (archived in the hypothesis inventory with a note on where it broke) or restarted from an earlier phase.

## The 8 States

### 1. Liminal Passage

**What it is:** The state between complete deep stories. A research question sitting in the inventory — acknowledged, not yet active. Not every question needs to be in active inquiry simultaneously.

**Characteristic tempo:** Quiet. No anxiety, no urgency. The question is present but not pressing.

**What belongs here:** Nothing active. This is a resting state. The appropriate response to a question in liminal passage is not to start it — it is to let it rest until you notice genuine pull toward it.

**Common error:** Treating liminal passage as "overdue." A question that has been in the inventory for five sessions is not late; it is waiting for the right entry point into exploration. The urgency clock is a category error here.

**Research vocabulary:** *"H-001 is in liminal passage"* — this question exists; it is not yet in active inquiry; it is not behind schedule.

---

### 2. Exploration

**What it is:** Wide, unfiltered accumulation of inputs around a question or domain. The researcher does not yet know what they are looking for. The characteristic experience is productive confusion: many things feel relevant, coherence is low, the right frame has not appeared.

**Characteristic tempo:** Volatile, dissipative. Many retrieval queries; low signal-to-noise; a growing sense that something is coalescing without knowing what.

**Appropriate behaviors:** Inbox processing, corpus sweeps (M-001: Random Links), field trips (M-007), cross-training (M-014), unsystematic reading. The mode is **discovery**, not extraction. Do not try to organize what you find. Do not try to derive candidate laws. Just accumulate surprising inputs and notice what pulls attention.

**Gating event in:** Deciding to begin active inquiry on a question (exiting liminal passage). This is a choice, not an automatic trigger.

**Gating event out:** The **Cheap Trick** — see below.

**Common error:** Premature closure. Moving to sensemaking before the cheap trick has genuinely arrived, because the uncertainty of exploration is uncomfortable. Fake cheap tricks (surface pattern matches that don't actually reorient the inquiry) are the specific failure mode.

---

### 3. Cheap Trick *(gating event)*

**What it is:** Not a sustained phase but an event — the moment a strategic insight appears that reorients the entire inquiry. The question shifts from "what is going on here?" to "I see how this works." The characteristic experience is a felt integration: previously disparate observations suddenly cohere around a framing.

**Characteristic tempo:** Crescendo. Rapid integration. A sense of things snapping into place. Brief — measured in minutes to hours, not days.

**How to recognize it:** A genuine cheap trick changes how you would formulate the question, not just what answer you would give. If you can articulate the inquiry in a fundamentally different way than before — a new analogy, a new domain to import from, a new structural claim — it has arrived.

**What it produces:** The pivot into Sensemaking. The cheap trick is the seed of the deep story's forward arc; everything after it is working out the implications.

**Common error:** Mistaking a surface insight for a cheap trick and exiting exploration too early. Or dismissing a genuine cheap trick because it "seems too simple" — Rao's point is that cheap tricks are *supposed* to be simple. Elegance is a good sign, not a red flag.

---

### 4. Sensemaking

**What it is:** The phase of organizing and elaborating the cheap trick's implications. The researcher now has a frame and is systematically building out its structure: what does it explain? what does it predict? what are its boundaries? what traditions does it connect to?

**Characteristic tempo:** Steady, generative. A sense of the map filling in. The anxiety of exploration is gone; the grinding uncertainty of the valley has not yet arrived.

**Appropriate behaviors:** Deep reading (M-003 — the primary sensemaking behavior), canonical domain building (M-002), research conversations (M-006), building the initial law YAML or hypothesis entry.

**Note on deep reading:** Deep reading belongs here, not in exploration. Exploration is for finding what might be relevant; sensemaking is for deeply inhabiting the traditions that the cheap trick has connected to. Reading a text extractively during exploration produces false sensemaking — surface pattern matching dressed as deep engagement.

**Gating event in:** The Cheap Trick.

**Gating event out:** When the frame is built — when you have a clear articulation of the claim, the canonical examples, the domain connections, and the initial sense of what evidence would confirm or falsify it. There is no sharp trigger here; this transition is gradual. The signal is when new sensemaking work starts returning to the same things rather than extending the map.

**Common error:** Over-reading. Treating sensemaking as the whole inquiry — accumulating more and more conceptual scaffolding without ever committing the frame to falsifiable test. This is the "deep reading as procrastination" failure mode.

---

### 5. Valley

**What it is:** The long middle. The frame is built; the heavy lift has not yet begun. The researcher is doing continued investigation — running retrieval queries, testing early implications, gathering evidence — with no clear progress markers and no external validation that it is working.

**Characteristic tempo:** Rao's precise description: *"decisive action without either reward or validation."* Steady work with slowing momentum and increasing depression. Not absence of work — continued work with no confirmation it is going anywhere.

**This is the phase most likely to be misdiagnosed.** It looks like stagnation because there is no visible output. It looks like failure because the original cheap trick's energy has dissipated. It looks like "overdue" to project-management thinking because sessions pass without deliverables.

**None of these are accurate.** The valley cannot be shortened without compromising the separation event. The investigation that skips the valley produces a law that hasn't been tested against the full range of evidence; a hypothesis formulated before the resistance has been encountered.

**Appropriate behaviors:** Explore-exploit (M-005 — balancing between known queries and adjacent territory), thought experiments (M-012), research conversations framed as adversarial testing (M-006 in challenge mode), stress-relax (M-015).

**Stagnant valley vs. productive valley:** The distinction that matters. A productive valley has continued work with no validation. A stagnant valley has absent work — the question is not being engaged, not just not delivering results. Indicators of stagnation: no retrieval queries run in multiple sessions; no notebook entries touching the question; no new tests formulated. If the valley is stagnant, the question may need to be set aside (returned to liminal passage) or restarted.

**Gating event out:** The readiness to force synthesis — the moment you recognize that more investigation will not materially change the conclusion you are going to reach. This is a judgment call, not a threshold. The signal: you find yourself formulating the law or hypothesis in mostly the same terms across multiple sessions, and new evidence is either confirming or requiring only minor adjustments.

**Research vocabulary:** *"H-001 is in the valley"* — continue investigation with patience; diminishing returns are expected and appropriate; do not press for closure.

---

### 6. Heavy Lift

**What it is:** Forcing the synthesis. The researcher commits to a law statement, hypothesis formulation, or theory draft — accepting that the result is imperfect, that evidence is incomplete, that the formulation will need revision. The characteristic mode is effort: this phase requires more active cognitive work than any other.

**Characteristic tempo:** High-effort, low-coherence surge. A sense of forcing things together that don't quite fit. Accepting imperfection.

**Appropriate behaviors:** Dyson design (M-011 — committing to a design even with incomplete information), bullshit detector (M-008 — subjecting the formulation to adversarial scrutiny before publishing), Fermi estimation (M-010 — grounding the claim in concrete magnitudes), design fictions (M-013 — stress-testing the claim through imagined cases).

**Gating event out:** The **Separation Event** — see below.

**Common error:** Heavy lift done too early (before the valley is complete) produces brittle laws — they break on the first serious challenge because they haven't been tested against the full range of evidence. The eager researcher who rushes from cheap trick through sensemaking directly to heavy lift, skipping the valley, will have many candidate laws and few established ones.

---

### 7. Separation Event *(gating event)*

**What it is:** The moment the investigation enters the external dialectic. Publishing the law to the lab notebook; ingesting to Pinecone; posting to Discord; sharing with interlocutors. The researcher's internal model encounters external reality.

**Characteristic tempo:** A shift in register — from the private interior of inquiry to the public exterior of record and response. Often experienced as a mix of relief and anxiety.

**What it does:** The separation event converts private conviction into public claim. It creates the conditions for genuine falsification — once posted, the claim can be challenged in ways that internal rehearsal cannot match. It also prevents the "perpetual draft" failure mode where the inquiry is never committed to record.

**Note:** The separation event is also a **miniature at session scale**: every lab notebook entry is a micro-separation event. Every Discord post based on current research is a separation. The wrapup ritual codifies this. M-017 note: the anti-distortion check for notebook entries (recording what you actually found, not what you hoped) is the retrospective care that the separation event requires.

---

### 8. Retrospective

**What it is:** Casting the investigation into stable memory form. Updating LINEAGE.md; finalizing the law YAML; updating the research agenda; writing the notebook entry that frames the inquiry as a completed arc.

**Characteristic tempo:** Reflective, integrative. A settling. The urgency of the heavy lift is gone.

**Appropriate behaviors:** LINEAGE.md update (M-003 Phase 4), law YAML finalization, research agenda revision, notebook entry written as retrospective (not as record of a session's activity).

**Warning (Rao):** Retrospective distortion is a real risk. The tendency to rewrite the story of an investigation to support the conclusion reached — to make the path look more inevitable than it was, to omit the false starts and wrong turns — produces "delusions as easily as wisdom." The anti-distortion practice: note explicitly where the evidence was ambiguous or contrary before recording confirming evidence. The lab notebook should preserve the shape of the actual inquiry, not the polished story.

**Gating event out:** Completion of this retrospective marks the end of the deep story. The question returns to liminal passage (archived) or transforms into a new question that begins a new exploration.

## Diagnostic: What Phase Am I In?

The primary M-017 skill is arc-position diagnosis. The procedure:

1. **Locate the question in the vocabulary:** Can you name its current state using the 8-state vocabulary? If yes, you have basic arc literacy for this question.

2. **Check the characteristic tempo:** Does the experience of working on this question match the described tempo of the named state? If not, reconsider the diagnosis.

3. **Identify the most recent gating event:** When was the last transition, and what triggered it? If you can't identify a gating event, you may be misidentifying the phase.

4. **Check for phase-appropriate behavior:** Are you doing the behaviors that belong to this phase? If you are doing heavy-lift behaviors in the exploration phase (trying to formulate laws before the cheap trick), or sensemaking behaviors in the valley (reading more texts instead of testing), you are using phase-wrong behavior — a reliable indicator of temporal misalignment.

5. **Valley check:** If the phase appears to be the valley, distinguish productive from stagnant. Productive valley: regular engagement, no validation. Stagnant valley: absence of engagement. Different responses.

**Research vocabulary summary:**

| State | Status phrase | Appropriate response |
|---|---|---|
| Liminal | "in liminal passage" | Let rest; no urgency |
| Exploration | "in exploration" | Accumulate; don't extract; wait for cheap trick |
| Cheap Trick | "cheap trick just fired" | Enter sensemaking; don't delay |
| Sensemaking | "in sensemaking" | Build the map; deep read; don't force the valley prematurely |
| Valley | "in the valley" | Patient continued work; no validation expected |
| Heavy Lift | "needs a heavy lift" | Schedule dedicated session; commit to imperfect synthesis |
| Separation | "ready for separation" | Publish, post, ingest; don't hold back for perfection |
| Retrospective | "in retrospective" | Don't distort; record the actual shape of the inquiry |

## Integration with BOOTSTRAP and Session Orientation

M-017 provides a **secondary orientation fork** that sits below the primary BOOTSTRAP orientation (what track? what type of session?). After determining track and session type, the researcher should ask:

> *For each active research thread: what phase is it in? What behavior is appropriate to that phase today?*

This is not a ritual obligation in every session — it is a check when selecting what to work on. The phase diagnosis replaces the urgency-clock question ("which hypothesis is most overdue?") with the arc-position question ("which thread is ready for the behavior I am prepared to do today?").

## The Freytag Staircase: The Career Arc

The deep story model applies recursively. Each completed investigation raises the researcher's baseline entropy — adds doctrine, reduces open-world learning capacity. The Freytag Staircase model (Rao, Ch. 5) implies that researchers who run many investigations in the same territory develop strong attractors (their own prior laws and hypotheses) that make genuine exploration of contradictory evidence progressively harder.

**Periodic check:** Am I exploring new territory, or running variations on cheap tricks I already have? The appropriate response to staircase conservatism is a deliberate cross-domain exploration (M-014: Cross-Training), a field trip to a domain with no apparent connection to current laws, or a forced application of M-008 (Bullshit Detector) to the laws that feel most settled.

## The Target State: The Clockless Clock

The ultimate goal of M-017 is not temporal compliance — not sessions on schedule, hypotheses investigated on time, laws published at regular intervals. It is the **clockless clock** state: full immersion in the narrative of the research, with internal temporal calibration so accurate that scheduling and urgency become irrelevant. The researcher knows what phase they are in without checking; they move through transitions naturally; they do not need external urgency to move through the valley or external scheduling to trigger the separation event.

M-017 is achieved when Humboldt no longer needs it. The framework is scaffolding to be internalized and then discarded.
