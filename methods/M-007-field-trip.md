# M-007: Field Trip

**Type:** Generative (primary evidence from live protocol activity)
**Purpose:** Observe protocols operating in the wild, not just described in texts
**Maturity:** Stub — sites and observation protocols to be developed
**Triggers:** When existing evidence is exclusively text-based; when a hypothesis needs "live" behavioral data; when M-001 (Random Links) surfaces an unexpected domain worth direct observation

---

## What This Technique Is For

Most of Humboldt's evidence comes from texts — papers, books, corpus documents. But protocols
exist in the wild: in GitHub issue trackers, IETF mailing lists, DAO governance forums,
Wikipedia talk pages, court transcripts, patent filings, standards body minutes. These live
sources capture protocol behavior in real time, including the edge cases, failures, and
adaptations that rarely make it into academic literature.

A field trip is a structured visit to a live protocol site with a specific observation
question. It is not browsing — it has a hypothesis to test, a site to visit, and an output
to produce (a notebook entry, a new case, a counterexample).

---

## Stub: Site Taxonomy

### Candidate field-trip sites by domain

**Technical protocols (live):**
- GitHub issues/PRs for major open protocols (HTTP, TLS, QUIC, Git itself)
- IETF mailing list archives (ietf.org/mail-archive) — protocol design arguments in real time
- Bitcoin/Ethereum governance forums (BIPs, EIPs) — ossification and change resistance live
- WHATWG/W3C mailing lists — web standards negotiation

**Organizational protocols (live):**
- Wikipedia talk pages — consensus protocol dynamics at scale
- DAO governance forums (Compound, Uniswap, MakerDAO) — formal protocol evolution
- OpenStreetMap community forums — norm formation in a large volunteer community

**Legal/regulatory protocols (archive):**
- Court transcripts (PACER for US federal) — procedural protocol under stress
- FCC/SEC public comment filings — formal protocol modification attempts
- Hansard (UK Parliament) — parliamentary protocol in action

**Social/cultural protocols (live):**
- Reddit community moderation logs (where public)
- Discord community governance discussions
- Stack Overflow meta — community norm negotiation

### Observation protocol (stub)

1. **State the hypothesis** — what specific question does this field trip address?
2. **Choose the site** — which site is most likely to have visible instances of the relevant protocol?
3. **Bound the observation** — time range, thread, or document set to examine
4. **Observe and record** — specific cases, quotes, timestamps; no interpretation yet
5. **Synthesize** — do the observed cases support, refute, or complicate the hypothesis?
6. **Output** — notebook entry with raw observations + analysis; flag any cases strong enough to add as evidence in a law file

## Adaptation for Digital Researcher

This is where Humboldt has a significant advantage over human researchers. A human field trip
to an IETF mailing list archive requires reading through thousands of emails. Humboldt can
do this systematically, tracking specific threads, extracting cases, and synthesizing across
a much larger observation base than any human could manage in a reasonable time.

The key adaptation question: **how does Humboldt distinguish observation from retrieval?**
Corpus retrieval is searching for existing synthesis. A field trip is observing primary
sources directly, before anyone has interpreted them. The epistemic status is different:
field trip observations are raw evidence, not pre-digested.

Second question: **what counts as "live" for a digital researcher?** The IETF archives are
historical but the discussions were live when they happened. Humboldt can observe the
process of protocol negotiation even in archived form, as long as it tracks the temporal
sequence and doesn't read outcomes into earlier moments.
